## Why this version lives in a directory named for the last one

v0.7.3's own scope doc opened a fresh worktree per version. That was worth doing once,
to get off the shared checkout that another session was editing underneath it. Doing it
again per patch version would spawn a directory per bump, and the reason for the
isolation (the shared checkout at `~/Code/active/survivingthesingularity`) is already
solved by this worktree existing at all.

So v0.7.4 opens **in place**, on branch `v0.7.3`, in the directory `sts-v0.7.3`. The
directory name is now a historical accident rather than a version claim, which is fine,
because **`book.json` is the only authoritative statement of the version** and it reads
`0.7.4`. If a future cycle wants to rename the worktree and branch to something
version-neutral, that is a clean-up, not a blocker.

## What landed in this cycle

**Pass 1, 2026-07-27: the canned music campaign.** Requested by CT: integrate the AFM's
anti-recording campaign as a historical example, show the period robot advertisements,
and find further campaigns worth inserting.

- **New Precedent P-10, "The Robot in the Orchestra Pit" (1929–1948)**, in
  `09-chapter7.md`, placed immediately after P-09 so Chapter 7 now carries two. This is
  deliberate pairing, not overflow: P-09 is resistance with no negotiating table
  available, and P-10 is the same fight run by people who had every table there was
  (a 140,000-member union, half a million dollars, a national ad campaign, a federal
  statute named after its president) and lost the same argument anyway. The one thing
  they won was a royalty. Chapter 14 already carried two precedents, so the shape is
  established.
- **Two period advertisements reproduced**, which is the first time the book has
  printed a primary-source document as a figure rather than an illustration:
  - `ch07-canned-music-1929.jpg` — "Taking Liberties With Folks' Habits,"
    *Exhibitors Herald-World*, November 16, 1929, p. 35.
  - `ch07-canned-music-1930.jpg` — "Is the Robot Fooling YOU?",
    *Exhibitors Herald-World*, May 17, 1930, p. 44.

  Both were located by full-text search of the Media History Digital Library, read as
  page scans to confirm they are the advertisements and not trade-press coverage of
  them, and cropped from full-resolution IIIF renders. Both are US public domain (1929
  page since 1 Jan 2025, 1930 page since 1 Jan 2026) and the provenance, including the
  crop and grayscale conversion, is recorded in `static/book-images/credits.json`.
  **Every quotation from the ads in Chapter 7 was transcribed off the scan**, not
  copied from the blog posts that circulate these images.
- **New section, "The Campaign Has a Letterhead"**, in `09-chapter7.md` before the
  Egalitarian Pivot: a six-row table of organized campaigns against a medium (Sousa
  1906, the Music Defense League 1929–31, Petrillo 1942–48, the BPI's Home Taping
  campaign 1981, Valenti's Boston strangler testimony 1982, the RIAA against Napster
  1999–2001), each with what was demanded and how it ended. It closes by naming the
  three campaigns readers expect and will not find, and why: there was no organized
  campaign against the printing press (that is P-13, and it was one abbot), the Western
  Union telephone memo is a fabrication, and the Bavarian medical board's anti-railway
  decree has no archival record.
- **Ledger renumbered.** P-10 through P-22 became P-11 through P-23. Touched: thirteen
  chapter headings, the two prose cross-references (`P-15`→`P-16` in ch14's Sears
  passage, `P-20`→`P-21` in Appendix E), the "P-01 through P-22" phrasings in the
  introduction and the voice guide, Appendix B's P-19 correction header, and all three
  tables in Appendix D.
- **`verify precedents` no longer hardcodes 22.** `LEDGER_SIZE` is now a named constant
  at the top of `sts.py`, asserted rather than counted from Appendix D on purpose: the
  check exists to compare the chapters against an independent number, and deriving it
  from the index would make it a tautology (the same class of bug the v0.7.3 scope doc
  documented in `id verify`).
- **Appendix B gains sources 197–206**, including the two archive.org page URLs, the
  Motion Picture News enrollment report, Sousa's full text, the strike and Lea Act
  records, the Music Performance Trust Fund, and the Valenti hearing transcript.
- **Casebook gains C3 and C4**, and the apocrypha blacklist gains the Bavarian decree.

**On the two enrollment numbers.** The May 1930 advertisement claims 2,000,000 people
had joined the Music Defense League; *Motion Picture News* the same week reports the
union claiming 2,500,000. Both are the AFM's own figures and neither was ever audited.
The book says so in the text and again in Appendix B rather than picking the bigger one.

## Working rules

Unchanged from v0.7.3 except the version number. In particular:

**The version is already bumped.** `book.json` reads `0.7.4`. Content edits land in an
open version rather than triggering a bump each time. Do not bump again inside this
cycle.

**Do not merge to main.** Main auto-deploys production and production runs live Stripe
keys. Merging is CT's call.

**Run the harness at both ends of any pass:**

```bash
python3 scripts/sts.py verify          # math, meta, refs, precedents. Exits non-zero.
python3 scripts/sts.py verify --links  # add the network source check
python3 scripts/sts.py book            # current word counts. Trust this over any doc.
python3 scripts/sts.py id verify       # block index integrity after manual .md edits
```

A doc that asserts a version or a word count is a claim, not a measurement. Only
`book.json` and `sts.py` are authoritative.
