Then  follow  the  gain  all  the  way  to  a  plate.  A  controller  helps  a

A  tool  that  saves  an  hour  can  turn  that  hour  into  more  output,  a

A proposal people can vote on
Hereʼs what a real next step could look like after the five-household
trial. Itʼs an example, not a program thatʼs been funded.
The food bank, the growers, a kitchen, the workers, and the neigh-
bors write down one offer for one area. What food. How you ask for it
without paying. What happens if you canʼt cook or canʼt get there.
How  you  get  in  without  a  smartphone  and  without  knowing  the
founders.
They  write  down  what  it  costs  to  run:  food,  paid  jobs,  transport,
repairs, and money set aside for when the truck dies. They count vol-
unteer hours separately and say which jobs canʼt depend on whoever
happens to show up. The people doing the work get paid. The person
eating doesnʼt have to work for the meal.
Then they go looking for steady money: the county, a co-op, a church,
a grocer, or some mix. The county could run it, or pay several groups
to  run  it.  Donations  can  add  to  it,  but  they  donʼt  carry  the  core
promise, because donations come and go. Each choice puts different
people in charge, so everyone should see the options side by side.
The offer also says who takes complaints, how you appeal if youʼre
turned away, what information gets collected about you, and what
happens when something breaks. It makes room for people who dis-
agree with the organizers. You shouldnʼt have to be liked to be fed.
