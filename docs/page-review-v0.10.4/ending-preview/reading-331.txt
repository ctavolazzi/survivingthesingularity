Rung 2: Feed everyone who asks, from whatʼs
already there
What. Route recovered food to kitchens that serve anyone who shows
up. No proof of poverty and no interrogation about whether some-
body deserves dinner. Ask what you need to provide a meal they can
use, including dietary and access needs. Every unnecessary form is a
door some hungry person wonʼt walk through.
Proof itʼs possible. Franceʼs 2016 law prohibited deliberately spoiling
edible surplus and required larger food retailers to offer donation
agreements to eligible charities (Chapter 14). Britain opened public
restaurants in wartime at low, set prices: the British Restaurants of
Chapter  11.  Neither  example  established  this  rungʼs  universal  free
meal. They show parts of the arrangement already working. Joining
those parts still takes safe food, kitchens, paid work, and a funded
promise to whoever needs it.
Who. A food bank, a church kitchen, a restaurant with a slow Tues-
day, a school cafeteria after hours, a co-op like the one in these pages.
How youʼll know. Meals served, people turned away, and requests the
service couldnʼt meet because of timing, transport, or dietary needs.
The  target  for  unmet  requests  is  zero;  count  people  who  couldnʼt
reach the door as well as those who did.
Rung 3: Make the meal a public service, like the
library
What. Move the meal from charity to entitlement: a funded, perma-
nent  public  service  that  doesnʼt  depend  on  who  volunteered  this
week, the way a library or a school doesnʼt.

2022  to  2023  school  year,  public  school  districts,  county  offices  of
education,  and  charter  schools  serving  transitional  kindergarten
through  grade  twelve  must  provide  breakfast  and  lunch  free  of
