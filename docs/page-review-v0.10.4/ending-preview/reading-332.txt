Rung 4: Take the ground off the market
What. Put land for food and homes into community land trusts and
public trusts, so it canʼt be bid away from the people who use it.
Proof itʼs possible. Community land trusts already do this across the
country,  usually  with  a  99-year  ground  lease  that  keeps  homes
affordable for the next household too. Grounded Solutions Network,
Community  Land  Trusts Chapter  12  shows  one  being  born  and
recounts New Communities in Georgia, which lost its original land
and later bought a new property. Chapter 15 shows the machinery
and its limits.
Who. Nonprofits, cities with surplus land, farmers who want their
ground to outlive them.
How youʼll know. Acres and homes held in trust for the people who
live and grow there.
Rung 5: Own the machines together
What. The robots, greenhouses, kitchens, and local computers that
produce the floor should belong to the people who depend on them:
cooperatives, public utilities, land trusts. Not a distant owner who can
raise the price or switch them off.
Proof itʼs possible. America already did this once, with electricity. By
1930, nearly nine in ten homes off the farm had power but only about
one  farm  in  ten  did,  because  stringing  wire  to  farms  didnʼt  pay.
