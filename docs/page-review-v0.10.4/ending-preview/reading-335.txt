Rung 9: Keep the market above the floor
What. Money stays for everything above survival. Restaurants, gad-
gets, art sold for a fortune, a billion dollars if someone wants to build
one. None of that goes away. Money just stops being the ticket to
staying alive.
Not abolished. Optional.
And the floor doesnʼt have to stay where it starts. Where robots and AI
reduce the full cost of growing food, building homes, or providing
care, the saving can help raise the floor. Count maintenance and the
remaining human work, then decide how the gain reaches people.
Thatʼs  the  whole  point  of  the  machines.  A  gain  in  our  ability  to
produce becomes a gain in our ability to live.
What would stop it
Honesty about the obstacles is part of the plan.
Money. Every rung costs something to run, even when itʼs free to
the person eating. The fights over who pays will be real. Have
them while people are being fed, not as a condition for starting.
Capture. A service can be captured by whoever runs it. Build in
complaints, reviews, and the scoreboard from day one.
Backlash. The resistance described in Stage 5 can be real even if
the nine stages never unfold in order. Some people will fight any
change that looks like losing ground. A concrete, measurable
proposal is harder to fight than a slogan.
The people it doesnʼt pay. Some of the people who profit from the
way things are will spend a lot to keep it. The answer is the same
one the electric co-ops found: build it anyway, own it together, and
make it work well enough that nobody wants to go back.
None of those is a reason to wait. Each rung stands on its own. A
town that only ever gets to Rung 3 has still fed its people.
