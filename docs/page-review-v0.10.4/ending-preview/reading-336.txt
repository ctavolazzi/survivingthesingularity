What you can do this month
If you work for a living: find out what a food business near you
does with food it canʼt sell, and ask a local food provider what
need remains unmet. Bring those findings to one meeting.
If youʼre a parent: check what meals your school district provides,
whether they reach every kid who needs them, and what keeps
anyone out.
If you organize: start the Rung 1 page. One spreadsheet, published
monthly.
If you build things: pick one machine from Chapter 17 that could
serve the floor, and ask who would own it.
If you hold office: put a public meal line in the next budget and a
lawyer-for-every-tenant ordinance on the agenda.
If you own a business: donate what you canʼt sell, and sit on the
committee that decides where it goes.
Precedent P-23: The Day Care Stopped Costing
Money (Britain, 1942 to 1948)
In 1942, in the middle of a war, a government committee chaired by
the  economist  William  Beveridge  published  a  report  naming  five
giants standing in the way of rebuilding Britain: Want, Disease, Igno-
rance, Squalor, and Idleness. Even during the war, national health
insurance  covered  only  about  half  the  population,  and  it  left  out
hospital and specialist care, dental care, eye care, and hearing care.
UK Government History blog, The founding of the NHS: 75 years on
The  doctors  had  real  fears:  a  loss  of  freedom,  new  bureaucratic
demands. Aneurin Bevan, the health minister, answered them rather
than dismissing them. “Any health service which hopes to win the
consent of the doctors must allay these fears,” he said. And on 5 July
1948, the National Health Service opened. Britain became the first
western country to offer medical care, free at the point of use, to its
whole population. UK Government History blog
