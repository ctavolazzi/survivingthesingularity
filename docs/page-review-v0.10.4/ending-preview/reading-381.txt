The rules, in one breath each
P-01. “Kids these days” usually means power just moved toward people
who donʼt need permission. Find out what they know, then test the
specific worry.
P-02. Donʼt ask how impressive the demo was. Ask what the world looks
like when itʼs boring, and what has to be built to get there.
P-03. Timelines, mine included, err by orders of magnitude. Plan by
doors, not dates.
P-04. Read the rule before you turn it into a morality play. Then ask who
wrote it, and what they sell.
P-05. A capability lasts only as long as someone sustains it, and its reach
never settles its purpose.
P-06. People survive being decentered. Ask whose authority needed the
old map.
P-07. Measure the task without pricing the person as obsolete, and audit
your own ledger before someone else does.
P-08. Spend your energy on position, not on whether, and count welfare
separately from adoption.
P-09. Take the livelihood claim seriously before judging the tactic. Fight
for terms, not against physics.
P-10. Stop arguing about the machineʼs soul and start writing its invoice.
P-11. Persuasion wears your values. Ask who profits if you feel this.
P-12. Name the dependency and the disruption. Decide in advance which
side of the cascade your street is on.
P-13. Choose a medium by the job it does, and make something instead of
only complaining.
P-14. Donʼt refuse the new machine. Use it to pay for what you want to
keep.
P-15. Ask whose land and whose opportunity. Then start your paperwork
before the confusion clears.
P-16. Look for the version that ships as a kit, and count the work outside
the box.
P-17. Seeing the wave isnʼt the hard part. Find the line item that charges
people for inconvenience: itʼs a countdown clock.
P-18. Your product is mortal. Your capabilities travel, if you can name
them in time and have somewhere to stand while you do.
