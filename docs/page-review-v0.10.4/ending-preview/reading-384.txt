Sourcing, and the blacklist
Every  entryʼs  sources  are  in  Appendix  B.  Historical  interpretation  stays
distinct  from  the  events  it  describes,  company  histories  are  labeled  as
company histories, and where only a catalog record or a later account was
available, the entry says so.
Several famous technology quotes are fabrications, and they were left out of
this book on purpose. You wonʼt find the Western Union memo calling the
telephone “a toy,” Bill Gatesʼs “640K ought to be enough for anybody,” the 1894
“nine feet of manure” prophecy, the Bavarian medical boardʼs decree that
watching a train go past would give bystanders brain disease, or “everything
that can be invented has been invented,” because none of them survives con-
tact with a primary source. The Bavarian decree deserves naming specifi-
cally: itʼs the most-cited example of railway technophobia on the internet,
and no archival record of it has ever been produced. Losing it costs nothing.
The documented resistance to the railway, from canal interests fighting it in
Parliament  to  the  “railway  spine”  diagnoses  of  the  1860s,  is  real  and  was
always  the  better  material.  History  is  a  strong  enough  argument  without
inventing any of it.
