Month 1: Get your bearings
Turn off notifications, put the phone in a drawer that closes, and do
one real thing before noon. (P-11)
For one week, tally two numbers each day: hours consuming
commentary about the transition, and hours making anything at all.
(P-13)
Start a timeline file. Every time an expert publishes an AI timeline,
paste it in with the date and the author. Add mine. (P-03)
Start a receipts file for confident AI predictions, skeptical and
optimistic: save the claim, author, and date, including mine. (P-24)
Write down the next three claims that AI is harming peopleʼs thinking.
Note the specific harm alleged, the evidence offered, and what would
change your mind. (P-01)
Month 2: Know your numbers
Run the energy audit on paper: what comes in (calories, kilowatt-
hours, dollars), what goes out (labor, attention, rent), and who
captures the spread. (P-07)
Map your tin: the five things your household canʼt go a month without,
and where each one actually comes from. (P-12)
Run a household Y2K. One hour, everyone affected present: “Itʼs a year
from now and our income got cut in half. What broke first?” Fix one
thing this month. (P-22)
Set a preparation budget and write it down like a line item: the share
of income and hours youʼll put into skills, soil, savings, and
community. (P-22)
Month 3: Know your work
List the tasks in your job the way a buyer would, then ask a coworker
what the list missed. (P-07)
Write your capability inventory: one page, no job titles or products
allowed. (P-18)
