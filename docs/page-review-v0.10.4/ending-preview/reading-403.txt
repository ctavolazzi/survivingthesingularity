APPENDIX H
Talk About It
This book is meant to be argued with, out loud, with the people you live near.
Here are questions for a book club, a co-op meeting, a classroom, or a kitchen
table, and a short list of what to read next. Take the questions in any order. The
best conversations usually start with the one that makes somebody in the room
uncomfortable.
Questions for the Introduction
The author says “we donʼt have to live like this.” What, specifically, do
you think we donʼt have to live with? What do you think we do?
The United Nations says the world grows enough food for everyone,
and the USDA says 30 to 40 percent of the American food supply goes
uneaten. Where does the food in your town go when it doesnʼt sell?
“Money should be optional. Not abolished. Optional.” What would
change in your own week if you didnʼt need money to eat? What
wouldnʼt?
Questions for Part I: What is the Singularity?
The book defines the Singularity as the point where intelligence stops
being scarce. Has the price of any thinking work you know of already
started to fall?
“A curve gives you a date. A door gives you a Monday.” What door, if it
opened, would change your work or your household most?
Chapter 2 recounts the June 2026 export controls on Fable 5 and
Mythos 5 and their lifting eighteen days later, while access to the two
models still differed. What evidence would distinguish an overreaction
from a justified precaution?
The nine stages get more speculative as they climb. Which stage do
you think is furthest from happening? Which is closest?
