Two kinds of fear
So why do so many people resist a future where the machines do
more of the work? From years of watching people react to this, two
groups stand out, and neither one is stupid.
The overwhelmed. Most people are so worn down by the daily work
of staying afloat that they have no bandwidth left to process a change
this big, let alone welcome it. When youʼre drowning, even a rescue
boat  can  look  like  a  threat.  Thatʼs  not  a  character  flaw.  Itʼs  what
exhaustion does.
The  status-invested. Others  arenʼt  being  crushed  by  the  current
arrangement;  theyʼre  doing  fine  inside  it.  Their  fear  is  different:
losing their relative position, losing control, and the vertigo of having
to  redefine  themselves  outside  the  job  title  thatʼs  been  doing  the
defining.
Both fears contain information. A worker afraid that the new system
will  take  her  dinner  is  reading  the  situation  correctly  under  the
current  rules.  The  answer  isnʼt  to  tell  her  to  think  positive.  Itʼs  to
change the rule that makes the fear accurate.
The preservation solution
Hereʼs the move that makes the fear unnecessary.
We donʼt need to take anyoneʼs money. We donʼt need a hostile redis-
tribution of wealth, and we donʼt need to win every argument about
economics first. We need to use the robots to grow the food and, in
time, build the houses, and let people keep what they already have.
