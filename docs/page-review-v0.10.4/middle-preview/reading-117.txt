Security is something you can rely on
A promise from a generous founder is welcome. A service that keeps
running when the founder gets sick is stronger. A right you can still
claim when an administrator says no is stronger still.
A cooperative can own equipment. A public body can fund a food ser-
vice. An existing grower can be paid to supply it. Residents can have a
say  in  whatʼs  provided  and  how  failures  get  fixed.  None  of  those
words makes anything fair on its own. Together they describe work
we already know how to start: assigning resources, responsibilities,
and a usable claim on the result.
The point isnʼt to make everyone dependent on one kindly operator.
People should be able to criticize the service without losing it, and
have somewhere else to turn when it fails. Ownership and oversight
matter because the machine that feeds a neighborhood could also
give whoever controls it enormous power over that neighborhood.
Calling it public doesnʼt solve that. Calling it local doesnʼt either.
