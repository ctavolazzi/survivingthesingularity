The battle lines
The  fight  over  this  transition  isnʼt  humans  against  machines.  Itʼs
between two mindsets, and they cut across every income bracket,
every  politics,  and  sometimes  through  the  middle  of  the  same
person.
The Entitled The Ready
What it believes Society falls apart if
peopleʼs needs get met
without forced labor. Life
is a ranking, and the
ranking has to hold.
The daily suffering can
stop, and should.
Automated food,
eldercare, and household
labor are things to want,
not fear.
What it wants Scarcity preserved,
because scarcity protects
status and control.
The daily burden of
survival lifted, so people
and their kids can actually
live.
Who tends to feel it People fighting for
position in the old world,
and people whose
standing depends on
others needing them.
People who carry the
physical weight of survival
now: parents, caregivers,
disabled people, the
elderly, the underpaid.
That  table  isnʼt  a  list  of  good  people  and  bad  people.  Itʼs  a  list  of
reflexes. A mother of three can reach for the hierarchy when her own
job is the one threatened. A comfortable executive can want the suf-
fering to stop. The point of naming the two is so you can catch your-
self.
The employee as a liability
The pressure behind all this is plain. To a business under competi-
tion,  payroll  is  a  recurring  cost,  and  replacing  paid  tasks  with  a
cheaper machine can look attractive on the ledger. The incentive is
built into the structure. Thatʼs not a conspiracy; itʼs arithmetic.
