Egalitarian  isnʼt  the  moral  garnish  on  this  strategy;  itʼs  the  load-
bearing  wall.  The  scarcity  psychology  you  just  read  about  is  one
reason to stop making a household absorb every shock alone. Nine-
teen households pooling tools, food, childcare, and repair skills may
be able to carry a burden that would flatten one of them. A regional
disaster can hit all nineteen, so they still need help beyond the circle.
The harvest study gives us a reason to take financial pressure seri-
ously; it didnʼt test a food baseline or a cooperative. A dependable
dinner removes one demand on attention. Thatʼs already worth build-
ing.
So the pivot, concretely: stop climbing and start weaving. Pool capital
goods, the truck, the mill, the servers, the greenhouse, because a
share of ten machines beats a loan on one. Keep circles small enough
that everyoneʼs name is known. Spread out the deciding, because a
network with one indispensable node is just a boss with extra steps.
And do it before you need it, because trust compounds like capital and
canʼt be printed in an emergency.
One warning, and it matters. Small circles are also where exclusion
lives. Knowing everyoneʼs name makes it easy to decide an outsider
doesnʼt count. The pivot is toward a table that keeps adding chairs,
not a fence with a nicer gate.
