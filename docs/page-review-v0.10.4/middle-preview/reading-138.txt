The mechanism. Rage against the machine aims at the one part of the
system  that  canʼt  hear  you.  The  owners  heard  perfectly  well,  and
answered with the army, because the battle line was never human versus
machine. It was, and still is, a fight over who sets the terms.
The rule. Take the livelihood claim seriously before you judge the tactic.
Then fight for terms, not against physics.
The practice.
Install a rage-conversion rule. When the anger comes, and itʼs
legitimate, honor it with a trade: every hour spent furious at the
machine buys one hour building your position. Keep the ledger in
your notebook.
Negotiate while you still have leverage. This month, write down what
you know that isnʼt written down anywhere: the judgment calls, the
failure modes, the customer knowledge. That document is your
severance leverage, your consulting practice, or the training data for a
tool you own. Unwritten, itʼs nothing.
Sort the movements before you join one. Any group organizing around
the transition gets one question: “What are we building, and what
terms are we demanding?” If the honest answer is only “weʼre
expressing opposition,” you know that record. Join the ones
negotiating transition funds, ownership stakes, and local capacity.
Same anger. Different batting average.
