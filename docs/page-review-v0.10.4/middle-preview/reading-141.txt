And  then  the  one  durable  win  of  the  whole  twenty-year  fight:  an
invoice. The Music Performance Trust Fund, established in 1948 and
financed by payments from the recording companies, administered
by a trustee independent of both the union and the industry, pays
musicians to play free public concerts. Itʼs still running.  Music Per-
formance Trust Fund, About A performance free to its audience, with
the musicians paid. Read that sentence again with a meal in place of
the concert.
Hereʼs the sting. Recorded music, the canned music, the Robot, is
now simply how most musicians reach anyone. The thing the cam-
paign existed to prevent became the thing its beneficiaries live on,
and the institution that still pays some of them is funded by it.
The mechanism. The union did everything right by the standards of legit-
imate  opposition:  organized,  funded,  national,  lawful,  sympathetic.  It
didnʼt preserve the orchestra pits. The public was enjoying recordings,
and a campaign for live music couldnʼt by itself preserve the old payroll.
The only move that produced something lasting wasnʼt a defense of the
old job. It was a change in the terms: who pays whom, for what.
The rule. Argue about the machineʼs soul and you can spend twenty years
getting  nothing.  Argue  about  the  machineʼs  bill  and  you  can  build  an
institution that outlives you.
The practice.
Read your own industryʼs robot ad this week. Find the most recent
public statement your union, guild, or trade body has made about AI,
and mark it in two colors: sentences arguing the machine is soulless
or dangerous to the art, and sentences naming a term (a rate, a royalty,
a credit, a consent requirement). Count both. The ratio is a forecast.
Draft the one clause youʼd actually want, in a single sentence, and say
who pays it: “any model trained on our archive pays X per use into Y,
administered by Z.” You can write yours this afternoon.
Find the fund and check whether youʼre in it. Many trades already
have a residual, a royalty pool, a licensing society, a trust. Plenty of
eligible people arenʼt enrolled, because enrollment was never the
exciting part of the fight. Spend twenty minutes finding out what
exists in your field and what it pays.
