CHAPTER 8
The Psychology of the
Collapse
Sophia. The mirror humans keep flinching at. (ITU Pictures, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons)
“Men, it has been well said, think in herds; it will be seen that they go
mad in herds, while they only recover their senses slowly, and one by
one.”
Charles Mackay, Memoirs of Extraordinary Popular Delusions and
the Madness of Crowds, Preface (1852 edition)
In this chapter:
The last battlefield isnʼt the grid or the market. Itʼs the three pounds of wet tissue
behind your eyes.
Your phone isnʼt just a tool. Itʼs a harvesting machine, and the crop is your attention.
Panic isnʼt the same as information. Itʼs a state, and states can be interrupted,
starved, and survived, especially with somebody at the door.
The people whoʼve already lived through a collapse know something the feeds canʼt
sell you: how to get up the next day, and who to call.
