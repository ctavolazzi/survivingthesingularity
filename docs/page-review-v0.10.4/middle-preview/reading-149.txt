Your cognitive budget is finite
Your brain is about two percent of your bodyʼs mass and uses roughly
twenty  percent  of  its  energy  at  rest.  Thatʼs  the  rough  twenty-watt
comparison from Chapter 5. It isnʼt a fixed allowance of decisions you
can  spend  down  with  notifications;  much  of  that  energy  supports
ongoing activity whether or not youʼre tackling a task.  Raichle and
Gusnard, Appraising the Brainʼs Energy Budget (2002) The practical
question here is what keeps pulling your attention away from the
thing you meant to do.
Switching  can  have  a  cost.  Gloria  Mark  and  colleagues  observed
information workers moving through other activities before return-
ing  to  interrupted  work.  Their  return-time  measure  included  that
intervening work; it wasnʼt a stopwatch on how long a brain needs to
regain  focus.  Mark,  Gonzalez,  and  Harris,  No  Task  Left  Behind?
(2005) In a separate experiment, interrupted participants completed
work faster but reported more stress, frustration, effort, and time
pressure.  Mark, Gudith, and Klocke, The Cost of Interrupted Work
(2008)
Neither finding gives you a universal recovery time or a maximum
number  of  interruptions  per  hour.  Try  a  protected  stretch  on  one
demanding task, keep the necessary safety alerts, and compare what
you finish with an ordinary stretch. Count what actually pulled you
away and whether it helped.
