You donʼt need deep pockets to start. You need a result you can point

Step 1: Start with your hands
Change your surroundings before you try to change your thinking.
Clean something up. Find a neglected stretch of your
neighborhood: a park edge, an alley, an empty lot. With
permission where itʼs needed, clean it, and finish the whole route,
not just the photo: collect what the local trash and recycling
service will take, get it into that stream, and come back in a week
to see whether it stayed clean. Chapter 17 has more on doing this
well.
Document it. Take before-and-after photos. Write down what it
took. Thatʼs the beginning of turning raw reality into something
you made.
Optional: own a few watts. If youʼd like, a small solar panel with a
charge controller and a battery pack sized to keep a phone and a
radio alive is a good first build. Get someone whoʼs done it to walk
you through it, or buy a ready-made kit. Itʼs a project, not an entry
fee.
Step 2: Civic connection
The time you spend on digital fear is time you could spend in your
physical community. Find a place that needs human hands: a com-
munity garden, a food pantry, a neighborhood center.
Direct exchange. Show up and work. Labor moves you from the
world of digital opinion to the world of physical results.
The human filter. Working alongside people is how you find the
ones who also want to be useful. Make the introduction wherever
you can, then give it a shared task.
