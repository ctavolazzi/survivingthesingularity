Step 3: Find your node
As the habit grows, youʼll need a place to work. That doesnʼt mean
renting a shop. It means finding space nobodyʼs using.
Look for underused spaces: barns, empty garages, forgotten
community halls, the church basement thatʼs dark six days a
week.
The intergenerational exchange. Find an older neighbor whose
house has more room than they use. Offer real help: fixing the
leak, doing the yard work, driving to appointments. In exchange,
ask to use the spare room or the garage as a workspace. You get
space; they get reliable help and company. Write down what each
of you is agreeing to, so it stays a good deal for both.
Share a result people can judge
Dennyʼs first useful act on camera was to stop performing someone
elseʼs  idea  of  him.  He  had  knowledge  a  machine  could  help  him
spread, and the right to decide whether he wanted to spread it at all.
When you share what you made, say what you tried, what it cost,
what happened, and what you still donʼt know. A failed attempt with
clear conditions can save the next group real money. And get consent
before someoneʼs hardship becomes part of your story. People donʼt
owe the internet their hardest day in exchange for help.
