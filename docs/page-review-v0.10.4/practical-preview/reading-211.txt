SURVIVING THE SINGULARITY


A large model, compressed. A 70-billion-parameter model at 4 bits:

      VRAM = ((70 · 4)/8) · 1.2 = 42 GB


Two 24 GB cards provide 48 GB in total, which is what the co-op scav-
enged. Tools like llama.cpp can split supported models across cards,
but adding the labels on the boxes doesnʼt guarantee a working con-
figuration. Test the model, memory use, and speed under the work-
load you intend. Once the software and weights are downloaded,
local inference can run without an internet connection.

The heat math
A rack drawing 600 to 1,000 watts turns roughly that much electrical
power into heat while it runs. Measure your own load; retired hard-
ware doesnʼt share one power rating. Inside an insulated building,
thatʼs heat youʼre paying an air conditioner to fight. The co-opʼs
answer was to carry it outside in liquid. The physics is one line:

      Q̇  = ṁ  · Cp · ΔT


To move 1,000 watts with a water-glycol coolant (    Cp ≈ 3, 800 J/kg·°C)
while letting it warm only 5°C through the loop:

      ṁ  = 1000/(3800 · 5) ≈ 0.053 kg/s ≈ 3.2 liters per minute

Thatʼs the required flow for these assumptions, not a pump specifica-
tion. The pump must deliver it against the loopʼs resistance, and the
radiator needs enough area, airflow, and temperature difference to
reject the heat. Measure coolant temperature under load before
relying on the arrangement.
