What the co-op did about Frank began with an offer, and Frank sent
Reubenʼs first draft back with questions. They settled on the inven-
tory the tool library could use, bought at honest wholesale, not
vulture pennies, the money real enough to matter, and a paid job,
because the tool library had been running on a clipboard and an
honor system and it turned out what it needed was a man who knew
the torque specs from memory and where every last thing was. The
libraryʼs income and costs would get a monthly review. Frank wanted
the figures as well as the welcome.
Then he hung his grandfatherʼs bell over the tool library door. The
first time it rang for a member checking out equipment, he looked up
from the counter before he could help it. The store had not died after
all. It had shrunk to fit through a doorway and moved two miles east.
The parts still came from suppliers beyond the valley. Frank still
knew which ones.
