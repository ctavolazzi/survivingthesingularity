                                                                           CHAPTER 15


A teaspoon of healthy soil can hold enormous populations of bacte-
ria, alongside fungal threads, protozoa, nematodes, and other life.
Together they run an economy:
1. The exchange. Plants turn sunlight into sugars and leak some of
   them out through their roots.
2. The mining. Bacteria and fungi feed on those sugars. Some fungi
   extend a plantʼs reach for water and phosphorus; legume bacteria
   fix nitrogen from the air (Chapter 9). These are different
   partnerships doing different jobs.
3. The release. Protozoa and nematodes eat bacteria and fungi and
   release nutrients near the roots in forms plants can use.

Cover crops, compost, deep mulch, and less tillage all feed that
system. University of Minnesota Extension, Soil Biology What none
of them do is make a garden feed itself forever. Every harvest carries
nutrients away in the food, and something has to put them back.
