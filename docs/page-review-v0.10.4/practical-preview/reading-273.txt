Precedent P-19: Organizing the Harvest (United
States, 1943 to 1944)

When the war strained the food system, the United States govern-
ment asked amateurs, office workers, schoolchildren, grandmothers,
to grow food. Not as a symbol. As logistics.

And it worked on a scale thatʼs hard to believe now. By the National
Park Serviceʼs account, in 1944 some 18.5 million Victory Gardeners
supplied about forty percent of the nationʼs fresh vegetables. National
Park Service, Victory Gardens on the World War II Home Front Not
agribusiness. Yards, vacant lots, schoolyards, and rooftops, run by
people the food industry would have written off as amateurs the year
before.
It didnʼt happen by itself, either. The USDAʼs 1943 program told orga-
nizers to think about the bus route: a family without a yard needed a
plot they could reach by streetcar, bus, or bicycle. Somebody had to
get permission for the land, prepare it, assign plots, arrange water,
and supply tools and advice, and the county agricultural agent was
