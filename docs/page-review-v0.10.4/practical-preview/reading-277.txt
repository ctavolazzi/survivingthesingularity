SURVIVING THE SINGULARITY


He rang no bells. He put it all on the big screen at Friday dinner and
let the room read.

Curtis got to the fence first, because Curtis always did, except the
fence had changed shape over nearly two years like everything else
about Curtis: “Thatʼs a product,” he said. “That inbox is a business.
Course packs, plans, consulting. People pay real money for half of
what we know.” He wasnʼt wrong, and nobody said he was, and for a
minute the old grammar hung over the table, the Claypot grammar,
and Elijah felt it reach for him like a current he knew by name: scale
it, platform it, first-mover. When he first arrived heʼd have already
had the deck built.

It was Frank Kowalski who took it apart, from the end of the table, in
the tone of a man doing inventory. “I spent thirty years selling parts
for two dollars over what the internet charged,” Frank said. “Know
what I was actually selling? Somebody who knows which part. You
canʼt download me. Him neither.” A thumb at Denny. “Fence the files
and youʼre selling paper. Ship the files free and every one of those
eleven towns needs a Frank and a Marta and a Priya of their own
anyway, and theyʼll find them locally, because that part doesnʼt ship.”
He folded his glasses. “The store taught me that. Late.”
“The press did this once before,” Elijah said, and kept it to two
sentences, because he had learned. “Books used to be chained to
desks. Somebody built a machine that made copying cheap, and the
people who shipped did better than the people who kept the chains.”
He thought about the leak. “And we can let somebody read our mis-
takes without buying the whole project. That would have saved me
some money.”
Marta was quiet until the end, which is how the co-op knew where
the decision would land. “I told this one his first day,” she said,
nodding at Elijah, “that nineteen households who know whose kid is
whose donʼt scale, and thatʼs the load-bearing wall, and I meant it,
and itʼs still true.” She looked at the wall of numbers. “The wall
doesnʼt scale. The blueprints of the wall scale fine. Ship them.”
