Common Sense came off Robert Bellʼs press in Philadelphia in January
1776, anonymous, in a paper wrapper, for two shillings. Clements
Library, Going Viral in 1776 It ran through printing after printing that
year and helped turn a quarrel about taxes into an argument for inde-
pendence, in language people could carry into their own kitchens.
But look at what actually happened on paper, because itʼs the whole
lesson of media power in miniature. Paine and his printer fell out
almost immediately. Paine meant his share of the profits to buy
winter clothing for the Continental army; Bell claimed there were no
profits. So Paine took an enlarged edition to William and Thomas
Bradford.  University of Minnesota, Common Sense  Across the
Atlantic, some London editions cut the passages about the king, and
in one surviving copy of John Almonʼs edition, a reader filled the gaps
back in by hand. The author framed the argument. Printers could
reproduce it, revise it, or censor it. Readers could fight back in the
margins.
