# v0.11.0: ownership, participation, and the title's promise

Local editorial revision of v0.10.4 at `bdf0584`, dated 2026-10-07. Five of the 35 canonical sections change. The public-download release remains v0.7.5. No commit or deployment is part of this revision.

## Changes

- Introduction uses the title phrase and explains survival as keeping people fed, housed, and cared for during changing paid work, with shared influence over tools and benefits. Useful provision remains worth pursuing if the forecast is wrong.
- Chapter 6 adds "Whose robots?" Ownership includes licenses, prices, repair, access, and ways to challenge decisions. Wiener supplies a historical proposal; Ostrom supplies evidence about shared natural-resource governance. The existing warning about local and public ownership remains.
- Chapter 19 adds "Get involved": a concrete request, a responsible decision-maker, meeting preparation, accessible participation, and follow-up. Tucson's action is bounded to city annexation and development-agreement negotiations. Subsequent county construction activity prevents presenting it as a project-wide defeat. Rung 5 supplies the cooperative ownership connection.
- Conclusion introduces solarpunk through an early 2008 proposal and the book's own interpretation. Le Guin's speech supports imaginative alternatives. The moral call becomes "Stop waiting. Show up. Start building." The final Bear Flag scene and "You're holding it." are unchanged.
- Appendix B adds seven source records and their limits. The uncertain anthology chronology, legal detail from a mirror, and additional parade of thinkers are omitted.
- Landing page replaces the guaranteed with-a-plan/without-a-plan outcomes with food, ownership terms, and public participation. Nearby competitive-survival and zero-safety-net copy is aligned with that promise. This is not a complete factual audit of inherited landing-page statistics.

The original dictated passage remains available in the earlier worktree's editorial note. This revision retains urgency with one added profanity and recognizes useful online organizing.
