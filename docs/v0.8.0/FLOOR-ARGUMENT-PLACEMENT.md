## The finding that decides this

The book argues for food independent of a paycheck. Across all 43,449 words of the current
manuscript, chapters 0 through appendix E, these terms appear **zero** times:

| term | occurrences |
|---|---|
| garment | 0 |
| sweatshop | 0 |
| farmworker | 0 |
| migrant | 0 |
| exploit | 0 |
| living wage | 0 |

A book making the case that food should not depend on employment currently contains nothing
about the people who grow and sew for a living now. That absence is the hole CT's argument
fills, and it is literal rather than rhetorical. It is also the strongest possible answer to
"does this belong in the book," because it is measurable.

## The collision to avoid

**The book already has nine stages, and they are not CT's nine stages.**

The manuscript's stages run Cash Grab, Panic and the Plug, Who Gets to Decide, Food Without a Job
Requirement, Disagreement and Loss, Possible Exodus, Biology and Longer Life, Intelligence at
Larger Scale, Edge of the Map. They carry chapters 2 through 4, the Part I divider, the
introduction, Appendix C and the Elijah protocol. Seventeen references across seven files.

CT's nine stages are a different model: a power and adoption sequence, not a capability
sequence. Both being nine is a coincidence, and a costly one. **Two numbered nine-stage
frameworks in one book will be conflated by every reader.**

**Do not import CT's nine stages as a numbered list.** Compress the model into prose inside
Chapter 7 or Chapter 6, framed as a recurring pattern rather than a second framework. The
insight survives. The numbering does not. If CT wants the numbered version preserved, it belongs
in an appendix beside the precedent ledger, explicitly labeled as a different axis from the nine
stages of Part I.

Note also that the book's Stage 1 through Stage 3 are already power stages. CT's stages 2 to 4
overlap them. That overlap is an argument for folding, not for adding.

## Recommended placement, three locations, roughly 700 words net

**1. Chapter 7, "The people doing the transition." Roughly 400 words. Primary.**
The exploitation evidence goes here, in the section whose title already promises it. Ethiopia at
$26 a month against a living wage near $110. Less than 2 percent of garment workers earning a
living wage. NAWS: 61 percent of US crop workers born in Mexico, 75 percent Hispanic, and the
survey excludes H-2A entirely so the real figure is higher. This is the "people doing the
transition" the chapter names and has never described.

**2. Chapter 7, "Work, bargaining, and the meal." Roughly 200 words.**
The C5 move: the alarm is about knowledge work. Stanford's measurement, 22 to 25 year olds in
exposed occupations about 19 percent below trend, through reduced hiring rather than firing,
under a paper whose own update is headlined "No Widespread Displacement." The rhetoric is running
ahead of the measurement. Show the gap and let the reader notice who is missing from the panic.

**3. Introduction, "The Uncompromising Truth." Roughly 100 words, and only the claim.**
One short passage stating the moral accounting: the current arrangement already runs on cheap
labor, and the fear being voiced is the fear of joining those people rather than the fact of
them. No figures in the introduction. The introduction states, Chapter 7 proves. That division
is already how the book works.

**Engels' pause goes in Chapter 6 or Chapter 7 Foundations, not the introduction.** It is the
answer to "what happens afterwards," and it needs its contested causation stated. It is too much
machinery for an opening.

## What it costs

- **About 700 words added to a book that just cut 53 percent.** That is the real price and CT
  should weigh it. The defense is that this is the one addition the manuscript measurably lacks,
  and that it strengthens an existing chapter rather than opening a new front.
- **Chapter 7 grows from 2,038 to roughly 2,640 words**, making it the longest chapter in Part II.
  Acceptable, since it is the chapter carrying the most argumentative weight.
- **Appendix B, Works Cited, must absorb the new sources.** It is currently 1,085 words and was
  deliberately reduced to a focused source map at v0.8.0. Adding Allen, NAWS, Cornell GLI, Clean
  Clothes, NYU Stern, Stanford and Hobsbawm will grow it. That reverses part of a v0.8.0 decision
  and should be acknowledged rather than done quietly.
- **No new precedent ID.** P-09 already covers the Luddites. Resist creating P-24. The ledger is
  a closed set of 23 referenced throughout, and extending it touches Appendix D, Appendix C and
  every cross-reference.

## Voice constraints that bind this specific material

Read `VOICE-GUIDE.md` before drafting. Three that will bite:

1. **Profanity budget is effectively spent.** The guide allows "a handful in 85,000 words." The
   current manuscript is 43,449 words and contains **exactly one** instance. CT's source text has
   several. At most one more can enter, and it has to be load-bearing. "Finally, it fucking sucks
   and always has" is a great line and it is probably not the one worth the budget.
2. **Anger aims at systems, incentives or ego, never at the reader.** CT's source aims at "all
   these alarmists," who overlap with the book's readership. The guide's own model for fixing
   this is the introduction's existing line about the AI-slop complaint, which lands on a motive
   rather than on a group. Diagnosis beats denunciation. Use that pattern.
3. **Contractions on, no em dashes, and the banned noun listed in the voice guide mechanics
   table stays banned.** Mechanical, non-negotiable, and easy to violate when importing raw
   source text.

The register for all three placements is **Argument**, not Field Manual. No "do this Monday"
list. The chapter's Precedent block already carries the practice.

  sentence about what Simeon earned needs an earnings source regardless of who he is.
- **Bangladeshi CPI.** Not required for this placement, because none of the three passages above

- **Ethiopia figure is 2019.** Date it in the text or refresh it before print.
- **"Less than 2 percent"** needs its Fashion Revolution source and date.
- **Acemoglu and Johnson** stay as characterization, not quotation, unless someone reads the book.

None of these blocks step 3. All of them block publication.

---

## What I recommend against

- **A new standalone section or chapter.** The argument has a home that is already named for it.
  A new section would duplicate Chapter 7's purpose and add structural weight to a manuscript
  that just shed half its length.
- **Importing the nine stages as a numbered framework.** Covered above. This is the single
  highest-risk move available.
- **Leading the book with it.** The introduction is 1,222 words and works. Putting the moral
  accounting first reframes the whole book as an argument about labor rather than about the
  transition, which is a different book than the one CT has written.
