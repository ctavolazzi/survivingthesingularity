# Evidence and attribution review for v0.8.1

Reviewed 2026-09-22 by Codex, evidence-review subagent. This is an editorial working record, not a claim that every sentence, quotation right, or technical design has been independently certified. Manuscript edits belong to the coordinating editor and the two prose editors. This reviewer changed no manuscript source.

## Scope and method

The review covered the factual argument across the 30-section reading order: introduction; preface; Chapter 0; three part dividers; Chapters 1 through 18; conclusion; and Appendices A through E. It also reviewed the inherited v0.8.0 floor-argument research memorandum. All 23 numbered precedents and all 22 opening epigraphs are accounted for below. Sources were checked selectively at the claim level, with particular attention to precise numbers, quotation attribution, causal claims, historical generalizations, technical capability, and the distinction between a proposal and an existing service.

The starting manuscript was v0.8.0. Current v0.8.1 passages and factual hotspots were read again during the parallel revision. This document records findings and recommended dispositions; it is not a frozen proof of the final typeset edition. The coordinating editor must reconcile these dispositions against the final source and PDF.

Evidence labels used here:

- **P:** Original document, first-person account, original research, or a facsimile/transcription of one. Primary sources can still be mistaken or self-interested.
- **I:** Institutional history, archival catalog, official explanatory account, or publisher attribution. Useful support, but not interchangeable with inspecting the underlying original.
- **R:** Scholarly research or synthesis of historical evidence. Its interpretation is not the event itself.
- **U:** Exact wording, edition, original record, or inference remains unverified in this pass. Remove the unsupported detail, qualify the attribution, or obtain the missing evidence before using it as settled fact.

A URL found in an index is identified as such where direct retrieval failed. Discovery is not full-text verification. The report distinguishes the existence of a source from verification of a claim within it.

## Argument: what the evidence can bear

The strongest defensible thesis is normative and institutional: useful automation should increase material security; food access should not disappear when employment does; public choices about ownership, funding, provision, and access determine who benefits. No prediction of AGI is needed to establish those commitments.

The manuscript should not substitute any of the following stronger claims:

| Overstatement | Defensible form |
| --- | --- |
| All complaints about AI are complaints about capitalism. | Fear that losing paid work means losing food or housing is also a criticism of the economic arrangements connecting work to survival. Other objections concern reliability, consent, surveillance, environmental burdens, and physical harm. |
| The problem is only mindset, never the tools. | Attitudes shape priorities and institutions. Design, reliability, resource use, deployment, and enforceable access also matter. A well-intentioned tool can fail. |
| Nobody objects to useful medicine or science. | Many people welcome these benefits while disputing a particular method, risk, distribution arrangement, or use of their data. No universal opinion poll is supplied. |
| All leaders seek domination rather than benefit. | Name and source a specific policy or describe an incentive or risk. Do not infer every officeholder's private motive. |
| Robots can already provide unlimited labor and cure disease. | Specialized systems already assist research and some hazardous tasks. Universal provision, broadly autonomous physical labor, and general cures are goals or hypotheses, not established present capabilities. |
| Free food means nobody will work or restaurants must disappear. | A baseline does not logically require either result. It can change bargaining power and business conditions; some firms may adapt and others close. No blanket prediction of zero effects is warranted. |
| Food security solves all forms of insecurity. | Food is a material first priority. Housing, care, disability access, transport, and other constraints remain. |

Sources supporting these boundaries:

1. [NIST AI 600-1, Generative AI Profile, final publication](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf), **P/I**. Risks include erroneous outputs, privacy, security, environmental effects, harmful bias, and human-system interaction. These are not reducible to lost wages. Use the final publication rather than the earlier draft ending in `ipd.pdf`.
2. [ILO, Generative AI and Jobs: A Refined Global Index of Occupational Exposure](https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure), **P**. Occupational task exposure estimates are not observed job losses and are not a universal forecast of replacement. Its accompanying [method explanation](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/how-might-generative-ai-impact-different-occupations) distinguishes exposure from actual outcomes and emphasizes transformation of tasks.
3. [FAO, SOFI 2026 launch statement, 21 July 2026](https://www.fao.org/director-general/speeches/details/launch-of-the-state-of-food-security-and-nutrition-in-the-world-%28sofi%29-2026-report-statement/), **P/I**, and [SOFI publication page](https://www.fao.org/publications/fao-flagship-publications/the-state-of-food-security-and-nutrition-in-the-world). The 2026 statement estimates 645 million experiencing hunger in 2025 and distinguishes hunger, food insecurity, diet cost, and affordability. These are different measures. Affordability includes income and necessary non-food expenses. Logistics and other costs beyond farming matter. Prefer the distinction over a paragraph crowded with numbers.

## Audit of the inherited floor-argument memo

The memo contains useful corrections, but its labels of **supported** sometimes exceed the evidence it describes. It must not be copied into the book as an independently proven sequence.

1. **Allen's Engels' pause estimates.** The reported 0.00 percent annual real-wage figure is a rounded historical estimate for a specified series and period, not literal knowledge of exactly zero change for every worker. The memo itself correctly distinguishes 1800-1830 from 1780-1840. Allen's account emphasizes capital accumulation and productivity; another author's political explanation must not be attached to Allen as his conclusion. One historical sequence does not prove oppression necessarily arrives first in every technological transition.
2. **Stanford employment study.** [Canaries in the Coal Mine research page](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/) identifies the original and updated observational results. A relative employment gap is not the same as that percentage of jobs being eliminated by AI. Concentration among young exposed workers and reduced hiring do not measure what all critics fear or why they fear it. Correlation, identification assumptions, and alternative explanations belong with any causal wording.
3. **NAWS crop-worker scope.** The memo corrected survey rounds to 2021-2022 Report 17. Its 75 percent Hispanic figure, wage estimate, birthplace figures, and H-2 A exclusion have specific populations. Foreign-born share derived from a domestic-born share must be labeled a calculation. Do not call an excluded population's effect a guaranteed lower bound without showing the denominator and composition assumptions. Do not equate ethnicity, birthplace, citizenship, and migration status.
4. **Bangladesh wages.** [Cornell's February 2025 brief](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/sites/default/files-d8/2025-02/cornell-gli-brief-bangladesh-february-2025_0.pdf) describes a legal monthly minimum and negotiations. A legal minimum is not measured earnings. Dividing by calendar days is not a working-day wage. Dollar conversion requires a date; currency depreciation alone does not measure local purchasing-power loss. The memo acknowledges missing CPI evidence. Do not quietly remove that qualification.
5. **Ethiopia wages.** The cited 2019 study is dated evidence. Do not present its 26 dollar monthly base figure as a 2026 finding or a whole-population earnings distribution.
6. **Living-wage percentage.** The memo's **less than 2 percent** figure passes through an advocacy page that does not establish the original sample, date, denominator, and measurement method. Do not use it until the original Fashion Revolution evidence is located. The distinction between **all workers** and **any workers** is essential.
7. **Claims that nobody discusses abundance.** [Altman's 2021 proposal](https://moores.samaltman.com/) directly refutes an absolute absence claim. It is a proposal, not proof that its financing works or its projected price declines will occur. The author's contribution can be the particular food-first priority and its moral insistence without claiming sole invention.
8. **Named worker Simeon.** The memo flags uncertainty whether this is a real person, composite, or illustrative example. Do not present a reported personal wage or biography without the underlying interview/source. Use openly hypothetical framing or omit.
9. **Nine-stage pattern.** A framework for examining power is not Gartner's hype cycle, a historical law, or a validated predictive model. It can organize questions without claiming inevitability.
