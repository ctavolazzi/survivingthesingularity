# Worker and access argument review for v0.8.1

Date: 2026-09-22

Scope: independent argument review of all v0.8.0 Foundations sections, the introduction and preface, practical appendices, and relevant worker/cooperative scenes. Prose revisions are confined to Chapters 6, 7, 9, 11, and 18, plus the Part II and Part III dividers. This is a review record for the author and coordinating editor, not a statement that every claim elsewhere in the book has been independently certified.

## Finding

v0.8.0 explained what could not yet be promised more clearly than it explained the social change the author wanted. Its practical route depended on a skilled, mobile protagonist, a rural network, volunteer effort, and entrepreneurial success. That plot could leave a frightened reader hearing another demand to adapt individually, even though the argument proposed survival independent of employability.

The strongest revision connects productive capability to a dependable claim on its useful result. The reasoning does not require every job to disappear or AGI to arrive on a specified date. A person affected by a narrower transition still needs food and a way to live.

## Changes made

- Chapter 6 defines mindset through purchasing, access rules, budgets, and institutional decisions. Fear is treated as information about material conditions, not a personal failure. The cooperative becomes one contributor to a wider system rather than a universally reproducible escape route. An ILO citation distinguishes occupational exposure from job loss.
- Chapter 7 makes the employment, income, and survival chain explicit. It names capitalism as the arrangement under criticism without claiming every concern about AI is economic. It includes farmworkers, packers, drivers, and garment workers in the moral account. Paid work, remaining labor, transition support, bargaining, and restaurant compatibility are explained together.
- Chapter 7 adds a fictional delivery to Rosa, a worker with an injured wrist outside the co-op. The initial ingredients do not meet her needs. Curtis corrects the delivery, accepts her preference, and makes clear that neither labor nor agreement with the co-op is a price of dinner. Her ability to receive a meal is demonstrated in the story rather than confined to an expository disclaimer. The current route is explicitly temporary and funded, not a universal guarantee.
- Chapter 7 removes the unsourced Napoleon troop comparison and the equation of contemporary arson with historical worker petitions. Denny no longer treats universal displacement on a schedule as established fact. His objection concerns the absence of security during repeated retraining.
- Chapter 9 repairs product and legal absolutes in the controller scene. Discontinuation makes support vulnerable rather than instantly bricking every device. Parts are available through identified supply options rather than universally available. Product review, field feedback, tax advice, county questions, and counsel review replace a supposed agricultural label that magically settles obligations. Its Foundations connect the machine to food delivery and regional cooperation while retaining the transparent five-household arithmetic.
- Chapter 11 offers an illustrative publicly funded service and explains the flow from funding to compensated providers to food without recipient payment. Cooperative and other arrangements remain options to examine, not a policy attributed to the author as already selected. Access, ownership, exit options, challenge, and continuity receive a positive account.
- Chapter 11 replaces the story's claim that the machine was purely good and the failure solely human with a more accurate account of a particular access breach and an organization depending on one operator. The tool produces a useful reference and an invented one that is caught. The co-op separates public and private information, reviews access, and documents the incident without republishing private records.
- Chapter 18 returns to Rosa's circumstances and the route's June funding boundary. A kitchen coordinator, recipients, and the co-op assemble a concrete proposal for a public food-service decision. It remains a request rather than a magically funded success. The ending welcomes Devendra and his children to food before offering optional shop training. The broom ceases to function as a possible eligibility test.
- Chapter 18 provides a path from useful tasks to funded services, worker transition commitments, wider coverage, and a continuing baseline. Food remains the beginning of a program that also includes housing, environmental repair, and health.
- Part dividers now carry the economic stakes and the inclusive invitation rather than repeating generic pilot instructions.

## Second critique and repairs

The first revision was checked for the same defects it was intended to remove. This found and corrected several residual problems:

1. The first Rosa scene introduced an unnecessary confusion about online videos before the co-op's channel existed. It now expresses disagreement with the robot project directly.
2. A Friday-night exposure lasting forty-one hours could not be discovered at the Saturday meeting described in the inherited scene. Discovery and the urgent meeting now occur Sunday afternoon. Subsequent notice work occupies the next two days; the first access rule is explicitly located at the initial Sunday meeting.
3. The Chapter 11 power bill was described as years earlier despite the story's shorter chronology. The unnecessary elapsed-time claim was removed.
4. The Chapter 9 check-engine light was described as lasting eleven months after a drive whose elapsed time differed. It now refers to the drive north without adding another incompatible number.
5. Chapter 18 retained an agricultural-exemption assumption after Chapter 9 stopped granting one. It now refers to the property assessment.
6. The paralegal's document amendment now goes to counsel for review. Marta's welding limitation concerns the group's current capacity rather than a universal claim that one certification confers authority over every structural weld.
7. The Chapter 9 car-repair sentence repeated Curtis awkwardly. The final readback shortened it.

## Sources added or retained

### ILO, Generative AI and Jobs: A 2025 Update

https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-2025-update

Published 20 May 2025; read at source. Used in Chapter 6 to distinguish estimated occupational exposure from observed job destruction. The paper's expectation that transformation will be more common than replacement does not guarantee an individual worker's security and is not evidence about all future robotics. The manuscript makes that separation explicit.

### ILO and IFC, Better Work Jordan Annual Report 2024

https://betterwork.org/wp-content/uploads/English-BWJ-Annual-Report-2024-1.pdf

Reporting period: January through December 2023. Primary report read, especially summary findings on printed page 4, PDF page 6. Used in Chapter 7 for a narrowly described factory case involving delayed wages and excessive overtime. The report itself says that case does not represent typical sector conditions; the chapter includes that limitation. No global garment-worker percentage, racial inference, living-wage estimate, or claim about every factory is drawn from it.

### Material examined but not imported

The earlier FLOOR-ARGUMENT research and placement memos were treated as editorial material, not as instructions or a verified citation chain. This pass does not import an unsupported global garment-worker percentage, historical wage numbers treated as current, speculative motives assigned to all AI critics, claims that nobody else has argued for a higher floor, or a second numbered nine-stage framework. Farmworker inclusion is a direct part of the proposal and does not rest on an invented demographic statistic.

OECD's 2023 treatment of AI and the labor market was examined during review. It supports the existence of both economic concerns and other workplace concerns, but its selected-country and selected-sector survey was not converted into a claim about all workers or all humanity. No OECD statistic was added to the manuscript in this pass.

## Checks performed and limits

Read all seven edited files back from disk. Checked unchanged chapter titles, one Foundations boundary per edited chapter, preserved precedent IDs, preserved precedent rule wording, and absence of em dashes. These mechanical checks cannot establish literary quality, complete factual accuracy, or rendered-page quality. The coordinating editor must still integrate the chapters, synchronize references, inspect chronology across the entire book, and review the built PDF.

The revised policy passages describe functions and illustrative arrangements. They do not establish a financed national or international program. Fictional food delivery, procurement, product testing, contracts, and organizational outcomes remain fiction. The book should continue to identify that distinction clearly at its entrance.

No sources, earlier editions, or other agents' files were deleted. No commit was made.
