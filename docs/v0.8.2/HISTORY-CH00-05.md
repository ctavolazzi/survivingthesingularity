# Historical refinement: Chapters 0–5

Date: September 22, 2026. Working edition: v0.8.2. All existing narrative and argument retained. Additions deepen the existing P-02 through P-07 cases rather than establish a second historical apparatus.

The broad craft choices are documentary specificity, human decisions, interests and obligations, and an explicit connection between an episode and the chapter. No passages, signature language, or named structures from Robert Greene's books were copied. No imagined historical dialogue, private thoughts, or sensory scenery were added.

## Chapter 0: the demonstration and the alliance

Read [Bell's June 27, 1876 letter and Library of Congress transcript](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/magbell/005/00500228/00500228.pdf), both pages. Added the encounter with Dom Pedro II and William Thomson, the movement between demonstration rooms, and Bell's reported conversation with Elisha Gray about Western Union. Bell explicitly attributes part of the audience-reaction account to Willie Hubbard. The manuscript preserves that distinction and does not turn a hoped-for alliance into an accomplished agreement.

Interpretation: capability and bargaining appear within one source. Bell's account supports what he reported and hoped; it is not a neutral adjudication of the patent dispute or Gray's competence. The prose omits Bell's disparaging assessment of Gray rather than repeat it as a finding. The original collection landing page returned 403 in this research run; the official transcript PDF was successfully read.

Correction: P-02's title changes from “The Toy at the Fair” to “The Voice at the Fair.” The old title implied a dismissive reception the cited letter does not establish. All body text retained.

## Chapter 1: the work behind a failed forecast

Read the [Smithsonian transcription of Wilbur Wright's May 30, 1899 letter](https://siarchives.si.edu/history/featured-topics/stories/letter-dated-may-30-1899), including his reading request and offer to pay. Read [NASA Glenn's account of the 1901 wind tunnel](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/WWW/K-12/airplane/wrights/tunnel.html), including the poor performance, shop engine, balances, and airflow correction. The [National Park Service chronology](https://home.nps.gov/wrbr/learn/historyculture/theroadtothefirstflight.htm) supplied a further chronological check.

Addition makes research, experiment, and measurement the center of the case. No implication that one mistaken newspaper forecast represented every contemporary expert. No additional precise estimates of investment or causal shares. NASA's historical engineering explanation is identified as such; the manuscript does not claim the editor inspected the original wind-tunnel hardware.

## Chapter 2: a law within a dispute

Consulted [Locomotives Act 1865, section 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/28-29/83/pdfs/ukpga_18650083_en.pdf), through indexed statutory text when direct PDF access returned 429. Independently read [Lords debate of May 26, 1865, columns 867–872](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1865/may/26/second-reading). The act establishes duties; the debate establishes participants' positions. They are different instruments.

Added the road-sharing problem, staffing and signaling requirements, Hardwicke's argument for replacing earlier restrictions, Kinnaird's example of separated farms, and Carnarvon's objections. A supporter’s anecdote is not proof that engines were safe. A stated purpose is not proof of eventual effect. The addition therefore does not claim either a successful safety policy or capture by a rival industry.

Independent critique identified an imprecise grouping of whistles and steam restrictions. Corrected the new sentence to distinguish a general whistle prohibition from restrictions on releasing steam. No original prose was removed.

## Chapter 3: the fleet's own justification

Downloaded the [university-hosted excerpt of Edward L. Dreyer's primary-source translations](https://edspace.american.edu/justinjacobs/wp-content/uploads/sites/986/2022/12/31-Historical-Accounts-of-Zheng-He.pdf). It is an image-only PDF. Rendered pages locally and visually read the Liujiagang inscription on printed pages 191–193, with adjacent material for context. The source and page images are retained in `research-sources/`. [Smithsonian catalogue](https://siris-libraries.si.edu/ipac20/ipac.jsp?uri=full%3D3100001~%21843382%210) and [WorldCat](https://search.worldcat.org/title/Zheng-He-%3A-China-and-the-oceans-in-the-early-Ming-dynasty-1405-1433/oclc/728085435) identify Dreyer's 2007 book. No claim to have read the entire monograph.

Added the 1431 inscription's combination of imperial commission, religious protection, travel and coercion. The manuscript attributes the account to the expedition, including its treatment of the Ceylon king. It does not validate the inscription's representation of conquered people or adopt its imperial vocabulary. No newly invented ship dimensions, fleet totals, or monocausal explanation of termination.

Correction: P-05 becomes “The Court and the Fleet (Ming China, 1405 to 1433).” “Sailed Home Forever” suggested the all-encompassing end that the existing body rejects. Independent critique asked for more historical action and less reflection; the revision adds the inscription's account of capture, presentation, pardon and return, and shortens a newly written interpretive paragraph.

## Chapter 4: two framings of one book

Read [Copernicus's dedication to Paul III](https://hti.osu.edu/sites/hti.osu.edu/files/dedication_of_the_revolutions_of_the_heavenly_bodies_to_pope_paul_iii.pdf), a Harvard Classics translation hosted by Ohio State, and Sachiko Kusukawa's [Copernicus's Book](https://www.sites.hps.cam.ac.uk/starry/coperbooks.html), Cambridge History and Philosophy of Science. The first supports Copernicus's named supporters and account of hesitation; the second supplies the publication history and Osiander's unauthorized preface. No reconstructed deathbed scene, invented private motive, or assertion of a single religious response.

The addition distinguishes an aid to calculation from a claim about physical reality. It explicitly denies that this makes present speculation equivalent to Copernican astronomy. P-06 becomes “The Moving Earth (Frombork and Nuremberg, 1543),” reflecting both composition and publication without declaring changed cosmology a universal demotion.

## Chapter 5: a contract beside the engine

Read Science Museum Group's [catalogue and interpretation of the March 1, 1786 agreement](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co50966/original-indenture-of-agreement-for-erection-of-a-agreements), object 1908-185. It identifies the parties, distillery, ten-horsepower engine, erection and maintenance responsibility, and patent premium. The record explains the related savings-based approach of the business. The manuscript identifies the museum's interpretation rather than claiming a new transcription of the original indenture.

Addition follows the distinction between measured improvement and contractual obligations. It makes no new estimate of workers displaced or a controlled comparison of economic outcomes. The earlier USDA series remains intact. P-07's unsupported exact endpoints are broadened to “The Horse and the Ledger (Britain and United States, eighteenth to twentieth centuries).”
