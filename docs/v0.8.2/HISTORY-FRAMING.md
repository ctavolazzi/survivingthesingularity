Date: 2026-09-22. Scope: P01 and P23 only. Full Preface and Conclusion were read before editing. Additions stay inside the precedents; all nonheading v0.8.1 prose remains in its original order, including the final Bear Flag scene. No other chapter was changed for this assignment.

## Preface, P01

Added 236 net words, including the expanded heading. The addition reads Austen's defense of novels alongside her heroine's misinterpretation of ordinary life. It supplies literary texture while resisting the simplistic claim that objections to a new medium must be foolish.

Primary source read directly: Jane Austen, *Northanger Abbey*, Chapters 5 and 25, Project Gutenberg text, https://www.gutenberg.org/files/121/121-h/121-h.htm. Chapter 5 presents the friends reading during rainy weather, the narrator's defense of novelists, and the imagined reader's embarrassment about admitting to reading a novel. Chapter 25 traces Catherine's frightening inferences to her reading and imagination. These are fictional incidents and a novelist's intervention, explicitly identified as such in the manuscript. They are not evidence that a particular real woman was shamed or that all British readers shared one attitude. Only three words of the novel are quoted.

Heading correction: "The Reading Rage (1790s)" becomes "The Reading Rage (Britain, eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries)." The old decade was narrower than the existing historical survey and the added Austen testimony. The edition's authorial advertisement says the work had been finished in 1803; the revision avoids using that composition date as a publication date.

Self-critique: the modern connection is an analogy about judgment and access, not a claim that generative AI and novels have the same risks. The passage preserves Austen's criticism of bad inference alongside her defense of reading. It does not turn the fictional General Tilney into a historical perpetrator, nor imply Catherine's fears establish that people with authority are harmless. A repeated transition in the first draft was changed. Chapter-link anchors were tested against the actual HTML; initially guessed anchors failed and were corrected to `link2HCH0005` and `link2HCH0025`.

## Conclusion, P23

Added 269 net words, including the expanded heading. The addition gives Stoll's argument concrete content, distinguishes changes in institutional practice from improvements in fairness, and pairs forecast uncertainty with a documented decision about software access.

Sources read:

- Clifford Stoll, 1995 Newsweek essay excerpt in Elon University's *Imagining the Internet* archive: https://www.elon.edu/u/imagining/expert_predictions/the-internet-bah-why-cyberspace-isnt-and-never-will-be-nirvana/. The attributed primary-text excerpt supports his two decades online, acknowledged pleasure and friendships, the list of enthusiasts' promises, and his categorical statements about newspapers, teachers, and government. The publisher's current page returned an access error in this session. The revision therefore explicitly describes the excerpt preserved by Elon and does not claim to have checked the complete essay. The old publisher citation is retained. No new direct quotation is used. The archive's day-level date is not repeated because other bibliographic versions differ.
- Tim Smith and François Flückiger, "Licensing the Web," CERN: https://home.cern/science/computing/the-birth-of-the-web/licensing-web/. This institutional account reproduces the April 30, 1993 public-domain statement, identifies the three software components, and describes the 1994 server release under a permissive license with CERN retaining copyright. The addition paraphrases these specific events. It does not assert that all internet technology was released, that everyone received internet access, or that all derivatives had to remain open. No direct quotation is used.

Heading correction: "The Passing Fad (1995)" becomes "What the Forecast Missed (1993 to 1995)." The new title does not put an unverified label in Stoll's mouth, and the period accommodates the CERN decision.

Self-critique: the release provides evidence of a deliberate access decision, not a controlled causal estimate of the Web's growth or proof that open licensing guarantees equality. The food comparison expressly retains material, work, and delivery requirements. The teacher example concerns the difference between useful assistance and replacement, not a prediction about education. The ending still lands in the existing story, after the historical material.
