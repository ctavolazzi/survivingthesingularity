## Long version

### 0:00 Cold open (15 seconds)

[Tight on you.]

> Two film companies. Same product. Same collapse. Same years.
>
> One went bankrupt. The other one [VERIFY] is selling skin cream and making medicine.
>
> The difference was one question.

[Title card: THE MIRROR TWIN.]

### 0:15 Same disaster (about 45 seconds)

[On screen: two columns, KODAK and FUJIFILM, both with a line falling after 2000.]

> A few episodes back I told you about Kodak. They built the first digital camera,
> couldn't let it eat their film business, and filed for bankruptcy in 2012.
>
> Kodak had a twin. Fujifilm. Japan's film giant. And [VERIFY] around 2000, Fujifilm's
> film business peaked and started collapsing on basically the same schedule.
>
> Same wave. Same beach. Different answer.

### 1:00 The question (about 1 minute)

> Kodak kept asking: how do we save film?
>
> Fujifilm asked something else. What is our chemistry actually good at?
>
> [VERIFY this paragraph.] And when they listed it out, it wasn't "film." It was thin
> coatings, layered incredibly precisely. It was collagen, because film is mostly
> gelatin, and gelatin is collagen. It was chemicals that stop photos from fading over
> time.
>
> And then they asked: who else needs that?
>
> [VERIFY.] Anti-aging chemicals: cosmetics. Collagen: skincare and medicine. Precision
> films: the layers inside LCD screens.
>
> The wave destroyed the product. It couldn't touch the skills.

### 2:00 The mechanism (about 30 seconds)

[Back on you.]

> Kodak thought it was in the film business. Fujifilm figured out it was in the
> chemistry business.
>
> Your product can die. Your skills travel. But only if you can name them before you
> need to.

### 2:30 What to do (about 1 minute)

[Numbers on screen. This is the heart of the episode, so take your time.]

> So let's actually do it. Three things.
>
> One. Write your capability list. Tonight. One page. And here's the rule: no job titles
> and no products allowed. Not "accountant." Not "barista." Not "graphic designer."
>
> Dig under those. Explaining hard things simply. Calming down angry people. Seeing
> what's wrong with a system. Making physical things precisely. Earning trust fast.
>
> If a line could go on a business card, it's a product. Cross it out and dig for the
> skill underneath.
>
> Two. For each skill, name two places that need it that aren't your industry. Film
> chemistry went to skincare. Where does yours go? You're not committing to anything.
> You're drawing the map while you're calm.
>
> Three. Put a date on your calendar, once a year, and ask the Fujifilm question on
> purpose. Is my product more at risk than last year? Which skill do I build next?
