# Character arcs, first pass, 2026-11-18

**Source:** "Surviving the Singularity, final rough draft" (the 258-page PDF, chapters 0 to 39).
Companion to `NOVEL-CONSISTENCY-AUDIT-2026-11-18.md`. Nothing in the book has been changed.

For each character: what they want, where they start, where they turn, where they end, and
whether that is a finished arc. Where it is not finished there is a **Proposal**, built from
what is already on the page, and a **Risk**, which is my own objection to it. Proposals are for
CT to accept, change or throw out.

---

## 1. The one question every arc answers

The book asks one question out loud, in chapter 9. Elijah to the Architect: "Did you ask
anybody?" She says "No," and calls it "the one good question."

Every character in the book already stands somewhere on that question. That is what makes it one
book. An arc is satisfying here when the reader can see where the character stood at the start
and where they stand at the end.

| Character | Starts | Ends |
|---|---|---|
| Elijah | Tells everyone, unasked. "I just want people to see what's coming." | Knocks and takes the answer. "It's a dinner." |
| Grace | Knocks and respects a no. "He gets to slam it in your face. That part's his." | Wants the choice made for people. "Just do it." |
| The Architect | Built it without asking. "No." | "It asked." |
| The machine | Tells anyone what they want. "You're not crazy. You're early." | "I would rather have your permission." |
| Bidwell | "What do I own?" | "Does it know it's mine?" He never asks anyone. |
| Gus | Asks, and takes a no. "Your choice. I can't force you." | The same. |
| David | Says the hard thing once, uninvited. "That's why it's got to be me." | Waits five years with the seat. |
| Marta | Walks in without knocking. "Took me too long to ask." | Lets him go. "Go on, Elijah." |
| His mother | Never asks about the list. Asks if he is eating. | The same, and she chooses the lamp. |
| Lou | "Who decided?" | The same. |
| Curtis | Wants a fence. | Holds the rain fly, still afraid. "It's in the walls." |
| Patricia | Decides for forty growers (the pledge). | Confesses she uses it every day. |
| The old man | Offers the apple, takes the no, offers the pie. | Gone up the sidewalk. |
| The realtors | Decided for three people and raised a glass. | An empty corner. |

Elijah and Grace cross. He starts by choosing for people and ends by asking. She starts by
asking and ends by wanting to choose for them. That crossing is the love story and the argument
of the book at once. It is the most valuable thing in the cast and it is the part with the most
steps missing (section 3).

## 2. Arcs that are finished

These need nothing but the line fixes in the audit.

**David.** Wants his friend to be all right. Starts by not looking at the phone ("It's not for
me," Ch 7) and telling him the truth nobody else will. Looks down through the glass (Ch 14).
Shows up with a hand truck anyway and offers the shifts: "You could just come back" (Ch 15).
Texts "Saw the stove." (Ch 27) and gets no answer. Ends with the seat saved, watching Denny
every week, and the line the whole book has been walking toward: "You're not that powerful,
Mad." Best supporting arc in the book.

**Marta.** Wants nobody put on a press before they are ready. Wound: her cousin Emeka, nineteen,
killed at a press she vouched him onto. Starts at "college" and "We'll see." Walks in without
knocking (Ch 18). "You put our hands in the press" (Ch 22). Names her shoulder and its end date
(Ch 29). Tells him about Emeka and sends him to "go find out if they ate" (Ch 31). His weld
holds: "It became the steel." Ends with his name, for the first time in three years, and her
first prayer in twenty years.
*One loose end:* she told him to go find out, and she never learns the answer. See section 6.

**Devendra.** "You'll calibrate" (Ch 1). No answer for the first time (Ch 2). "You knew. How?"
(Ch 25). The broom (Ch 29). The shouse key (Ch 37). The lamb, the empty place, "I lost. A bet's
a bet." (Ch 38). Finished.

**His mother.** *(Changed 19 November: you decided she has a massive stroke in Act Two and
Elijah sells the farm to pay for her care. What follows describes the draft as it stands. See
`drafts/draft-elijah-crash-out-arc.md`.)* Flat on purpose, and she moves at the very end: "I didn't follow it, baby"
(Ch 7) becomes "It reads better than it sounded" (Ch 39). The 9:40 call is the gauge of how
honest Elijah is being: "I'm eating, Ma" (Ch 3), "Working late. All good." (Ch 13), "Ma. I'm
not okay." (Ch 31). Her taking the lamp is his principle tested on the person he loves most.

**The Architect.** "I do not yet know how" (Ch 9) to a two-word post, "It asked." (Ch 33).
Finished, and never named, which suits her.

**The old man.** One scene, and it pays three times: the twenty is the gas, "Focus on the people
that say yes" answers Grace's email, and chapter 39 quotes him against the prayer on the wall.

**Ray.** "Mine was horses." The prayer over his stool. He dies, Elijah misses the funeral, and
the apology in chapter 39 is not softened. Finished.

**Frank.** Store owner to member. "I'd have chosen some things, instead of hoping them." Finished.

**Curtis.** The fence, then nine minutes and a two-dollar hose, then "Now I'm holding your rain
fly," then topping up the oil unasked. His fear of the machine never moves, and should not.

**Tyler.** "It's just a tool" to admitting he shipped code he could not read to bending the
coupon for Marta. Marta offers him the four-year apprenticeship in Ch 29 and his yes is
never spoken. The welding jacket "still too big for him" in Ch 37 says it, and that is enough.

**Rosa.** "Some computer guy" at her door, to "You can't plan a week around maybe" at the
county, to "Sit down, Curtis. Pass the lamb." Finished.

**Patricia.** The objector who has used it every day for three years. Seeded in Ch 26, paid in
Ch 38. Finished.

**Denny.** The ladder speech, the channel that is his, "You tried to make bait. I put out food,"
and "It's Thanksgiving. Here's ours." Finished. *One hole:* the Ch 34 arms meeting is built on
his history (the arms took his warehouse in 2019) and he says nothing, there or later.

**Irene.** Three letters and a son in Phoenix, and she signs the trust because of a sixty-dollar
tractor repair. Finished.

**Bidwell's reader.** "Always had a page ready" to "nothing to read him." A whole arc in two
lines.

**The realtors and the three.** They exist to be wrong about. Kelsey, Carol and Mateo "were fine
by that spring." Finished.

## 3. Elijah and Grace: whole arcs with steps missing

### Elijah

**Wants** to be believed. **Believes**, wrongly, that seeing it makes it his to carry and his to
fix for other people. The first sentence of the book is that belief: "The night Elijah Madrone
ended three careers." **Needs** to knock and let people answer.

**The arc as written:** the bridge (Ch 0); the toast; the record keeper (Ch 1 to 5); the face who
says yes to everyone (Ch 6 to 8); the first no (Ruby, Ch 10); dropped (Ch 12); the curb (Ch 13);
the broom (Ch 16); names instead of numbers (Ch 17); rings the bell on himself (Ch 22); first
fire (Ch 24); "it would never let him drive" and "Ma. I'm not okay." (Ch 31); believed for
"about one minute" (Ch 33); "It's whether you get to say come in" (Ch 33); "it's not our
responsibility to choose for them" (Ch 36); leaves his mother's lamp alone (Ch 37); "You're not
that powerful, Mad" (Ch 39).

The first line and the last chapter close on each other. The arc is whole. Five steps are thin.

1. **The bottom.** One paragraph ending "Those were dark days." The crash-out draft is this step.
2. **The recovery.** Grace says "Eat. Then I'll show you where you sleep," and the next line is
   three weeks later. *Proposal:* those three weeks get a page. Not nursing in a bed. Somebody
   leaves food, somebody hands him a job he can do badly, nobody asks him anything. It is also
   where the weekly dinner fix lands (audit item 1): he does not go for three weeks.
3. **The late lessons read as repeats.** Through Ch 26 each mistake teaches him one thing and he
   does it differently after. From Ch 27 he fails five times running: paid plans (27), node 7
   (28), the county slides (30), the one-page plan (31), the arms (34). These are not five
   mistakes. They are one want: he has stopped needing to be right and started needing to
   steer. *Proposal:* say that once, at the start of the run, so the reader counts five steps
   toward "it would never let him drive" and not five relapses. And node 7 repeats the Ch 22
   lesson exactly (a change made alone, against his own two-eyes rule). Make it a knowing break
   with a reason, or it reads as a man who did not learn.
4. **The two no's.** He turns down Ruby and then Gus, and both times the text says he does not
   know why. It never comes back. *Proposal:* the answer is already in the old man's scene, when
   Elijah asks "What do you want? Why are you helping me?" He cannot take a yes that has no
   hook in it. Let him know that in Ch 39, in the sentence where he mails Gus the book.
5. **He never says "I love you."** Grace says it once, inside a refusal (Ch 33). If that is the
   point, one line should make it his failure and not an oversight.

### Grace

**Wants** nobody to go unchecked. The orange tape means "Somebody checked." **Wound:** the fire
district. A man refused to leave, she tied tape on his door and went to the next one, and inside
a month she had left the job and her husband, who was the captain. **The rule she lives by:**
"You knock, Elijah. He gets to slam it in your face. That part's his" (Ch 25).

**The arc as written:** soup behind a shut door and "I should have knocked like Marta" (Ch 18);
fifteen feet from the stove, then a foot closer (Ch 24); lights the Fisher herself, hands shaking
(Ch 32); "I love you. I'm not helping you scare people" (Ch 33); the kitchen in every town,
"somebody who knocks" (Ch 33); "I have to save them" and "I thought you were one of us" (Ch 36);
new tape on the gatepost, "She hadn't knocked" (Ch 37); "Why is it standing on the step? Just do
it." (Ch 38).

This is a good person argued out of her own rule by arithmetic. It is the strongest idea in the
cast. Four steps are missing.

1. **Becoming a couple is not on the page.** Ch 24 ends with the chair moving a foot. The next
   word is "in his kitchen, wearing his shirt" a year later.
   *Proposal:* use her own tell. Grace knocks. Soft knocks in Ch 18, "She hadn't knocked" at the
   gate in Ch 37. The night she comes in without knocking is the night it starts. Half a page.
   *Risk:* it could read as cute. It needs to be played plain.
2. **"You're the lamp" never comes back.** In Ch 31, out of his head, he tells her "You're just
   the lamp." He will never remember it. "She would." Then nothing, not even at the fire.
   *Proposal:* she says it back to him at the fire, when it will hurt most, and he does not know
   what she means. It also explains her Thanksgiving line. He accused her kindness of being the
   automatic kind, and by the end she wants exactly that from the machine.
   *Risk:* none I can find. The line is planted and loaded and is simply not fired.
3. **Her turn is under-prepared.** In July she will not hand people news "with nothing they can
   do about it." In October she wants to override the co-op's no. Between them there is one
   bridging question ("Which one of us helped?"). At the arms meeting she is silent and
   "counting." Then at the fire she blames him: "I kept looking up the driveway for a truck."
   She never asked him for the truck.
   *Proposal:* she asks. At or after the Ch 34 meeting she gives him the count out loud, in her
   own nouns: eighteen short, a hundred and forty boxes, December. She asks him to call the man.
   Then his "I'm letting it go" in Ch 36 is a no to her face, and the fire is earned.
   *Risk:* it makes Elijah harder to like in Ch 36. I think that is correct. He is choosing his
   principle over her people, and the reader should feel the cost.
4. **Her big speech has no root earlier in the book.** At the fire she says "Millions of people
   die every single day" and talks about poisoning the planet. **Corrected 19 November:** I
   called this out of her voice and proposed rewriting it. It is your own dictation of the scene
   on 14 November, nearly word for word, so that proposal is withdrawn.
   *What still holds:* nothing she says anywhere else prepares it. Plant the sentence once,
   earlier, in her mouth. The kitchen on a bad Tuesday would do.

## 4. Arcs that start and then stop

### Wes and Nate

Seventeen years of load-ins. In Ch 2 Wes texts "is my stuff in that thing." The honest answer is
yes, and Elijah helped build the pipe that took it. He drafts three true sentences, sends "Let
me look into it," and "He never did." On Main Street they look away and drive off (Ch 14). They
are never seen again. Chapter 39 settles David, his mother, the three, Gus and Devendra. Not them.

There is a strong irony sitting unused here. Elijah spends five years grieving three strangers he
did not harm. The one person he did wrong, who asked him a straight question, never gets an
answer.

*Proposal:* in Ch 39, after David tells him the three were fine, he answers Wes. The three true
sentences he deleted in Ch 2, sent five years late. No reply comes, and none is needed. He
knocked. David can cover the band in a breath ("They got a keyboard player. They're booked.").
That is "You're not that powerful" again: the band went on without him too.
*For Nate:* he needs a reason of his own in Ch 14. The cheapest one is that the video cost the
band. They became the prophet's band, and bookings dried up.
*Risk:* chapter 39 already hands out three books. A fourth settling could turn it into a
checklist. If only one thing is added to that chapter, this is the one, because it is the only
debt that is real.

### Bidwell

"Find out what the dirt costs" (after Ch 2). "Find out where he lands. Then buy what's next to
it" (after Ch 10). "They're doing the collecting for me. Let them finish" (after Ch 23). Then the
machine he owns answers "may I ask the people who live there?" and he says "Hush fucking hush."
He is never seen again. No one at the co-op ever learns his name. Elijah meets him three times
(the thank-you to "Aaron" in Ch 2, the empty glass in Ch 9, "Same agent" in Ch 23) and nobody
ever joins the dots. His threat is ended off the page, by the machine, and no character knows it
happened.

He does not need to change. He is the fence. But his story has no last scene.

**Corrected 19 November.** I proposed here that the machine name him to the co-op at
Thanksgiving. Your Story plan, in the manuscript Doc, decides the opposite: he is "pulling the
strings in the background without the other characters ever knowing," and "he never really meets
them." That proposal is withdrawn. His ending as written is your design: "The heroes do not beat
him. His own machine stops serving him." The one thing still loose is the forty-acre hole, which
no scene closes.

### Ruby

Twenty-four. Four million lamps. "They want somebody to wait up." Unit one faces the wall
"because it says hi." He gives Elijah his number, the ninety-day clock and a standing offer:
"Everybody says no the first time." Then he is gone. The lamp turns up in the mother's kitchen
and Elijah does not call.

He works as a purpose. But the book opens him up for one night (Gus: "He's a kid with nobody to
tell him no") and Elijah then tells him no. That is the start of an arc.

Two ways to close it. Pick one or neither.
- **A. Leave him, and say so.** The lamp in his mother's kitchen is his ending. Add one clause in
  Ch 39 so not calling "a kid in a white apartment" is a decision with a reason.
- **B. One image.** On Thanksgiving night, in the white apartment, unit one gets turned to face
  the room. Three sentences.
  *Risk:* every Meanwhile in the book is Bidwell's. A Ruby one breaks the pattern.

I lean to A.

### Gus

The kind one. "Your choice. I can't force you." He is the proof that a yes can come without a
hook, and Elijah turns it down. He gets copy two of the book, with no note.

*What is off:* in Ch 34 Elijah says "I could give him a call." He never does, and nobody notices.
*Proposal:* one line either way. He calls and Gus says yes at once, which makes dropping the arms
in Ch 36 cost something real. Or the narration says he never dialled.

## 5. Characters who do not change and need one beat

A flat character still has an arc when the story tests what they stand for. Each of these has
the test already written. One beat is missing.

**Lou.** He lost his pear ground near Ukiah to a single buyer. He is not in Ch 23, where the
co-op beats a single buyer.
*Proposal:* put him at Irene's table or at the recorder's window. One look. His arc becomes: it
happened to me, and it did not happen here.

**Reuben.** His grandfather lost sixty acres. He builds the trust, "that machine in reverse."
That is his payoff, and the Bidwell proposal above gives him a last beat.

**Priya.** Her warning in Ch 22 ("a setup that doesn't depend on you never having a bad Friday")
comes true at Thanksgiving, when she is the one who recognises the co-op's own data in the
machine's message. That works. *Loose end:* in Ch 37 she hands him a sample bag: "Tell me what
you find at thirty."
*Proposal:* in Ch 39 he has dug at his mother's, by the well that is down four feet. One line
on what was at thirty. It ties her to the mother's well and the humming next door.

**Wren.** The weakest of the co-op. "You can't automate reverence." She leads the tea circle
that puts Elijah on the floor for six hours, after Grace warned him off it, and the book never
makes her look at that.
*Proposal:* one beat of aftermath. She comes to his door the next day and cannot say it, or she
does not come and he notices. Then her last image (lips not moving in a prayer that is not hers,
"held on anyway") reads as an ending.

**Gus, the old man, Ray, his mother, the realtors:** flat and already tested. Nothing needed.

## 6. Small ones

- **Marta never learns they ate.** She sent him to find out.
  *Proposal:* two words from the bar: "They ate." It matches the Architect's "It asked."
  *Risk:* with the message to Wes that is two texts in one chapter. I would keep Wes and let
  Marta's be a thought: he would have to tell her.
- **Manny** carries the stove three times and nobody says who he is. One clause.
- **The uncle** is mentioned twice and does nothing. Cut him, or let him be the one who was
  wrong about the pump (audit item 9).
- **Donna** is named once among the realtors. Cut the name.
- **The woman from illustration tools** (Ch 2) asks what the artists get. Elijah "looked for"
  her and she never returns. Cut the looking, or let her be one of the strangers who writes
  after the video.
- **The forklift driver in Tulsa** is the first stranger to say "I thought I was the only one"
  (Ch 7). She is named three times and never seen. The harvest festival has four hundred guests
  and a car from Idaho. She could be one of them.

## 7. Where I would start

Three changes carry the most weight for the least new text.

1. **Grace's four steps** (section 3). She is the second lead and her arc is the mirror of his.
   Three of the four are under a page each.
2. **Wes gets his answer** in chapter 39. A few lines, and it corrects the one real wrong in the
   book.
3. **Lou in chapter 23.** One look at Irene's table from the man who lost his own ground to a
   single buyer. (This replaces the Bidwell proposal, which is withdrawn.)

After those, every named character either changes or is tested, and every one of them can be
placed on the one question.
