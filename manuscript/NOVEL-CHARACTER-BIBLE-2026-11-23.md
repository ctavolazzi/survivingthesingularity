# Surviving the Singularity: Character Bible

One entry per character: what they want, where they come from, and how they talk. Facts are checked against v5 as it stood after the final edit of Nov 24. Where an older dossier or note disagrees with v5, the entry says so under Flags and doesn't pick. Any motivation marked (proposed) is my reading of the page, not a call he has made.

## Elijah Gaelen Madrone

**Core motivation**
- Surface want (his call): to be heard and believed. "I just want people to see what's coming" (Ch 0, Ch 8). "Am I crazy?" (Ch 0, 7, 15, 31) is the fear under it.
- Real want (his call): to belong.
- Under it (proposed): being believed is how he tries to earn a place. He counts only when he's right, and he can't stand being alone with what he sees.
- Payoff (proposed): the co-op takes him in once he stops needing to be right. Ma believed him all along: she copied his list into her notebook and filled the barn with buckets. "She had believed him. One person had, and it was her." (Ch 13)
- Mirror (proposed): Grace needs someone to stay; Elijah needs somewhere to belong. Neither can ask for it.

**Backstory (checked against v5)**
- A Sonoma County farm kid. Ma's place is ten miles out of town, and the madrone hill behind it was his grandmother's: "That's your tree... It can burn to the ground and come back from the root" (Ch 0).
- His father was Joe (Ch 13). Melody, his half-sister on his father's side, lives in Miami.
- In sixth grade he was the kid with the K'Nex roller coaster, the Magic cards and a binder of dragons (Ch 1).
- He played Telecaster in Crop Dust with Nate and Wes; there's a photo from the Gravenstein fair in 2016. He pawned the guitar in Santa Rosa (Ch 15). The amp is a Fishman Loudbox Mini Charge (Ch 5).
- He drove delivery for six years (Ch 11), taught himself to code, and worked on Nina Haverford's data team at Claypot, where he built the pipeline that fed Companion. He was laid off eleven months after the toast.
- He signed a trust page at twenty-six. That let him sell the farm in September 2024, after Ma's stroke, to an LLC that is Bidwell's money. He told no one. Reuben has held the deed with his name on it for a year and a half (Ch 29).
- Ages on the page: 35 (Ch 7), 36 (Ch 15), 37 (Ch 20). He spent five months in the truck in Santa Rosa, then went 800 miles north in March 2025.
- At the co-op he lives over the pump house, then in the shouse with Walt Calder's stove (Ch 24). He fills eleven notebooks (Ch 35). In Ch 37 he plays "The Weight" on the amp once, alone and rusty, gives the key to Lou, renames Ruby's contact "Don't," and leaves the amp by Walt's stove with "I love you too" in the pouch (found in Ch 38). In Ch 39 he still has Ruby's number and chooses not to call it, and he turns Wes down for now. The Epilogue ends with him sending Grace a recording of himself playing.

**Voice (2026)**
- Length: short and flat when cornered ("Yeah," "I'm fine," "How long."). When he's lit up he runs long, stacking clauses with "Like," and lists of three.
- Vocabulary: data and systems words (n, offsets, the model, the water number, cooling), pump-and-manual practical words, and a Millennial ex-tech register ("for sure," "kind of").
- Tics: "Yeah. No, yeah, of course." (Act Two). He repeats for weight: "This is different. I'm telling you. This is different." He hedges ("It's kind of dumb") right before saying the thing anyway.
- How it changes: the prophet line through Ch 20 ("Nobody gets a vote on this. It's like weather."), questions later in Act Three ("What do you need this week?"), and plain words in Act Four ("I'd rather have been wrong." "Not yet, Ma.").
- Would never say (proposed): "I told you so" in any form. "Reckon" or other old-timey talk. On the page he does say "I need help" once, to strangers on camera (Ch 13), and it goes badly. To someone he knows, not until "Ma. I'm not okay." (Ch 31). He says "folks" twice after two years at the farm (Ch 27, Ch 31).
- Real lines from v5:
  - "No, no. This is different. I'm telling you. This is different." (Ch 0)
  - "I didn't know. I was frightened. I still am sometimes. What do you need this week?" (Ch 25, to Devendra)
  - "Ma. I'm not okay." (Ch 31)

**Flags (dossier vs v5; v5 not changed)**
1. The "01 Elijah" dossier still says Second Crop and his grandmother's Fisher stove. v5 has Crop Dust, the Fishman amp, and Walt Calder's stove.
2. The dossier says he deletes Ruby's number (Ch 37). v5 has him rename it "Don't" (a locked line).
3. The dossier says he hands Devendra the shouse key. v5 has Lou (Ch 37).
4. The dossier says "five weeks homeless." v5 and the Appendix say five months.
5. "Born spring 1988" is only in the Nov 23 notes, not on the page. The ages on the page fit it.

## Grace

**Core motivation**
- Surface want: to get to every person on the route before it's too late, even if that means choosing for them. "People say no to all kinds of things that would save them" (Ch 36). At Thanksgiving she wants the machine to "Just do it" (Ch 38).
- Real want (his call): for someone to stay. She wants to be enough. On the page:
  - the porch race her sister Faith always won (Ch 22)
  - "I used to think if I was just good enough" (Ch 22)
  - the captain who let her go: "I still left." "He let me." (Ch 24)
  - Faith on the porch with their dad: "She stayed." (Ch 33)
  - the soup she left on his step for three days, waiting (Ch 18; she names it in Ch 33)
  - "I thought you were one of us" and "I can't believe I did this again" (Ch 36)
  - at the bonfire she looks halfway over her shoulder and slows, long enough for him to say something; her chin quivers, the tears don't come (Ch 36)
  - "She took a long time with the straps." (Ch 37)
  - Nobody says it outright.

**Backstory (checked against v5)**
- A dark-skinned woman in her thirties (Ch 16). Faith is two years older. Faith got the Christmas solo, and Grace got the robe. They raced to the porch rail when Dad came home, and the winner got to sit with him while he played. Faith always won (Ch 22).
- Her dad is "the Loudbox guy" who doesn't play anymore. He gave his days to the TV, then the phone, then one man on the phone who "means me." Faith sits next to him on that porch now. The sisters say Merry Christmas, and it takes about four minutes (Ch 22, Ch 33).
- She worked for a fire district as the one sent ahead to knock. A man at the end of a road refused. She wrote REFUSED and tied orange tape on his gate. The fire came that night, and the tape was the only thing on the road still a color (Ch 24).
- She was married to a captain who always said "You can't make them leave, Grace." She quit the job and left him within one month (Ch 24). "He let me." She hadn't sat that close to a stove in six years.
- At the co-op she runs the town kitchen and the route: 140 households, a budget that keeps running out (June, then December). She signs off "Blessings, G." She loves bass music (Tipper, the October festival, Ch 25), and she has sat with people on tea (Ch 31).
- Her grandmother said "there's one of him every couple of generations" (Ch 33).
- Endings: she lights Walt's stove at noon on Thanksgiving, takes the blanket off Elijah's amp without switching it on, sings "The Weight" the way her dad used to on the porch, and finds the note in the pouch. Her voice goes at the chorus. She types "Come home," deletes it, and says one thing into Denny's phone (Ch 38). Elijah sees her on Denny's livestream: "Stove's going. I lit it myself." (Ch 39). He never hears her play.

**Voice (2026)**
- Two registers: the soft voice, and the hitch voice for trailer hitches and real anger (named in Ch 17).
- Length: medium. Short instructions, then one long run when she tells a story (the fire district story is her longest speech).
- Vocabulary: route numbers said exactly ("a hundred and forty boxes"), the language of organizing ("Can we pause? Can we just pause, and check in?"), church-raised politeness ("Blessings"), and real swearing when the hitch voice comes out.
- Tics: she uses a person's name to land a correction ("Face down, Curtis." "It feeds people, Frank." "You knock, Elijah."). Statements become instructions. Hands on forearms and faces.
- Would never say: what she wants for herself, outright (his rule). Would never ask for help or say "I'm fine" (proposed).
- Real lines from v5:
  - "I used to think if I was just good enough, she'd stop keeping score. Turns out she was never keeping score. I was." (Ch 22)
  - "You knock, Elijah. He gets to slam it in your face. That part's his. But you knock." (Ch 25)
  - "People will vote for the fire if it's warm." (Ch 27)

**Flags (dossier vs v5; v5 not changed)**
1. Full name Grace Lane (his call, Nov 24). It isn't used on the page; she's just Grace. Her dossier said "Grace Arden Land" and is now corrected.
2. The dossier quotes "I have to save them" (Ch 36). v5 doesn't have that line; the nearest is "People say no to all kinds of things that would save them."
3. The dossier says she "lights the Fisher" in Ch 32 and Ch 38. In v5 it's Walt's stove ("Grace lit the stove at noon," Ch 38).
4. The dossier lists her want as "to save everyone" and her flaw as "can't let people choose." That's now the surface want, under his real want above.

## Lou Parker

**Core motivation**
- Surface want: to feed the 140 households and keep the co-op's tools in the co-op's hands. "Long as the robot's mine" (Ch 20).
- Real want (proposed): never to be bought again, and to hand what he knows to someone who will stay. He lost his orchard to one buyer (Ch 20). He calls Elijah "son" and tells him the hard thing: "You'd rather be gone and tidy than here and a mess" (Ch 37).

**Backstory (checked against v5)**
- He grew pears outside Ukiah and lost the orchard to one buyer and one price, after the packing house that bought everybody's pears got bought by a bigger one (Ch 20, Ch 23). In Ch 34 he tells Curtis he lost a crop fighting the packing house over grades.
- In Ch 17 he's "about sixty," cracking walnuts without looking at his hands. In Ch 20 he's sixty-one. One knee is titanium, "and the titanium one's the good one" (Ch 20).
- His wife, Marianne, taught fourth grade for thirty-one years and left him her pension (Ch 20; named in Ch 33). His mother, a Parker, raised him alone and worked nights at the hospital in Ukiah. He said the Our Father in the back seat so she would come home (Ch 20, Ch 33, Ch 38). He started the co-op with Greg Holt, who tried to run it and left (Ch 33, Ch 37). The joke "cheaper than my wife's nephew" is in Ch 17.
- He grows most of what's on the table, on land that isn't his.
- Appendix only: his mother and Bidwell's mother were half-sisters, so he and Bidwell are cousins. Nobody says so in the book.
- Endings: he takes the shouse key and sees the amp left by the stove, "the first untidy thing you've done since you got here" (Ch 37). At Thanksgiving he cooks, lays one more place at the corner nearest the stove, and pours one glass there after Grace finds the note (Ch 38).

**Voice (2026)**
- Length: short questions that sound simple and aren't, then one hard statement. When he tells a story it runs long and plain, with no lesson stated. He also has a second register, which the book calls "pure ivory tower" (Ch 20). Three times he preaches outright: at the drip tape (Ch 20), at the tomato dinner, where he stands up (Ch 33), and at the fence post and the barrel stove (Ch 35: "mutually assured survival," "And furthermore.").
- Vocabulary: a grower's numbers (cost, hands, households, rows), dry profanity ("Shit," said happily), and plain words for big ideas ("five scared people and a clock").
- Tics: nicknames that tell you where Elijah stands with him: "Ivy League" (Ch 20 to 26), "professor" when Elijah lectures (Ch 26, Ch 35), "Thoreau" once he eats what he grew (Ch 33 on), and "son" (Ch 29, Ch 37). He answers a question with a question. He changes the subject to the work ("The plants look thirsty"). He says "man" to everyone and "honey" to Wren and Patricia. "My friend" appears only in Ch 35.
- Would never say (proposed): a policy word. An earlier note here said "never a sermon," but the page lets him preach three times (see Length). He's sixty-one in 2026, a grower who uses a phone and a laptop when he has to, so no "reckon" or old-timey talk.
- Real lines from v5:
  - "Who ate it at your place?" (Ch 20)
  - "That's up to you, big boy." (Ch 35)
  - "That's the whole trouble with you, son. You'd rather be gone and tidy than here and a mess." (Ch 37)

**Flags (dossier vs v5; v5 not changed)**
1. The dossier quotes "I'm saying pick one" (Ch 26). It isn't in v5.
2. The dossier says Curtis gives him the hose ("Keep it," Ch 37) and he lifts it like a trophy. In v5 Curtis says "Keep it" to Elijah, about the two dollars of hose, and Lou lifts the irrigation hose over his head "like a man holding up a trophy" (both Ch 37).
3. The dossier names the nephew Kyle. v5 doesn't name him.
4. The cousin tie to Bidwell is only in the Appendix, so readers learn it there. That fits his call that the watcher is never revealed, but it's worth his knowing that it's the only place it appears.
