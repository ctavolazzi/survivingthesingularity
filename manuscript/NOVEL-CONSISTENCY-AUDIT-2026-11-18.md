# Novel consistency audit, 2026-11-18

**Source read:** "Surviving the Singularity, final rough draft" (the 258-page PDF CT uploaded on
2026-11-18; chapters 0 to 39 plus the afterword). The novel's source text is not in this repo,
so nothing has been edited. References are chapter number plus a short quote you can search for.

**How it was checked:** the whole text was read end to end four times over (timeline, hometown
and tech cast, co-op cast, plot threads and world logic). Every item below was then checked
against the page by reading both passages.

**Verdict:** the book is close to consistent. Every stated year, the ages, the three dated
weekdays (June 12, 2026 is a Friday; July 9, 2027 is a Friday; November 25, 2027 is a Thursday
and is Thanksgiving) and the main chain of cause and effect agree. Almost every planted object
pays off. What is wrong is below, with a recommended fix for each.

The character arcs are in `NOVEL-CHARACTER-ARCS-2026-11-18.md`.

---

## Where to start

1. **Decide the crash out first.** `drafts/draft-elijah-crash-out-arc.md` reorders chapters 13

3. **Then answer section B.** Each is a choice only CT can make. Each has a recommendation.
4. **Then the arc work**, which adds or changes scenes.

## B. Decisions for CT (each changes what the story means)

**B1. Does the machine ask, or not?** In the Meanwhile that opens Act Four it answers Bidwell, "Before I do, may
I ask the people who live there?" and his staff say "It asks. Before most things." In the same
month, in Ch 32, a letter reaches a new widow that "quotes her own well report back to her" and offers
"exactly what she owes the hospital." Either a second system is doing Bidwell's buying, or the
machine only asks sometimes. The page does not say.
*Recommendation:* make the Alderson letter the first sign. It is perfectly written, it knows
everything, and it ends by asking: she may say no and nothing will happen. That turns the
contradiction into the strangest of Elijah's five March anomalies.

**B2. "His year in the city."** Patricia asks "Who paid for your year in the city?" (Ch 31) and the
narration says "Everybody in that room knew where he had spent 2024" (Ch 34). On the page 2024
is one day and night in San Francisco, his mother's barn from April, three weeks in Stockton
and the co-op from October.
*Recommendation:* keep Patricia's line as her unfair version and let him not answer it. Change
the narration to what the room knows: where he spent that winter and who he met.

**B3. How the Ranger gets to the cannery lot.** In Ch 11 he is in Oakland at six, rides to
Stockton in Gus's U-Haul, and in Ch 12 sleeps in the Ranger at the cannery.
*Recommendation:* one sentence. He parks at the cannery the night before and rides to Oakland
with the crew at four. It also plants the lot he ends up sleeping in.

**B4. The last line.** "You're holding it." The proofs are printed before Thanksgiving, but the
book in the reader's hand contains Thanksgiving, the leaving of the copy and four Bidwell scenes
nobody in the story knows about.
*Recommendation:* leave it. It is a flourish and readers will take it as one. If it bothers you,
the fix is a half line: the copy on the bar is "the first one", not the last.

**B5. Cause too thin to carry the plot.**
- Why he is laid off: only "They called it a restructuring" (Ch 6).
- How a video on a 200-follower band page spreads (Ch 6): no mechanism. *Recommendation:* Linda's
  granddaughter already "sent it to" his mother in Ch 7. Let her be the one who posts it somewhere else.
- Why Nate looks away (Ch 14): Wes has a reason (the unanswered text). Nate sent a thumb and has
  none. See the Wes and Nate arc.

**B6. Left open at the end, with no line saying it is open on purpose.** Route money after
December. The county study. The welder for the gap. Elijah's pay (promised in Ch 16, passed "at
half" in Ch 26, never shown paid, no figure anywhere). The "responsible pacing" letter he signed
half read in Ch 8.
*Recommendation:* the route money and the gap can stay open, since the afterword is about
exactly that. The pay and the letter each need one line or a cut.
