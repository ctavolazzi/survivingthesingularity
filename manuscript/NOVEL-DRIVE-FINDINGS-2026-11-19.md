## 2. Where my earlier work conflicts with your decisions

I wrote the arcs and profiles from the PDF alone, before I had seen your plan. Your plan wins.
Two of my proposals are withdrawn and the files are corrected.

- **Bidwell.** I proposed that the machine name him to the co-op at Thanksgiving. Your plan
  decides the opposite: he is "pulling the strings in the background without the other
  characters ever knowing," and "he never really meets them." **Withdrawn.** His ending as
  written is your design: "The heroes do not beat him. His own machine stops serving him."

  proposed rewriting it. That speech is your own dictation of the scene on 14 November, nearly
  word for word. **Withdrawn.** What still holds: nothing earlier in the book prepares that
  sentence, so it wants planting once, earlier, in her mouth.

Three things in your plan that my files did not have and now should:

- **The four factions:** Naturalists, Accelerationists, We'll-Sees, Salt of the Earth. They are
  never named in the story text, and the Thanksgiving table splits along them. Section 4 gives
  each character a lean.
- **Elijah's arc in your words:** "from prophet to a man who can sit at a bar."
- **The core conflict in your words:** "the right to choose for yourself." My "one question"
  ("Did you ask anybody?") is the same thing seen from the other side.

## 4. The character pages: the blanks, proposed

In the form your pages use. "Leans toward" is yours where you had written one. Want, flaw and
arc are proposals built from what is on the page. Strike and rewrite freely.

### Denny Osei
- Leans toward: the Salt of the Earth (yours).
- Want: somewhere to stand that nobody can move. "Where do my daughter and I stand for those six
  months?"
- Flaw: he will not stop, and he will not be anybody's example again. So when the thing on the
  table is the thing that happened to him, he says nothing and turns his glass.
- Arc: from the lanyard to the porch. He was "the answer to that question" twice and it nearly
  killed him. The channel is the first thing that is his since the warehouse. On Thanksgiving he
  props a phone against the gravy boat and says, "Here's ours."

### Priya
- Leans toward: the We'll-Sees (yours).
- Want: evidence that holds. A result she can run again next year.
- Flaw: she will not say yes or no until the data is in, and a table sometimes needs one
  tonight. "Let's sit with it."
- Arc: from the pencil to her own handwriting. She warns that the co-op has put everything in
  one place. At Thanksgiving she is the one who reads the machine's message and says, "That's
  ours."

### Reuben Vance
- Leans toward: the We'll-Sees (yours).
- Want: to run the machine that took his grandfather's sixty acres backwards, on paper anybody
  can read.
- Flaw: he trusts paper more than people. He files before anyone asks, and what he cannot file
  he tables.
- Arc: from the folder to "debts." He takes one parcel out of the market for good. Then a letter
  arrives "better written than any lawyer I know." At Thanksgiving he says "forgive us our
  debts" and almost smiles.

### Curtis
- Leans toward: the Naturalists (yours).
- Want: never to be caught short.
- Flaw: he prepares against people. He counts where he could ask.
- Arc: from the fence to the rain fly. He moves further toward people than anyone in the book
  and not one inch toward the machine. "It's in the walls."

### Frank Kowalski
- Leans toward: the Salt of the Earth (yours).
- Want: to choose "instead of hoping," and to be the one who knows which part.
- Flaw: he knew and said nothing. He had time to laminate the sign.
- Arc: from the counter to the bell. He loses a store four generations old and becomes what
  every one of the eleven towns turns out to need. At Thanksgiving he says the prayer half a
  beat early for the boy he let go.

### Irene Calder
- Leans toward: the Salt of the Earth (yours).
- Want: to stay in her house, among people who were honest with Walt.
- Flaw: she keeps her accounts in favors and trusts nothing that is not written down.
- Arc: from the porch to the head of the table. She throws the letters away, asks one question

  her hands out.

### Devendra

- Want: first the moat. Then a place for his kids.
- Flaw: he uses calm to avoid looking. He reads three pages and says "interesting."
- Arc: from the sticky note to the squared fork. He loses the bet two years early, on his own

### Rosa
