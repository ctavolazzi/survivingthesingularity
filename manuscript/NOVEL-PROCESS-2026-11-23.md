## 1. The model: his Chapter 0 prayer rewrite

Before:

> David's father sat with a club soda and a lime, under a little framed prayer he'd hung behind the
> register the year he quit drinking. Elijah had sat across from that frame a couple thousand
> times. He had never read it.

After, his words:

> At the far end, on the last stool, David's father sat with his usual club soda and lime. Tucked
> away above him was a small framed prayer he'd printed out and hung behind the register the year
> he quit drinking.
>
> Elijah had sat across from that frame a couple thousand times. He had never actually read what
> was inside it.

His first draft also said "on the opposite end". The sentence already opens "At the far end, on the
last stool", so his call was "Drop opposite end".

What each change does:

1. "his usual" marks a habit, not a one-time thing.
2. "Tucked away above him" puts the frame in the room, relative to the person.
3. "printed out" is one texture detail. It makes the prayer homemade.
4. One sentence becomes two, and a paragraph break gives Elijah's line its own beat.
5. "never actually read what was inside it" makes the thing that matters clear.
6. No new events, and only a handful of new words.

## The rules behind the passes

### His clarity method

At each key moment, ask two sets of questions.

The journalist's six: who, what, when, where, why, how.

How it lands:

1. Where is it in the room?
2. Is it a habit or a one-time thing?
3. Is one sentence doing two jobs?
4. Is it easy to see, or easy to miss?
5. Is there one texture detail that says something about the person?
6. Is the thing that matters actually clear?

Answer by showing (objects, actions, placement), never by explaining a feeling. Add no new events.
Use few words, in his voice. Look hardest at first appearances, the first lines after a break,
details that pay off later, and the big emotional beats.

### Verbs

- His words: verbs "that also convey sound or weight or other physical properties of the story
  world": slammed, clanked, rattled, hefted, scraped, rapped.
- Vivid, specific verbs in his voice over stock ones like "put" and "looked".
- Emotionally charged clarity words: one word in narration makes the feeling plain without naming
  it. His example: "He hit it again with his fist" became "He pounded it again with his fist."
- Never name the feeling, never go purple, one charged word per beat, and don't swap back a verb an
  earlier pass chose.

every voice is its own, consistent, and of 2026. Tyler and Wren sound their age. Nothing folksy. Plot
and beats stay. Three readers have marked dated lines in Chapters 16 to 38 as a start; none of them
