# What ten readers found, 2026-11-19

Ten subagents on Sonnet 5.5 read the revised manuscript, each for one thing. Their full reports
are on the reading page under **Readers**. They are not in this repository because they quote
the book on nearly every line.

This is the digest: what held, what was fixed, and what is still open. Chapter numbers are the
book's. How the readers were briefed is in `NOVEL-METHOD-2026-11-19.md`.

---

## What held

- After the stroke his mother says nothing but "Barry." She never phones, and is never in her
  own kitchen again. No leftover from the version where she was well.
- He has no kin but his mother and Melody. His father is dead about ten years.
- The romance: nothing assumes he and Grace are a couple before the night she knocks, and
  nothing denies it after.
- Every date that falls on a real weekday is right (12 June 2026, 9 July 2027, 25 November
  2027), and the long counts agree: nineteen households, a hundred and forty on the route, six
  robot arms, fifty-three parcels, eleven notebooks, the $211.46 power bill, the eighteen days
  the models were dark.
- Every line that a later chapter quotes back matches the original word for word.
- The best-built chains, each planted early and paid late: the length of hose, the orange
  tape, the names page, the framed prayer, the Fisher stove, "Loser cooks," the three glasses.

## What was fixed

About thirty slips, all written into the Doc the same day. The list with reasons is `OPS3` in
`tools/consistency-pass/ops.py` and on the Changes tab. The ones that mattered:

- The sale money paid for her room the day he signed, though the sale closed days later.
- He left for the farm on a Sunday and arrived on a Tuesday.
- Manny "had offered the back room once," and no such scene existed.
- Three lines claimed "the first time" for things that had already happened.
- "He had been flown out before." Nobody ever flew him anywhere.
- A line added for Lou made him contradict himself. It was cut.
- Several things carried and never used were paid off: the pot, Devendra's wine.

One story change came out of the reports: the deed. See the continuation file.

## What is open

### 1. The middle is still the softest part

One reader went through chapters 24 to 31 scene by scene. Before the deed was added, these
scenes put nothing of Elijah's at risk and changed nothing for him: the second half of the
first-fire chapter, the Tyler and Frank scene at the bench, all five scenes of chapter 26, the
opening and close of chapter 27, Devendra's arrival, and the county hearing.

The deed puts a wire under chapters 25, 29 and 31. Chapters 26, 27 and 30 are untouched by it.

What the same reader noticed about those chapters:

- The pushback on Elijah is gentle every time: his weld is broken, his pay passes at half, his
  paid-plans idea loses a show of hands, his slides run out of time, his plan gets "Let's sit
  with it." He never pushes back.
- Outside threats are told, not shown. The land buyers appear once in these chapters, in a
  list. Claypot and the Memphis distributor hit Devendra and Frank, not the co-op. The storm
  and the county hearing are the only pressure on the page.
- Chapter 25 has no dates in it at all.

The cold reader's attention drifted in the same places: the opening of chapter 21, the opening
of chapter 26, the end of chapter 26, the first half of chapter 29, the first third of chapter
31, and part of chapter 35. It also drifted in chapter 4 and in the Afterword.

### 2. The same lesson, and the same mistake, many times

- "Ask first, you cannot choose for others" is taught to him by about eight people.
- He builds the polished thing alone and then presents it five times: the renders, the script,
  the dashboard, the county slides, the eight-hundred-dollar plan. Marta, Denny and Lou each
  name it, and he does it again.
- He pitches scale to a table four times (chapters 16, 27, 31, 34).
- A co-op member corrects him with a short story more than a dozen times.

The line added at the first fire, where he catches himself wanting to run the place, is meant
to make the later ones read as one want. Whether that is enough is a question for the
read-through. The cold reader called the pattern "that again."

### 3. Questions a first-time reader was left with

The cold reader logged every "wait, why?" and marked whether the book ever answered it. Never
answered:

- What did the second half of the letter say, the one he signed with his finger in San
  Francisco?
- Why does he say no to Gus? (Ruby now has "Grub." Gus has nothing.)
- How did Melody learn of the sale?
- How did Priya know the title of his manuscript?
- Why does he never tell the co-op who bought the farm? (This is now the deed, and the answer
  is that he does not, and it costs him.)
- Who sends the polite buy-out letters: the machine, or Bidwell's people?
- Why does Grace, who taught him "Ask. Don't guess," want the arms brought in over a no?
- Why do he and Grace never speak again?
- What becomes of Bidwell after "Hush"?
- What is wrong with Marta's hand?

Its five biggest unanswered questions about the story, in its order: the machine (its
candidacy, the letters, Bidwell); whether Elijah and Grace are finished; whether the co-op ever
learns who bought the farm; the letter and what becomes of Ruby, the Architect and Gus; Marta's
hand and who replaces her.

Where it could not stop reading: the toast, the bar scene with David, the Architect's kitchen,
the whole run from the parking lot through the crash out, node 7 in the storm, the morning
after the tea, the fire, the leaving and Thanksgiving, and the last chapter.

### 4. Planted and never paid

From the plants-and-payoffs reader, after the fixes:

- Melody's "Do I need to come home?" Only the blocked number answers it.
- The leaked journal and its three crawlers. Never traced. When the machine quotes the co-op
  at Thanksgiving, the book credits the files they gave away, not the leak.
- "WELDER FOR THE GAP: UNRESOLVED," and whether Tyler says yes to the apprenticeship.
- The Architect writing "Correct." in his notebook. Never recalled.
- "Gus would come," and Gus's number.
- David sitting on the customer side of the bar "exactly twice in fifteen years," and the lime
  knife. Neither returns at the reunion.
- The letter signed with his finger.
- The sticky note with the bet, after chapter 6.
- Reuben's fix for the trust documents "by March."
- Patricia's "Who paid for your year in the city?" Nobody answers her.

### 5. Used so often it may stop working

- "Barry?" Nine times and once from the lamp. Four of them close a Sunday phone call.
- Metal ticking as it cools: fifteen.
- Grace's hand on a forearm: twelve.
- "We'll see": nine. "College": thirteen before Marta finally says his name.
- "You look thin" and "Are you eating": about sixteen.
- The 9:40 call: ten.

Some of these are the book's music and should stay. The count is here so the choice is made on
purpose.

### 6. Clocks that are set and never run out

The welder for the gap. The county study's due date, which is written on a folder and never
printed. Reuben's "by March." The route money, which runs out in December and is still open on
the last page.

### 7. The supporting cast against CT's own character pages

- His pages never mention Lou, Wren, Gus, the Architect, or Marta's cousin Emeka. All are on
  the page, and Lou's "You can't change anyone's mind" is what sets off the break with Grace.
- At the Thanksgiving table the book sorts people into four groups by what they do, never by
  name: Tyler and the two newest households leaning in; Wren, Patricia and Curtis drawing
  together; Marta, Priya and Reuben with their arms folded; and Rosa, Denny, Frank, Irene and
  Lou, who keep the food moving. Devendra is not placed.
- Rosa is the opposite of the plan. His pages lean her toward the group that is against the
  machine. On the page she is the one who says "Pass the lamb."
- Bidwell does learn the co-op exists, which the plan says he never does. He never gets the
  rest of what the plan gave him: no military edge, no face on a television, no ending after
  "Hush."
- People present and silent when their own history is the subject: Marta at the Rod Code
  fight and at the leak, Lou at Irene's table (he now stands by the door with his hat), Denny
  at the arms meeting, Tyler when the no-AI pledge is read out.
- Missing for long stretches: Rosa for about a fifth of the book; Marta in chapters 25, 26, 35
  and 36; Priya in chapters 23 to 25.
- Raised and never answered for the cast: Denny's daughter is never seen or named; the mother
  of Devendra's children is never mentioned; Devendra's "I don't want to take anything before
  I've..." is never finished; Curtis's basement and his fear have no history; the question
  Elijah wanted to ask Lou as he left is never said.
