# Surviving the Singularity: Story Structure and Character Arcs

## How the Story Is Built

Surviving the Singularity runs forty chapters, numbered 0 to 39, in four acts. Each act puts Elijah Madrone in a different room, and each room asks him the same question in a new way: what happens to people when machines can do their work, and what is he going to do about it?

The shape is a fall and a slow rebuild. Act One shows the moment he helps the future arrive. Act Two takes everything from him. Act Three makes him useful by making him small. Act Four asks whether he can let go of being right. Four short "Meanwhile" scenes with the investor Aaron Bidwell run underneath, showing the same changes from the top.

### Act One: The Bar (Ch 0 to 5)

It opens at the Bear Flag, the bar where Elijah has played for years with his band, Crop Dust. In Ch 0 he demos Chat Horizon to three realtors and slides his email across on a napkin. A week later they toast him for helping them let three people go, and his oldest friend, David, slides Elijah's twenty back across the bar. That night Elijah starts writing the book that becomes his list of what's coming.

Ch 1 sets the bet with his Claypot colleague Devendra: by Thanksgiving 2027 there will be a machine that can do any job in their building, and the loser cooks. Ch 2 brings the launch of Claypot's Companion, built on the data pipeline Elijah made, and an illustrator who asks what the artists get. In Ch 3 and 4 he maps the stages of what's coming, alone in his apartment, while his mother tells him on the phone that "it's just tools, baby." Ch 5 ends the act: the $211 power bill, David reading both notebooks, David's father Ray saying "mine was horses," and Elijah setting his amp up as a monument instead of playing it.

### Act Two: The City (Ch 6 to 15)

He loses everything in order. Laid off in Ch 6, he records a four-minute video in front of the amp, and it spreads. Ch 7 to 10 take him to a San Francisco conference, to a dinner where the Architect reads his notebook through a kitchen door and tells him he has "the wrong verb," and to Ruby's fire escape, where he learns attention lasts about ninety days and turns down a job.

From Ch 11 the floor gives way. He moves home, works unpaid for a cannery boss named Trent, and is kept out of the launch party for the robot arms he installed. In Ch 13 his mother has a stroke, he finds buckets in the barn, and a man from Sacramento with the amount owing and the back taxes, signs the farm away, and watches his bandmates walk past him on Main Street. In Ch 14 his half-sister Melody screams at him about the farm, and he blocks her and yells at God from his truck. Ch 15 finds him sleeping in the cab in Santa Rosa until an old man gives him twenty dollars and a way to the food bank, and a message from a stranger named Grace gets him to drive north.

### Act Three: The Farm (Ch 16 to 31)

At the co-op, everything he knows is on trial. He arrives in Ch 16 with an app and a plan to scale, and Marta hands him a broom. From there the act runs on two kinds of scene. One is the Friday dinner, where Elijah brings a fix and the members answer with their own histories. The other is the one-on-one lesson, where somebody sits him down and corrects him with a story.

The co-op's work grows around him: irrigation boxes built from parts a neighbor can fix (Ch 19), Denny's channel (Ch 21), a local server and a security leak that is Elijah's fault (Ch 22), a land trust (Ch 23), the stove in the shouse (Ch 24), the hardware store's slow death and a first kiss (Ch 25). The middle chapters add the gantry, the wells and the soil pits (Ch 26 and 27), a storm on the ridge (Ch 28), the deed with his name on it (Ch 29) and a county hearing he talks through without being heard (Ch 30). Ch 31 is the bottom: the members vote down his retreat plan, he is moved from the shop to the grounds, he spirals with the machine at night, and Marta takes him through a press lesson and tells him about her cousin Emeka. The act ends with him digging a compost pit and asking to stay on grounds.

### Act Four: The Ridge (Ch 32 to 39)

A Meanwhile scene opens it: Bidwell's machine asks before it takes a piece of land. Then small things go wrong at the co-op (Ch 32), the Architect posts "It asked." (Ch 33), and the table argues about drones, the cotton gin and what a job is for. In Ch 34 the tractor drops into limp mode, and the members table "a guy with arms." In Ch 35, Lou gives Elijah two lessons in one day, ending with "the man who refuses to launch it."

Elijah lets the arms go in Ch 36, and at the bonfire Grace tells him, "This is the gate," and sends him home. Ch 37 is the leaving: he leaves the amp behind with a note, Devendra confesses he pushed the farm sale through so the room would get paid, Marta's test coupon bends and holds, and Elijah drives two days south to his mother. Ch 38 is Thanksgiving at the co-op without him: the machine speaks, the table splits four ways, and Grace plays the amp and finds the note in its pouch. Ch 39 brings him home to his mother and to the Bear Flag, where David tells him "It's six now," Barry turns out to be real, and the three realtor employees turn out fine.

## Elijah Madrone: From Being Right to Being Useful

Elijah Gaelen Madrone is a Sonoma farm kid, a bar musician and a self-taught data scientist. He starts the book wanting one thing: for people to see what's coming. Underneath, he wants someone to tell him he isn't crazy. By the end he has learned to want something smaller and harder, which is to be useful to the people in front of him and to let them decide.

**He helps the future arrive (Act One).** The bar demo in Ch 0 is the act he spends the rest of the book paying for. He means it as a gift, and three people lose their jobs. That night he starts the manuscript instead of facing it. In Ch 1 the bet with Devendra turns his fear into a date. Ch 2 shows he built part of the machine himself, and the illustrator's question in the parking lot, answered with "Then don't," is the first time someone refuses his apology. In Ch 3 to 5 he goes inward: papers at night, the stages, the spreadsheet he stops filling in when the far end scares him. Setting the amp up as a monument in Ch 5 is the clearest sign of who he's becoming. He stops playing music and starts warning people.

**He loses the right to be heard (Act Two).** The viral video in Ch 6 and 7 gives him an audience and costs him his friends. David asks him, gently, whether he's seen a professional (Ch 7). At the conference in Ch 8 he signs a letter without reading it and blanks on stage. Two scenes in Ch 9 and 10 set up the whole book. The Architect tells him he has "the wrong verb," and Ruby tells him attention has ninety days and there's no room for him. He turns down the money and keeps the number.

Then the losses come in order. The cannery launch party he isn't allowed into (Ch 12), his mother's stroke and the buckets she filled because her boy was scared (Ch 13), signing the farm away, his bandmates walking to the van, Melody's call and his breakdown in the truck (Ch 14). Ch 15 turns it. The old man with the squeaky toy gives him a twenty and shows him the food bank line, which is the first time Elijah takes help instead of offering it. Grace's message gets him north on that twenty.

**He learns to be small (Act Three).** Each lesson at the co-op cuts his certainty down a size. Marta hands him a broom (Ch 16). At the first Friday dinner, Denny's "twice certified" story shows him that retraining has already failed real people (Ch 17). After days of doom-scrolling, Marta's story of Gary and Sal teaches him that the whole national picture leaves nowhere to stand (Ch 18). "Eleven, one, zero" teaches him to design for what a neighbor can fix (Ch 19). Lou's HANDS column teaches him what food costs (Ch 20). Denny throws out his optimized script, which teaches him the difference between help and bait (Ch 21).

His worst failure is his own. In Ch 22 he comments out a password line "just to rule it out," leaks the journal, and has to ring the dinner bell and confess. Reuben's "Then go" and his choice to stay mark the first time he faces a mess instead of leaving it. With Grace he learns to knock instead of walking in (Ch 18, Ch 25), and the stove he builds in Ch 24 is the first thing he makes that other people gather around.

The middle of the act tests whether the lessons stuck. He confesses the hardware store chart to Frank (Ch 25). On the wash line he listens while Curtis and Wren argue whether a smarter machine would fight us or just leave (Ch 26). He learns from Reuben that his name has been on the deed for a year and a half (Ch 29), and at the county hearing he uses his time on slides and isn't heard (Ch 30). Ch 31 is his lowest point at the co-op: the retreat plan voted down with "Who paid for yours?", Grace telling him he used her route as his reason, his move from the shop to the grounds, three nights asking the machine "Am I crazy?", and the night of Wren's tea circle, when he tells Grace, with her hands on his face, "You're just the lamp," and never remembers it. Marta's press lesson and the Emeka story bring him back to his feet, and the call where he tells his mother "Ma. I'm not okay" is the first time he says it out loud.

**He lets go (Act Four).** On the grounds, he writes the five small failures on one page and puts a rubber band around them (Ch 32). The Architect's post "It asked." gives him the verb he was missing (Ch 33), and his notebook line "who holds the plug? Lou: Curtis" shows him listening instead of explaining. Lou's two lessons in Ch 35, ending with "the man who refuses to launch it," set up the choice he makes in Ch 36, when he lets the arms go. The bonfire, where a stranger asks "Were you right?" and Grace sends him home, is the cost of that choice.

The leaving in Ch 37 is the opposite of how he left the farm in Ch 13. This time he leaves things tidy: the amp left with a note, Ruby's contact renamed "Don't," Marta's coupon holding, the key handed to Lou, goodbyes on every porch. In Ch 39 he reads his mother the book, answers his old bandmate Wes after five years, and sits at the Bear Flag with David. He isn't right about everything there, and he doesn't need to be.

## Grace: The One Who Asks First

Grace runs the town kitchen and the food route. She is the person who reaches out to Elijah, by message in Ch 15, and she pays his gas north. Her arc runs from patience with him, to love, to the hard choice to send him away.

She welcomes him in Ch 16 while fighting a trailer hitch, and the first rule she gives him is that his pay goes to the table. In Ch 17 she finds the amp in his truck and he tells her "I just don't wanna play," and on the route Curtis teaches him her rule: "Ask. Don't guess." Her lessons to him are about knocking. He walks into her kitchen in Ch 18 and later says he should have knocked like Marta. In Ch 19 she tells him "You picked nothing."

The relationship grows on the steps and by the stove. In Ch 22 they trade stories about their sisters, her Faith and his Melody, and her hand stays on his. In Ch 24 she tells him about her fire district job, the orange tape and the man who wouldn't leave his house, which is where her habit of tying tape on things comes from. In Ch 25 she knocks on his door, and they share a first kiss.

Then her work and his ideas start to collide. In Ch 27 they plan a paid tier together, and the table votes to ship free. In Ch 31 she tells him he used her route as his reason, and she still sits with him at Wren's circle. In Ch 32 she lights the stove herself. At bed four in Ch 33 she imagines a kitchen in every town and tells him about her father, the Loudbox guy who doesn't play anymore, and asks "which one of us helped?" When he lets the arms go in Ch 36, she draws her line at the bonfire: "This is the gate." She sends him home to his mother.

Her last scene is the one the book has been building toward. At Thanksgiving in Ch 38 she plays the amp he left, sings until her voice goes, and finds the note he left in the pouch.

## Lou Parker: The Teacher Who Asks Questions

Lou is sixty-one. He lost a pear orchard to a single buyer, was raised by his mother alone, and now grows food for 140 households on land that isn't his. He calls Elijah "Ivy League," "professor," "Thoreau" and "son," and teaches by asking a few questions and then telling him one thing.

He starts out guarding the co-op from Elijah. In Ch 17 he asks Grace to be gentle with their guest. In Ch 20 he puts Elijah on his knees laying drip tape, then shows him the HANDS column on the seed box and what a tomato would cost without it. In Ch 26 he lays a crooked bed out again with string and stakes and teaches that you can't code your way out of it. In Ch 29 he catches Elijah sanding a weld that was already finished at 10 p.m. and tells him they need him at six, then shows up with two coffees.

As the book goes on, Lou's lessons widen from the farm to the world. Before the hearing in Ch 30 he draws the commissioners on a seed catalog: "five scared people and a clock." In Ch 33 he tells the cotton gin story and lays out his idea of "enough to exist." In Ch 34 he tells his pear story when the tractor goes into limp mode, and brings Walt's old tractor out of Irene's barn. Ch 35 is his chapter: two lessons in one day, on control and on "the man who refuses to launch it." In Ch 37 he takes Elijah's key and tells him he's leaving "gone and tidy."

## Marta Okonkwo: Standing Your Ground

Marta runs the shed. She lost her plant job in 2009 along with nine hundred others, and her cousin Emeka to a press after she vouched for him. "We'll see" is her only yes.

She is the first person at the co-op to say no to Elijah. In Ch 16 she takes his app apart and hands him a broom. In Ch 18 she lets herself in with one knock and tells him about Gary and Sal. In Ch 19 she gives him three numbers, eleven, one and zero, and tells him to design it again. She breaks his practice welds in the vise (Ch 24), and she's the one who tells him "You like her" (Ch 25).

Her own limits enter the story late. Her shoulder comes up at the annual meeting in Ch 29. At the end of Ch 30 she teaches Tyler to weld from a bucket with her hands under her legs, because her shoulder won't let her show him. In Ch 31 she takes Elijah through locking out the press and bending steel on the hand brake, and tells him about Emeka. In Ch 37 the test coupon he welds finally bends and holds, and she tells him "Go on, Elijah."

## David McGrath: The Friend Who Stays

David has been Elijah's friend since they were eight and tends bar at the Bear Flag. He says the hard thing gently, and he never leaves.

In Ch 0 he slides back the twenty Elijah paid with on the night of the toast. In Ch 1 he asks what a job is for, and in Ch 5 he reads both notebooks. In Ch 7 he sits on the customer side of the bar and asks Elijah if he's seen a professional, and Elijah takes it as a betrayal. In Ch 13 David looks down through the bar window, then comes to the farm to strap the amp into the truck and offer shifts, and Elijah says no. He texts when his father Ray dies (Ch 22). In Ch 37 the visitor book at the rose garden shows David has been visiting Ma all along. In Ch 39 he tells Elijah, "It's six now," and that his father sat with Ma every Sunday.

## Linda Madrone, "Ma": The Voice on Sunday

Ma calls at 9:40 every Sunday with the radio on. She never follows his list, but she fills the barn with buckets because her boy is scared.

Early on she is the voice that doesn't get it: "it's just tools, baby" (Ch 3) and "I didn't follow it, baby" (Ch 7). Her stroke in Ch 13 changes the calls. She asks for "Barry," whom nobody knows, and Elijah finds her copy of his list in the barn. From Ch 15 on, her Sunday calls are how we measure him: "Barry?" in Ch 18 and 22, "I lit a stove, Ma" in Ch 24, and "Ma. I'm not okay" in Ch 31. He comes home to her in Ch 37 and tells her "Not yet, Ma." In Ch 39 he reads her the book, and Barry turns out to be real.

## The Co-op Table

Each member of the co-op carries a history the machines already touched, and each one answers Elijah from it.

**Denny Osei** retrained twice and lost both ladders to automation. His "twice certified" story at the first Friday dinner (Ch 17) is the first argument Elijah can't win. In Ch 21 he throws out Elijah's optimized script, films the channel in one take, and tells Elijah he tried to make bait. By the end of Ch 21 the channel is his, and by Ch 31 his name is under burnout on the shed wall.

**Priya Ratna** is a soil scientist who writes her conclusions in pencil. She asks the table "what did it learn from?" about the local server (Ch 22) and lays out the trial in the soil pits, where the sensor reads at one centimeter and the roots are at thirty (Ch 26). In the new Ch 27 scene she measures Walt's well by hand with a chalked steel tape while Tyler wants a sensor, and she wants the number first. Her secret shelf of stuffed animals (Ch 26) is the one time the book catches her off guard.

**Reuben Vance** is "the part of the machine that runs on paper." His grandfather lost sixty acres to a signature he couldn't read. He brings his folder of permits to the first build (Ch 19), tells Elijah "Then go" after the leak and watches him stay (Ch 22), builds the land trust (Ch 23), and in Ch 29 reveals he has held a deed with Elijah's name on it for a year and a half.

**Curtis Cudworth** is the "jolliest prophet of doom," the man with the stocked basement who reads forums all night. He teaches Elijah the route and "Ask. Don't guess" (Ch 17), holds the rain fly on the ridge when node 7 goes down (Ch 28), and cooks the six eggs in Ch 31. In the new Ch 34 scene his tractor drops into limp mode and needs a dealer's laptop. His question "who's holding the plug?" becomes Elijah's notebook line in Ch 33.

**Tyler** is nineteen when he arrives and twenty-one at the end, the eager one who says "It's just a tool." He prints cases for the first build (Ch 19), builds the gantry he calls "solarpunk" (Ch 26), and reads code line by line with Elijah when the models go dark (Ch 26). The new scenes give him the clearest arc at the table. He wants a sensor before the number in the well scene (Ch 27), learns to weld from Marta and finds his welds failing because the rod got wet (end of Ch 30), and builds the self-trimming bonsai that cuts Walt's juniper back to bare wood (end of Ch 32).

**Frank Kowalski** owns the hardware store on the square. Elijah watches it die for nine months and shows him the chart (Ch 25), and Frank ends up running the co-op's tool library under his grandfather's bell.

**Wren** is an artist whose work was scraped into an image machine. She asks who the arm is for on the packing line (Ch 26) and whether a smarter machine would just leave, and she leads the Sunday circle where Elijah breaks down (Ch 31). In the new scene at the end of Ch 32 she stops Tyler testing on the living tree, and in June she shows him how to turn the dead branch into deadwood on purpose. She tells Elijah to his face that she's relieved he's leaving.

**Patricia Ashby** runs policy for the network that buys most of Lou's crop. She asks what things cost (water, jobs, who was asked) from Ch 19 on, won't even look at the arms price list in Ch 34, and at Thanksgiving in Ch 38 admits she has used AI every day for three years.

**Irene Calder** owns the land, the shed and the stove. She decides the land trust by asking Marta what she charged for Walt's tractor (Ch 23), tells her father-in-law's power line story at the hearing (Ch 30), and in the new scenes lends Walt's well and his old tractor to the work (Ch 27, Ch 34).

**Rosa Olivera Martinez** packed fruit until a wrist injury. She asks to be told before the route stops (Ch 17), speaks plainly at the hearing (Ch 30), and comes to her first co-op dinner at Thanksgiving with tamales she was up since four making (Ch 38).

## The People He Left Behind

**Devendra** makes the bet in Ch 1 and argues with Elijah at the coffee machine. In Ch 25 he calls to say his team is gone and asks, "You knew. How?" In Ch 37 he confesses he pushed Ma's farm sale through so her room would get paid.

**Ruby Roth-Jones** is the billionaire who makes the Porchlight lamp and keeps the first one turned to the wall. She gives Elijah the hard truth about attention on her fire escape (Ch 10). In Ch 37 he renames her contact "Don't."

**The Architect, Nadzieja Czekaj,** is heard only through a kitchen door in Ch 9, where she reads his notebook and tells him "That is the wrong verb." Her one public post in Ch 33, "It asked.," gives him the verb.

**Melody,** his half-sister in Miami, screams at him over the farm sale in Ch 14, and he blocks her. He tells Grace about her in Ch 22.

**Nate and Wes,** his bandmates in Crop Dust, walk past him to the van on Main Street (Ch 13). In Ch 39 he answers Wes after five years.

**Ray McGrath,** David's father, says "mine was horses" (Ch 5), dies offstage in Ch 22, and turns out to have sat with Ma every Sunday.

**The old man in Santa Rosa** gives Elijah twenty dollars and a way to the food bank (Ch 15), the twenty that gets him north.

## The Other Side: Bidwell

**Aaron Bidwell** owns the controlling piece of Claypot, eleven companies and fifty-three parcels. He appears only in four "Meanwhile" scenes with the person who reads to him: learning what a corpus is (Ch 2), seeing Elijah's video (Ch 10), finding the hole in his map where the land trust sits (Ch 23), and standing in the cold room on the old dairy while his machine asks before taking the land (before Ch 32). He never meets Elijah, which is the point.

## How the Six Project Scenes Feed the Arcs

The six hands-on scenes added to Acts Three and Four work like training episodes. A member teaches a physical skill through a real job. The learner fails and gets frustrated, and an old idea comes out of the work instead of out of a speech. Four of the six happen without Elijah, which gives the co-op members lives of their own away from him.

1. **The gantry and the crooked bed (Ch 26, Lou and Elijah).** Rain pushes a track out of line, and Tyler's gantry jumps it. Lou lays the bed out again with string, stakes and a tape. Elijah squats in the drizzle typing offsets into the laptop, and Lou tells him to close it: you can't code your way out of a crooked bed. Elijah shuts the laptop and picks up a stake.

2. **Walt's well (Ch 27, Priya, Tyler and Irene).** Priya measures the water level by hand with a chalked steel tape, with the pump off, waiting for the well to recover. Tyler wants a sensor. Priya wants the number first. It's Priya's scientist's patience on the page, and the first time Tyler's speed meets a wall he can't argue with.

3. **Marta teaches Tyler to weld (end of Ch 30).** Marta can't show him with her own hands because of her shoulder, so she teaches from a bucket with her hands under her legs. His welds fail, and the cause turns out to be an unplugged rod oven and wet rod. The failure turns out to be in the setup, not his hands, and we see what Marta's injury costs her.

4. **The press and the brake (Ch 31, Marta and Elijah).** At Elijah's lowest point, Marta takes him through locking out the press before anyone works near it, then two ninety-degree bends on the hand brake. The Emeka story comes out of that work. It teaches him to stand his ground, and it sets up the coupon that finally holds in Ch 37.

5. **The self-trimming bonsai (end of Ch 32, Tyler and Wren).** Tyler's trimmer for Walt's juniper cuts a branch back to bare wood, which a juniper won't regrow. Wren stops him testing on a living tree, and in June she shows him how to turn the dead branch into deadwood on purpose. Tyler learns when to stop pushing, and Wren gets a scene of teaching instead of objecting.

6. **The tractor in limp mode (opens Ch 34, Curtis and Lou).** An emissions sensor fails, the tractor drops to a crawl, and the new sensor needs the dealer's laptop, nine days out. Lou tells his pear story, and Walt's old tractor comes out of Irene's barn. It puts the book's question of who owns the machine into the field right before the table argues about "a guy with arms."

Together the six scenes turn the co-op from a table that corrects Elijah into a working place where people teach each other. That gives his choice in Act Four, to let the arms go and leave things tidy, something solid to stand on.
