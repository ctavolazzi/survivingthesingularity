## Read this first: four things I ran into

1. **Three of her lines cannot be said anymore.** "It looks like somewhere" (24), "Then you're
   somewhere" (31) and "It reads better than it sounded" (39). She has one word now. I did not
   hand them to anybody else. That also ends "Where's the Fisher?" "Somewhere." in 37, which
   was the answer to the first two. It is the real cost of the stroke, and I think you should
   see it before you take the rest.
2. **The proofs.** Your text says they come back "the Tuesday before Thanksgiving." The earlier
   note had him read the book to her "over two Sundays." There are no Sundays between that
   Tuesday and Thursday. Here he reads it Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday morning.
3. **David and Sundays.** If David sits with her on Sundays and Elijah has been back three
   weeks, they would have met in her room before the bar. So Elijah finds out from the
   visitors' book at the front desk the day he comes home, and stays away from the bar until
   Thanksgiving. He walks in already knowing. David tells him anyway.
4. **"Eight months."** By the book's own count Ray sat with her about seven: the stroke is in
   October, and chapter 22 puts Ray's death "seven months" after the morning with the hand
   truck. The line below still says eight, because that is the version you saw. It is one word
   to change.

**Why the lamp calls him Barry.** In your text it says "You must be Elijah. She's told me about
you." She cannot tell it about anyone now. It has listened to her for months and it has one
name. It is being kind with what it has, which is the thing the Architect is afraid of in
chapter 9. He does not correct it, and he has just stopped correcting her. If that is too much,
the plain version is one line: "Welcome back," the lamp said. "You must be Elijah."
