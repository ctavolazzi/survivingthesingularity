## 1. The short version

1. **The book works where a person pays for something in front of us**, and it has many of those: the toast, the
   buckets in the barn, the old man's twenty, the broom, Denny's ladder, the soup jars, the cooked trays, the pot
   carried back inside, the missed funeral hour, the stove, the pond, the fire, the tape on the gate, "It became the
   steel," the note in the amp, "No, man. You're good."
2. **It goes flat where the largest things are summarized.** Two of the biggest were told in a sentence: he confesses
   the board (Ch 22; since played, edit 201) and a year passes between two people who want each other (Ch 26). (A third
   claim here, that the sale of the farm in Ch 13 is summary, was a reader's and was wrong; Claude read the pages
   later the same day. The sale is a full scene in CT's words.)
3. **The middle now has a chain of losses, and three links are weak:** Elijah never stands in front of the Ochoas; the
   year of waiting has no obstacle on his side; and several costs are refunded inside the chapter that charges them.
4. **Continuity is close.** About thirty errors were fixed on 26 November. Seven remain in chapters CT called done,
   and each needs his yes (section 6).
5. **Two threads are not paid:** Bidwell (four "Meanwhile" scenes and nothing after) and Praether (one scene so far).
6. **Structure:** five chapters are each several chapters long (13, 15, 17, 26, 33); two are short essays with no talk
   (4 and 12); the same three kinds of scene repeat (section 7).
7. **Smell and women-only scenes**, both asked for by CT on 26 November, are nearly absent: 13 real smells, and no
   scene with only women in it.
8. **Length:** 95,675 words. Everything CT has added this week costs words, and the plan to pay for it is in section 8.

## 4. The three weak links in the rewritten chapters (20 to 26)

1. **Elijah never faces the Ochoas.** Lou walks the benches with Lupe. Grace takes them the news. Elijah watches a
   truck from the shed. He redoes forty boards and never carries one to their door. One short scene would do it: he
   takes the new board up himself, and Lupe makes him sign the clipboard.
2. **The year has no obstacle on his side.** She kisses him and says "I really hope you stay," and he does nothing for
   eleven months. The material is on the page and unused: Chapter 23's "don't date where you eat," and Chapter 26's pay
   vote, where Curtis "asked it looking at me." A man who won't take a handout will not be the one man at the table who
   is paid and also with the woman who brought the vote. Said once, in his head or to Marta, that is the year.
3. **The truck stop** (CT's scene). Curtis says "Light comes back on, you call me" and "Hose is in the glove box." Two
   lines later the light comes on and he does neither, then drives the same hundred miles back on the same light. One
   line fixes it: ahead is seven hundred miles with nobody; behind is a hundred miles and a man who will fix it for
   nothing. That is also CT's point ("the best bet he has ... is on the farm with these people").

Also from the reader, smaller: the land trust's nine-week clock comes from sellers who said yes, and Irene said no (the
pressure is really Dale); the down payment has no figure; "there was just something so alluring about Grace" reads
told (**his line**); Lou's "Does she?" is hard for the man who guarded those Sunday calls (**his line**, and the plan
wants Lou wrong once).

- **Five chapters are several chapters each:** 13 (4,559 words, nine scenes), 15 (5,637), 17 (5,040), 26 (4,361, eight
  scenes over eighteen months) and 33 (4,255, 58% of it in quotes). The love story's payoff sits at the tail of a soil

- **Two chapters are essays:** 4 (761 words, no dialogue) and 12 (988 words, 4%). A reader would fold 4 into 3 and 12
  into 11.
- **CT's rule is that dialogue carries it.** Chapters under 12% in quotes: 0, 4, 5, 12, 13, 28.
- **Three kinds of scene repeat:**
  - In Act Two a stranger explains the world to a silent Elijah six times running (the cooling woman, the panelist,
    the Architect, Ruby, Gus, the old man), and for four chapters (8, 10, 11, 12) he wants nothing and only refuses
    (reader).
  - In Act Three an elder corrects him with an object seven times; the text notices ("First the vise, and now the
    shovel.").
  - The Lou, Wren and Curtis argument about machines runs four times (Ch 20, 26, 33, 34). The plan already cuts the
    one in 33 by a third; the one in 20 is the essay stretch a reader marked flat.
- **Endings:** Chapters 0 to 4 all end at the notebook or the desk, and six scenes in 16 to 26 close on a notebook
  line (reader).
- **He fails to go south three times** (July, Thanksgiving, June). The third costs nothing as written.
- **"Huh"** is said by five different people in Chapters 17 to 26.
