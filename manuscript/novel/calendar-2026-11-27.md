The dates are Claude's reading; the time words beside them are pulled from the page by the script so each date can be
checked at a glance. A check's lines are found in the book by the script, and a line that is gone stops the run. Sixteen
of the checks are real calendar arithmetic (a weekday or a count of days or months). The rest are reasoned from the
lines quoted and say so.

## What holds (23 checks)

The book's dates are sound. Every weekday that can be tested against a real calendar comes out right:
Thursday 25 November 2027; Friday 12 June 2026 and its eighteen days; Monday 1 March 2027, "the first of the month",
the morning after a Sunday call; Tuesday 28 December 2027, the last route box, on a route day; Saturday 27 September
2025, the first fire; Friday 9 July 2027, when the Architect posts and Denny asks "Is Friday still Friday?"; Friday 20
November 2026, the Riemann dinner; the lien sale, eleven days after a Wednesday, falling on the Sunday Elijah names.
The long counts hold too: eleven months from the toast to the layoff, fifteen months back to the first page, sixteen
months of uncashed checks, two and a half years since he blocked Melody, a year and a half that Reuben held the deed.

## Elijah's age is not a contradiction. It sets his birthday.

Thirty-five in January 2024 (Ch 7), thirty-six in the first days of March 2025 (Ch 15, twice), thirty-seven that May
(Ch 20). All three are true if his birthday falls **between about 3 March and the middle of May**. The audit and the
checkpoints carried this as a number that "may not hold". It holds. For the birthday plan: he arrives on Tuesday 4
March 2025 at thirty-six and is thirty-seven by the drip tape in May, so the birthday that "comes and goes" is in his
first ten weeks on the farm, and the party a year later is in spring 2026. Tyler's ages (nineteen, twenty, twenty-one)
hold the same way, with a birthday between mid November and January.

## What does not hold (3, all known before today)

1. **"They've been there since my first week"** (Ch 34). He arrives Tuesday 4 March 2025; the arms come the Wednesday
   after the Sunday lien sale, 12 March, his second week. Smallest fix: "since my second week", or "since the month I
   got here".
2. **The squash** (Ch 34). In the tractor scene it "has to come in this week"; at the mid October meeting, at least
   nine days later, and in festival week, it is still in the field. Smallest fix: loosen "this week" and the nine
   days in the tractor scene.
3. **"The last one ran out in June"** (Ch 27, October 2026). The route is running and Grace has just said it has no
   money "past June". Smallest fix: "runs out in June".

## Two to read (new)

4. **The board is "ours" in April** (Ch 19 against Ch 22). The order comes "earlier that April", the first design eats
   "the better part of a month", and Marta and Tyler both say April for the finished board. It holds only if the
   order came in the first days of April. Tight, not broken. Smallest fix if he wants slack: "Earlier that spring".
5. **Route boxes on a Wednesday night** (Ch 36). The route runs on Tuesdays (Ch 16, 34, 37), and Elijah and Grace
   build a hundred and forty route boxes "on the Wednesday of festival week". Low. A half line would cover it ("for
   Tuesday", or the route moved for the festival).

## Scenes told out of order, on purpose

Eight scenes step back in time and the page says so each time (for example "Two days before that, on the Wednesday",
"Earlier that April", "The servers had come at the end of May"). They are marked "on purpose" in the sheet. No scene
steps back without the page saying so.
