## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

**Portrait.** "A man with a gray ponytail." He "was good with engines, and he owned the valley's
best-stocked basement." At Elijah's first dinner he is reading aloud from his phone about fires
in the city, and arguing for a fence.

On the route he keeps a folded sheet in his pocket and "kept taking it out at red lights even
though Elijah offered to hold it." When the kitchen gets Rosa's order wrong he says "That's
ours. The mistake. We'll call," and phones Grace "on speaker, because he didn't trust himself to
pass it along right." He writes Rosa's answer on the sheet "where she could see him write it."
"He didn't ask her to join anything."

Elijah's truck has had its check-engine light on since the day he arrived. The dealer wants
twelve hundred dollars and a week. "Curtis found it in nine minutes," with "a fourteen-dollar
gadget" and "two dollars of hose. He did the repair while the coffee brewed, and left the rest
of the hose on the dashboard."

On the ridge in the storm he holds a garbage bag over Elijah for a rain fly. "His arms had to be
dead." The morning after the worst night of Elijah's life he "cooked six eggs on top of the
Fisher, salted them, put the pan on the floor next to Elijah with a fork in it, and left."
"Eggs."

When the news comes he wipes his mouth, puts his napkin on his chair and walks out. "He was going
home to count his basement." He knows the Lord's Prayer "cold."

**Reads as.** He prepares against people and then spends what he has hoarded on whoever is in
front of him. Elijah's notebook has it: "Curtis: wants a fence, but shows up for every work day.
The fence is fear."

**Voice.** "That's who you plan for. That's who you fence out." "Doesn't change your dinner."
"It's got a camera on it, Lou. Today it's looking at lettuce." "I wanted a fence for people like
you. Now I'm holding your rain fly." "A rule's just greed somebody wrote down." "Dinner's
dinner." "Keep it." "It's in the walls. He put it in the walls."

**His plate.** The basement, which the book never once goes into.

**Blank.** Surname, age, family, whatever he did for a living. What is down there.

**Suggestions.**
1. Never show the basement. Everything he hands anybody (the gadget, the hose, the cast-iron
   pan, the garbage bag) came up out of the store he keeps against them. Let the reader work
   that out.
2. A man who thinks the world is ending knows the Lord's Prayer cold. That is one line of past
   and it is enough.
3. He would not let Elijah hold the route sheet. On the last morning he says "Keep it" about two
   dollars of hose. Those are the two ends of him, and they are both about what he lets go of.
