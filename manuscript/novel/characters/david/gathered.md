## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

*Added 19 November: after Ray dies, David is the one who sits with Elijah's mother on Sundays. He is angry about it and does it anyway. In chapter 39 he is the one who tells Elijah who Barry was.*

**Portrait.** He rode the same school bus as Elijah for eleven years and has poured his beer for
fifteen. He stopped asking what Elijah wanted a long time ago. "Elijah would sit down and it
would already be there."

He keeps a pair of drugstore glasses by the register. When Elijah brings in two notebooks David
reads them between customers. "It took him forty minutes, and he didn't skip. Nobody else had read
every page." Then he says, "It's a lot of work, Mad." Another night he sets a beer down on the
printout, "right on the abstract, and it left a ring."

He has sat on the customer side of that bar "exactly twice in fifteen years. Once was the night
Elijah's father died. The other was when the Giants won the Series in 2010."

When he has to say the hard thing he uses "the voice he used when he had to cut somebody off."
When he pushes the twenty back the second time, "he said it to the twenty." Then he goes around
the bar and picks up the knife.

He answers a four-minute video with a thumb. He turns up with a hand truck because Elijah's
mother called him, ties the stove down himself, "a trucker's hitch on each corner, and yanked
every line," hugs her on the porch, and lifts two fingers off the wheel going down the drive.

**Reads as.** He loves by doing the next practical thing and by never making you ask. He will not
say something false to make you feel better and he will not chase you. He has watched a man he
loves disappear into a system once already.

**Voice.** "You're scaring the hoes, Mad." "They'd have found a reason, Mad. They always do."
"Mad. So does my dad." "I pour beer, Mad. What's it going to do, pour it?" "I wish you were
drunk. I'd know what to do with you." "I'd have told you anything you wanted. I'd have just
wanted you to go to bed." "Well. I'm not doing it twice." "You could just come back." "You're
not that powerful, Mad."

**His plate.** The stool. "It was the same stool. It had a new split in the vinyl."

**Blank.** His face. Whether the bar is his. Anybody he goes home to.

**Suggestions.**
1. Whose bar is it? The page leans one way without saying it. Ray "hung" the prayer "behind the
   register the year he quit drinking." A man does not hang things behind a register that is not
   his. In chapter 39 the prayer hangs "between the liquor license and a dollar bill." Let Elijah
   read the name on the license. One name tells you David is running his father's bar, and what
   it cost him to say "I watched my dad go like this for ten years."
2. He never gets a body, and I would not give him one. His hands do it: on the tap during the
   toast, on the lime knife, flat on the bar "the way he did for the real ones."
3. He stands behind the bar in every scene but one. In chapter 14 he "stayed where he was, behind
   the bar." If the reader has been shown that the bar is where he stands when he cannot help,
   the two times he comes around it land without a word.
