## 0. Top findings (read this first)

1.  **The yes-habit is real on the page and has one fixed formula.** "Yeah. No, yeah, of course." (898, 978, 999), "Yeah. No, yeah. For sure." (1179). Text names it: "He had been raised to be agreeable, and then he'd spent his twenties playing weddings, where the whole job is to say sure, we can do that one." (897). It runs Ch0 to Ch12, comes back once in Ch33 ("He didn't agree with her. He said it anyway." 2750), and is the subject of his Ch31 collapse (2594).
2.  **His three refusals of money/power in Act Two (Ruby 1162, Trent 1226, Gus 1281) are the "no"s that the text says he does not understand**: "He didn't know where that had come from" (1163), "he didn't know why" (1297). They are never explained by anyone in the text. Each is followed by isolation (Ch12).
3.  **"Goes off" is an explicit phrase only at 574 and 1740** ("Elijah went off." / "And Elijah went off."). Later the text paraphrases it: "He could feel himself going, and he went" (1999), "He was off" (2770), "Five years ago he'd have gone off" (3250). 3250-3253 is the clearest place where the text says he would once have gone off and shows him not doing it (compare 2699, where he stays silent while Grace lights the stove).
4.  **The single most repeated lesson is "ask first / don't decide for the other person"**, taught by at least eight different people (see 8a-B, 8a-C). He articulates it himself at 2770 ("It's whether you get to say come in") and at 2946, and acts on it at 3055-3056 (leaves the lamp). The text then shows the same principle costing him Grace (2946-2986) without the text resolving whether he was right.
5.  **Repeated mistake with the same shape:** the polished, solo-built, then-presented thing (renders 1910, optimized script 2016, dashboard with laser pointer 2108, fourteen slides 2509, one-page \$800 plan 2541). Marta/Denny/Lou each name it (1913, 2018, 2110, 2510) and he repeats it afterwards.
6.  **Acting alone / "meant to put it back"** recurs: the password line (2058-2059, Ch22), node 7 (2427, Ch28). The two-person rule was written for the first (2087) and he breaks its spirit in the second ("The rule was for things that touched the open internet. He had let himself believe that.").
7.  **Gaps the text leaves open on Elijah:** never explains why he does not cash Grace's co-op checks (2351) or why he cashes one at the end (3155, "He heard the word when he did it"); never says what "ideas" he had about the buckets (1470); Manny's "four words" offer through a truck window (3064) is not on the page earlier; the "thumb" that buzzes at 3223 is unattributed.
8.  **The arc beats the author's note describes are all there, but compressed:** he is believed at 2724-2732 and it "lasted about one minute"; at 3032 he says "I'd rather have been wrong"; at 3251-3253 he answers a stranger in four short lines and does not pitch.
