## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

**Portrait.** Kowalski's Hardware is "the third bay off the square, with wood worn pale where
four generations of forearms had rested on it and a bell over the door that Frank's grandfather
had hung." Frank "could tell you the torque spec for a head bolt from memory." He "would carry a
two-dollar part out to your truck like it was an appliance."

The store goes the way he later describes it. Gaps in the fastener aisle "that stayed gaps."
"The hours sign got re-taped twice." Then: "The paper on the door, when it came, was laminated.
Which meant Frank had known long enough to laminate it."

He comes to the co-op to run the tool library and hangs his grandfather's bell over its door.
"The first time it rang for a member checking out equipment, he looked up from the counter
before he could help it." He watches Tyler solder, makes "a small unhappy noise," folds his
glasses into his apron pocket and holds out his hand. "Son, these joints are cold. Give me the
iron."

On Elijah's last morning Frank "rang the bell over the tool library door, once, with his finger.
He didn't come out."

**Reads as.** He knew for a long time and said nothing, which is Elijah's own sin in chapter 25
turned around. What he sold was never the part.

**Voice.** "Fella in Hemingway gets asked how he went bankrupt. Says: two ways. Gradually, then
suddenly." "I'd have chosen some things, instead of hoping them." "Know what I was actually
selling? Somebody who knows which part. You can't download me." "The store taught me that.
Late." "That's a thermometer, Curtis."

**His plate.** The bell.

**Blank.** His age, a wife, children, where he lives. What is in the third bay off the square
now.

**Suggestions.**
1. The laminated paper is your Golden Plates move at full strength. I would use it once more:
   two years on, somebody drives through town and the paper is still on the door. Nothing lasts
   like a thing you laminate.
2. "When I was his age we put a man on the moon" makes him about seventy-seven, against "thirty
   years" behind that counter. Either is fine. They should agree.
3. At Thanksgiving he is "in a tie," and he says the prayer "half a beat early, in his inventory
   voice, for the kid to follow." The kid is the one he let go in the fastener aisle. Nobody
   needs to point that out.
