## The next pass: substrate, 19 November

From `NOVEL-SUBSTRATE-2026-11-19.md`.

She is the second lead and her arc is the mirror of his. He starts by telling everyone,
unasked, and ends by knocking and taking the answer. She starts by knocking and respecting a
no, and ends wanting the choice made for people. One reader built her whole file: every line,
every gesture, what she knows and when.

### On the page

- **Wound.** She worked for a fire district, sent ahead of the engines to knock on doors. An
  older man at the end of a road said "My house, my choice." She wrote REFUSED, tied flagging
  tape on his gate, and went on to the next forty doors. The fire came through that night. The
  text never says outright that he died.
- **The captain.** Her husband's line was "You can't make them leave, Grace." She left the job
  and him inside a month. "I couldn't tell you which one I was leaving."
- **What she does because of it.** Orange tape on everything she is responsible for: the gate,
  the hitch pin, the cooler, the lid of her receipts box, Elijah's door after the worst night
  of his life, the gatepost the morning he leaves. She feeds people. She answers every message
  herself. She puts her hand on people's forearms, twelve times in the book. She has two
  voices: a warm one, and the one she uses on trailer hitches.
- **Her rule, stated once:** "You knock, Elijah. He gets to slam it in your face. That part's
  his. But you knock."
- **What she will not say.** The file lists eight things she wants and never asks for: his pay,
  to sit nearer the stove, to be with him, his backing in front of the table, money for the
  route, what he said to her the night of the tea, that she had not been sleeping, and, the
  one that ends them, for him to go around the co-op and bring the arms in anyway. "I kept
  looking up the driveway for a truck."
- **Where she is wrong.** She lies to him once: when he asks what he said that night, she says
  "Nothing that mattered." She uses it on him seven months later.
- **Her high point** is lighting the Fisher by herself, hands shaking, in chapter 32. "I've
  been sleeping. All the way through."
- **Her last words in the book** are at Thanksgiving. "Why is it standing on the step? Just do
  it." Then, asked what Elijah would say: "He'd say it's their choice." Her last image is
  saying "as we forgive" while looking at his empty plate.

### How her turn is staged, step by step

The reader marked every statement she makes about asking, knocking and choosing, and which way
it leans. In order:

1. "Ask. Don't guess." (17) Leans toward asking.
2. Leaves soup behind a shut door for three days. "I should have knocked like Marta." (18)
3. "You showed them something true. And they chose." (24) Asking.
4. "I'd have signed. I'd have signed in the parking lot." (24) The first lean the other way,
   about a woman who could not choose.
5. "You knock... He gets to slam it in your face." (25) Her rule.
6. She knocks on his door and does not wait. (25)
7. "Every winter there's somebody on that route we don't get to in time. I can tell you their
   names." (27)
8. "People will vote for the fire if it's warm." (27)
9. "We asked nicely. I want that written down somewhere." (30)
10. She respects his "It's my choice" about the tea, and it costs her. (31)
11. "You don't hand people that... Promise me you won't post it." (33) The first time she
    controls what other people are told.
12. "Marta opened the door and walked in. Which one of us helped?" "I don't know." (33)
13. "Once they're packing boxes, nobody sends them back." (36) The first time she says a no
    should be overridden.
14. "Just do it." (38)

### What is blank

- Everything before the fire district. Where she is from. Who raised her. Whether "Blessings"
  is a faith or a habit.
- How she came to the co-op, and when. The oldest tape on the gate was there, faded, before
  Elijah arrived. The book never says who she tied it for.
- Whether the man at the end of the road died. Whether she went back.
- What she wants for herself. Every want she has in the book is for somebody else.
- What becomes of her. After "Maybe you should go home" she has two glimpses and one dinner.
  The route money, the thing she cares about most, is still unsettled on the last page.
- Her age, beyond "in her thirties." Any family. Any friend who is hers and not the co-op's.
  Marta says "She tells me everything," and that friendship is never shown.

### My read

- **The hole in her turn is a specific one.** Up to chapter 36 everything she doubts is about
  one person's no. At the fire she wants a whole group's no overridden, and she makes that
  jump only after Elijah names the gate for her. There is no step where she herself connects
  the man at the end of the road to the hundred and forty on the route. One scene, in her own
  nouns, would do it. The county hearing in chapter 30 is where I would look: Rosa stands up
  without being asked and says "You can't plan a week around maybe," and Grace sits with the
  receipts on her knees and says nothing.
- **She has a loss and no rise.** CT: "We're putting our characters through moments of great
  loss so that they can rise." Her loss before the book is the man at the gate. Her loss in
  the book is Elijah. What she does after it that she could not have done before is not on the
  page. Wanting the machine to "just do it" reads as the wound winning. If that is the ending
  he wants for her, it is a tragedy and should be played as one. If not, her rise is missing.
- **Her not asking is the richest thing about her and has no cause.** By the method that
  worked for Elijah, that is where her backstory goes. A woman whose whole job was to ask
  strangers to save themselves, and who was told "you can't make them," and who now cannot ask
  for one thing for herself. The book has all the behavior. It has no scene that says where it
  began.
- **She is the one person who treats Elijah as a person and not a use,** and she still writes
  to him because she wants something for other people. "I invited a person," she tells Marta.
  The section on the creator's story below makes this matter more.

### First steps

1. Run the dossier reader again on the current Doc, so the file includes the deed and her line

2. Ask the cold-read question of her alone: at each of her scenes, what does a first-time
   reader think she wants?
3. Fill her sheet. Bring CT the blanks that only he can fill: where she is from, whether the
   man died, and what her ending is.

## Character arcs, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-ARCS-2026-11-18.md`.

**Wants** nobody to go unchecked. The orange tape means "Somebody checked." **Wound:** the fire
district. A man refused to leave, she tied tape on his door and went to the next one, and inside
a month she had left the job and her husband, who was the captain. **The rule she lives by:**
"You knock, Elijah. He gets to slam it in your face. That part's his" (Ch 25).

**The arc as written:** soup behind a shut door and "I should have knocked like Marta" (Ch 18);
fifteen feet from the stove, then a foot closer (Ch 24); lights the Fisher herself, hands shaking
(Ch 32); "I love you. I'm not helping you scare people" (Ch 33); the kitchen in every town,
"somebody who knocks" (Ch 33); "I have to save them" and "I thought you were one of us" (Ch 36);
new tape on the gatepost, "She hadn't knocked" (Ch 37); "Why is it standing on the step? Just do
it." (Ch 38).

This is a good person argued out of her own rule by arithmetic. It is the strongest idea in the
cast. Four steps are missing.

1. **Becoming a couple is not on the page.** Ch 24 ends with the chair moving a foot. The next
   word is "in his kitchen, wearing his shirt" a year later.
   *Proposal:* use her own tell. Grace knocks. Soft knocks in Ch 18, "She hadn't knocked" at the
   gate in Ch 37. The night she comes in without knocking is the night it starts. Half a page.
   *Risk:* it could read as cute. It needs to be played plain.
2. **"You're the lamp" never comes back.** In Ch 31, out of his head, he tells her "You're just
   the lamp." He will never remember it. "She would." Then nothing, not even at the fire.
   *Proposal:* she says it back to him at the fire, when it will hurt most, and he does not know
   what she means. It also explains her Thanksgiving line. He accused her kindness of being the
   automatic kind, and by the end she wants exactly that from the machine.
   *Risk:* none I can find. The line is planted and loaded and is simply not fired.
3. **Her turn is under-prepared.** In July she will not hand people news "with nothing they can
   do about it." In October she wants to override the co-op's no. Between them there is one
   bridging question ("Which one of us helped?"). At the arms meeting she is silent and
   "counting." Then at the fire she blames him: "I kept looking up the driveway for a truck."
   She never asked him for the truck.
   *Proposal:* she asks. At or after the Ch 34 meeting she gives him the count out loud, in her
   own nouns: eighteen short, a hundred and forty boxes, December. She asks him to call the man.
   Then his "I'm letting it go" in Ch 36 is a no to her face, and the fire is earned.
   *Risk:* it makes Elijah harder to like in Ch 36. I think that is correct. He is choosing his
   principle over her people, and the reader should feel the cost.
4. **Her big speech has no root earlier in the book.** At the fire she says "Millions of people
   die every single day" and talks about poisoning the planet. **Corrected 19 November:** I
   called this out of her voice and proposed rewriting it. It is your own dictation of the scene
   on 14 November, nearly word for word, so that proposal is withdrawn.
   *What still holds:* nothing she says anywhere else prepares it. Plant the sentence once,
   earlier, in her mouth. The kitchen on a bad Tuesday would do.

## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

*Added 19 November: at the fire Elijah tells her, kindly, that this is about the gate. She hears an attack and answers: "And now you're taking out your mother trauma on me!" The text is in the Beats tab, under B.*

**Portrait.** She arrives fighting a trailer hitch. "Come on, you son of a bitch. Come on. I
greased you. I greased you Sunday." She kicks the tongue, it drops, "and she said 'Thank you,' to
it, sincerely." She is "in her thirties, in rubber boots and a canvas coat with an apron under
it, with a dark braid coming apart down her back." The trailer says KITCHEN on the side, painted
by hand. "It stopped crooked."

Then she sees him and "her whole voice changed. It went soft and warm, like a hand on a kid's
forehead." She takes his forearm "in her hand, just held it, the way some people shake." "You're
taller. You look so tired. Did you eat? I told you to eat."

There is orange flagging tape on the gatepost, on the hitch pin, on the handle of the cooler. He
asks. "Hm? Oh." She "glanced at it and away. 'Old habit.'"

When he goes under in November she knocks, "softly, around six each night," and leaves the door
shut. On his step: "three quart jars, lined up against the wall, lids on. Soup. Each one had a
square of paper under a rubber band." "No pressure. Blessings, G." She takes a pie to a widow's
porch for four hours, "a pie she hadn't made and said so." When she starts soup in the
twenty-quart pot, it "meant she'd already decided it was for the whole road."

She sits "a good fifteen feet from the stove." Then eight. "I haven't sat this close to one in
six years." Then she picks up her chair and moves it "a foot closer."

At the Lord's Prayer she says "thy will be done" "with her eyes open."

**Reads as.** She has two voices. The soft one is for people and the hard one is for machinery,
and the day she points the hard one at a person is an event. She checks on everyone. Marta has
her exactly: "Grace will carry you till her arms give out, and she will never once ask if you
can walk."

**Voice.** "I watched you get passed around down there like a casserole." "P.S. Eat something
first." "Ask. Don't guess." "You knock, Elijah. He gets to slam it in your face. That part's
his. But you knock." "God. We're fun." "People will vote for the fire if it's warm." "Nobody
ever has to prove they're hungry. It just shows up on Tuesday." "Why is it standing on the step?
Who told it to do that? Just do it."

**Her plate.** The orange tape. "That's what the tape is. Somebody checked."

**Blank.** Her surname. Where she is from. Any family. The captain's name. Which fire. Where she
slept before she slept at his place.

**Suggestions.** (Her four missing steps are in the arcs file. These are about making her alive.)
1. The man in the fire said, "My house, my choice." She wrote REFUSED and went to the next door.
   At the end of the book Elijah's whole position is that sentence: "it's their choice," and of
   his mother's lamp, "It was her house." At the bonfire he should say the man's words by
   accident, as near as you can get them. He has no idea what he just said. She does. "I can't
   believe I did this again" then needs no explaining.
2. She brings a pie she did not make "and said so." She cannot let even that stand. Then in
   chapter 32 she tells him what he said that night was "Nothing that mattered." It is the only
   lie she tells in the book. The pie is what makes the reader feel it, so the two should not be
   too far apart.
3. She ties tape on things that are fine: a hitch pin, a cooler, a box of receipts. She is
   marking what she has checked. So she carries a roll. Show it once in the coat pocket in
   chapter 16, fat. Show it once near the end, down to the cardboard.
4. The serving spoon is her gavel. She opens dinner holding it, points it at him to make the
   knocking speech, and when she cannot back his plan she is "looking at the serving spoon." It
   is already doing the work. I would only make sure she is not holding it at Thanksgiving.
5. The braid is always "coming apart." She is always arriving from work. The first time we see
   her not arriving from anywhere is "in his kitchen, wearing his shirt."
