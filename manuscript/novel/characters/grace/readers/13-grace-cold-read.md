# Grace: a cold read

Reader: first read, lines 519 to 3283 only. Entries are written as I reach each passage, in order, and are not revised later.

Context I carry in when she arrives (line 1595): Elijah has lost his job, his band, his best friend's trust, his mother's mind, the farm, and is living in his truck on four dollars. Every offer so far (Ruby, Trent, Gus) has been a rich man's offer and he has said no or been dropped. The book keeps showing me a pattern: Elijah says "yeah, no, yeah" to people who want something, and the one person who gave him something for free was a stranger on a curb.

## Log

Reaction: I like that she opens with "I'm going to be honest because I think you'd want that" (1596), then I flinch at it, because it is also what a pitch says. "Wait, why?": she says she watched the video "about fifty times" (1597) and almost wrote in March. That is a lot of attention from a stranger, and I do not know yet whether to find it warm or a little much. (3117) The narration then says what she wants outright: "she wanted the thing to quit asking and choose for everybody." (3119) I believed it entirely as the woman of "I have to save them" (2939). I felt a chill I could not place until I remembered the Architect's kitchen: a kind one who does not ask. The narration's "She was the kindest person at that table, anyone there would have told you so" (3119) is a verdict I did not need and I minded it.

**3120-3123 ("He'd say it's their choice").** Tyler asks what Elijah would say. "Grace didn't look up. 'He'd say it's their choice,' she said." (3122-3123) I did not expect this and I liked it more than anything she says in Act Four. She states his side fairly, without a sneer, after what she said at the fire. It tells me she heard him.

**3124-3126 (the four tables).** The book lists where Tyler, Wren, Curtis, Marta, Priya and Reuben sit. It does not tell me where Grace sits. "Wait, who?" I looked for her and could not find her.

**3127-3146 (Patricia, Rosa).** Grace is silent and absent for twenty lines. Patricia's confession is good. Attention left Grace here and I did not notice until the prayer.

**3153 (the prayer, eyes open).** "Grace said that line with her eyes open." The line is "Thy kingdom come, thy will be done" (3152). Right after she asked the machine to stop asking, she says "thy will be done" with her eyes open. I loved that.

**3161 (her last image).** "That was Grace. You could hear her under the rest. She was looking at an empty plate with a fork squared next to it." The line she says is "those who trespass against us." (3160) Wants: to forgive him, or to be forgiven, I cannot tell which. The tape, the REFUSED, the trespass, the plate. I felt it, and I also felt the book arrange it for me.

**3232 (Elijah at the Bear Flag).** He lists what he cannot change, including "a woman walking back toward a fire." (3232) She has been reduced to an image here.

**3263 (the live stream).** "He heard her. Under all the others." (3263) The only last contact is her voice in a prayer he watches on a stranger's laptop. He does not text her. The book ends without a word between them.

## End of Act Four (line 3283)

Who is she: a rescuer who learned to knock and cannot stop wanting the thing at the door to just go in. She is a person with a wound, and the wound is the story the book tells me about her. What she wants from her life: to never again write REFUSED on a sheet and walk away, and I think, beneath that, someone to come home to who will stay. What I expect: she stays at the co-op. She keeps the route going through the county study, keeps tying tape, and will be the person who, at some point, either takes the machine's side or has to watch it take hers. I do not expect a reunion on the page. I expect it to be offscreen and quiet.

### 3. The bonfire (chapter 36)

Lines I believed: 2931 (waiting for the truck), 2935 (people refuse what would save them), "I have to save them" at 2939, the pain at 2941, the pleading at 2957, the lamp at 2969, the content of 2971, "again" at 2985, "Don't touch me" at 2990, and the tape at 3048.

(2211), and the lamp (2613-2616, 2679). I did not see the demand that he go around the table with the arms (2933), or the size she gave the cause (2939, 2954).

Whose side, in order: 2924-2929 Elijah. 2931 Grace. 2932-2934 Elijah. 2935 Grace. 2936 Elijah. 2937-2939 Grace for feeling, Elijah for facts. 2940 Elijah. 2941 split. 2944 Elijah. 2945-2946 Grace, because his wording was bad. 2947 Elijah. 2948-2950 Grace. 2954 Elijah. 2957 Grace. 2959-2960 Elijah. 2962-2965 Elijah, then wavering. 2967 Grace. 2969-2971 Grace. 2977-2985 Grace. 2987-2990 Grace. 2994-2999 Grace.

Did her position feel like the same woman? Partly. Same woman: the one who needs to reach people in time, the one who goes to "I have to save them," the one who carries a wound in the same place for years, and the one who will not say a hard thing in front of the table (2570). Not the same: the woman of "Ask. Don't guess." (1858) and "Then I'm not taking any. I'm sitting next to you." (2599), who argued herself out of an easy yes at 2778, now urges a fait accompli at 2933 without a pause. The jump from "I can tell you their names" (2398) to "millions" is the one I could not close. I think the book wants it to be the same woman after fear, and I believe that of the shape and not of the sentences.

### 4. "Just do it" (3115) and her last image at the prayer

What I felt: dread, then tenderness. "Just do it" is the exact opposite of what she had been teaching me for two years, and it is also the most honest thing she says, because it is what the captain's line cost her. At 3123 she says "He'd say it's their choice," and I forgave her. At 3153 she says "thy will be done" with her eyes open, and at 3161 she says "trespass" looking at the empty plate. I felt sorrow, and the wish that someone would tell her she did not have to save everyone, and then I remembered Marta said it at 1898. What the book wants me to feel: that she is the kindest person in the room and also the one the Architect warned about, and that Elijah's refusal and her need are both right and the only thing between them is a plate. I think it also wants me to forgive her on his behalf. I felt that I was being given the forgiveness rather than earning it.

### 5. Is she a person, or is she there for Elijah?

Both, and I lean toward the second. For her being a person: the fire district and REFUSED (2197-2205), the shaking matches (2711), "What would you build?" (2769), the hitch voice and the warm voice, her admitting she heard "cook" as "ingredients" (1858), her saying "I couldn't. Not in front of them" (2570), her not looking at Marta or Frank (2479), and her line "He'd say it's their choice" (3123), which she gives in his absence. She has a body of habits (the arm, the tape, the soup, the phone rule) that nobody else has. For her being there for Elijah: she arrives at his lowest, in his email, with soup, and the first words about her in the narration are about what she does to him ("It went through him like a shot of something," 1767). Her one wound mirrors his (the gate, "My house, my choice" against his mother's farm). Her big scene in the book is a fight in which she reveals a line he said while sick. She leaves him with a tape, she is erased from the four tables at 3124, and her last image is her looking at his empty plate. She has no scene without him except the stove and the dinner, and in both she is reacting to him. The book is narrated almost entirely from his side, so everything she is arrives through his noticing. She is a person built to a good outline and used as a mirror.

### 6. What happens to her after the last page

I think she stays on the forty acres. The route continues through the county study. She keeps tying tape on the gate for as long as she is there. She is the person who will find out first what the machine does with a list of who ate this week, and that will be the next test of her. I think she and Elijah meet again, maybe at the Friday table or at the gate, and she does not apologize, and he does not ask her to. I do not think she leaves, and I do not think she is happy.
