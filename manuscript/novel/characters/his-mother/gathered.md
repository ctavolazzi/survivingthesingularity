## The next pass: substrate, 19 November

From `NOVEL-SUBSTRATE-2026-11-19.md`.

CT's words: "She's nice, she's kind, she's funny, she's sweet, she's charming, but she's also a

### On the page

- She is seventy-one and the book never says her name.
- **Before the stroke.** She calls every Sunday at 9:40, when her day ends, and starts talking
  before hello. The kitchen radio has been on one country station since before he was born.
  Nobody else washes the pot. She answers every message twice. "It's just tools, baby." "You
  look thin." She writes the date on a battery before it goes in the flashlight, and there is
  a flashlight in every room. She is behind with the bank from March and tells nobody.
- **What she did about his fear.** She filled the barn with rice, beans, water barrels and two
  of the same generator, and kept a notebook that begins "Elijah is worried about the
  computers. Be nice about it," with "What deal? Ask him" on page after page. She never asked.
- **After the stroke** she has one word. She pats his hand. She looks at the door. She turns
  her head toward the radio. She puts one finger on a photograph of the stove.
- **Barry** was her first love: two summers, a pitcher, a Mustang. The surname, the school and
  the car are mine and unconfirmed.
- **Her husband** died about ten years before the stroke. He watched Iron Derby with his son
  and drove five under the limit. His name, Joe, is mine and unconfirmed.
- **Melody** is her husband's daughter from before. "She's not my fucking mother."
- **Her own mother** is the grandmother of chapter 0: the madrones, the Fisher stove, the
  downed oaks. The farm came down her side.
- Seven of her lines were lost to the stroke. Four were saved. The record is in `ops.py`.

### What is blank

- Her name.
- Her life as her own. What she wanted at twenty. Why Barry and then not Barry. How she came
  to marry a man who already had a daughter.
- **What passed between her and Melody.** Melody's rage is on the page at full volume and its
  cause is not. This is the largest blank in the family.
- Whether she understood, at any point, that she was slipping.
- What the stroke is from the inside. The book stays with Elijah, so this may be right to
  leave blank. It should be a choice.
- What it means that the word she is left with is a man's name from before her whole adult
  life.

### My read

- **She is two people and the book has only shown one.** "Nice, kind, funny, sweet, charming"
  is on every page she is on. "Perfectionist and catastrophizer" is on the page as objects:
  the pot, the batteries, the barn. It is never on the page as something she does to a person.
  The person it would have landed on hardest is a stepdaughter. If the woman who let nobody
  else wash the pot was also a woman a girl could never do right by, then Melody is not only a
  villain on a phone, and "greedy little grub" is a child's answer to a house where she was
  the one who was always wrong. That would make three people out of one phone call. It would
  also cut against "sweet," and that is his call and nobody else's.
- **Her arc after the stroke has to be made of what she reaches for,** because she has one
  word. The book already does this three times: the door, the radio, the stove. A fourth and
  fifth, placed on purpose, would be an arc. What does she reach for last?
- **The barn is the best thing in her story and it ends in chapter 13.** She put by a winter of
  food for a danger she did not understand, because her son was frightened. He sold it with
  the place. He "signed for the rice too." Nothing in the back half of the book remembers the
  buckets. The co-op exists to feed people.
- **Barry may be the key to both of them.** Elijah spends the book not being seen: by the end
  his own mother looks at him and asks for someone else, and he stops correcting her. The
  same word that erases him is the one thing in the book that is purely hers.

### First steps

2. Bring CT the Melody question directly, with the reading above as one option. He invented
   Melody in two messages and dictated her call in one. He will know.
3. Decide her name, even if it is never printed.

## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

*Changed 19 November: you decided she has a massive stroke in Act Two, after months of
forgetting things, and goes into a care facility, and that Elijah sells the farm to pay for it.
After the stroke she has one word, "Barry," and it turns out to be her first love. In your
words she is "nice, she's kind, she's funny, she's sweet, she's charming, but she's also a
perfectionist and a catastrophizer," and she eats a little tub of Ben and Jerry's every day.
She never believed the video. She was humoring him. This profile describes her as the draft
has her before the stroke. The new version and every later scene it touches are in
`drafts/draft-elijah-crash-out-arc.md`.*

**Portrait.** She is seventy-one and the book never says her name. She calls every Sunday at 9:40,
"when her day ended, whether she had seen him that week or not," and starts talking before
hello. Behind her the kitchen radio plays "the same country station since before he was born,"
and somewhere in every call there is "the clank of the pot she never let anyone else wash."

She answers every message twice. To the video that is about to turn his life over she sends "I
watched it. You look thin." Then: "Is that Mom's stove?" Later she asks about the floor. "Is that
floor clean? You were sitting right on it."

When he comes home and cannot stop crying, she "didn't ask." She puts a blanket on him "that
smelled like the hall closet." She makes "the chicken thing with the rice, and then the soup you
make from the chicken thing," sets each plate on the coffee table, and turns the radio up a
little "so he'd know she wasn't listening." On Sunday at 9:40 she calls his phone from the next
room.

She calls at two in the afternoon once in the whole book. "The radio was off behind her."
Somebody has died.

**Reads as.** She cannot follow the list and never pretends to. She tracks his body: thin, tired,
eating. Everything she feels comes out as something done with her hands or with the volume on
the radio.

**Voice.** "You sound tired." "It's just tools, baby. There's always somebody has to fix the
tools. That'll be you." "I didn't follow it, baby." "She married? I'm asking." "Go be useful,
baby. You're underfoot." "I said working." "It looks like somewhere." "I know, baby. Are you
eating?" "Well. Then you're somewhere." "It reads better than it sounded."

**Her plate.** The pot. It goes into the rack in four separate phone calls.

**Blank.** Her name. Her face. What the farm raises (we get chickens, a well, a barn and a fallow
field). Who Manny is to her.

**Suggestions.**
1. She spots her own mother's stove in a four-minute video before she takes in the end of the
   world. If the pot was her mother's too, nobody has to explain why no one else washes it.
2. The radio is already a gauge: on is ordinary, up is privacy, off is a death. In chapter 39 it
   is "The radio was on. So was the lamp, probably." If the lamp is the first thing in forty
   years allowed to talk over that station, the reader will feel what Elijah is leaving alone.
3. Her name. Keep it unsaid. If you want it in the book, let it be read: on the birthday card the
   church ladies left with the lamp.
