## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

*Added 19 November: your two lines, "You don't control when you get hungry. You don't control when you have to pee," go to Lou in chapter 35. He is the one who already gives Elijah the book's wisdom.*

**Portrait.** "A man of about sixty who was sunburned right up to his hat line," with "very pale
eyes in all that sunburn." He cracks walnuts into a bowl through a whole dinner "without once
looking at his hands." He has "one knee that's mine and one that's titanium, and the titanium
one's the good one." His first question about the machine is "What's it eat?"

He grew pears outside Ukiah "on ground his grandfather cleared," until "the one buyer named the
price, and the price was less than a pear cost to grow." He tells that "the way you'd tell
somebody your blood type." His wife "taught fourth grade for thirty-one years and left me her
pension."

He does his sums with a pencil on a turned-over seed box, and at the bottom he writes HANDS. He
draws the county board on the back of a seed catalog, "five boxes with five names." At six every
morning he is "standing at the head of the lower beds with a coffee, looking over the kale." He
goes to the county meeting "in a shirt with a collar, which nobody had ever seen before."

"He asked you about three questions first, to find out where you were standing, and then he told
you one thing."

On Elijah's last morning he does not come up from the field. He "lifted the hose over his head
with one hand, like a man holding up a trophy."

**Reads as.** He has already lost the thing the co-op is afraid of losing, and he got up the next
morning.

**Voice.** "Shit. That's cheaper than my nephew." "You look like a man trying to put pants on an
octopus." "It's hands. It's somebody bent over at six in the morning." "Somebody is always
eating the difference." "I have been in a relationship with plants for fifty years, and not one
of them has ever called me back." "Then what are you doing on your knees in my tomatoes?" "It's
five scared people and a clock." "The plants look thirsty."

**His plate.** The seed box with HANDS on it.

**Blank.** His surname. His wife's name and when she died. Children (there is only a nephew).
Where he lives.

**Suggestions.**
1. Three questions and then one thing. His wife taught fourth grade for thirty-one years. He
   teaches the way she did, and nobody in the book has noticed, including him. Protect it: he
   should never once lecture.
2. The collar. One adjective decides whose shirt that is. If it still has the creases from the
   package, he bought it for the meeting. If it is old and it fits, she bought it.
3. He "talked with his hands, which he only did when he meant it." So his hands are still in most
   scenes. Count them. They should move twice in the book, at most.
