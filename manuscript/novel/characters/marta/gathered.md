## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

**Portrait.** She greets him with her back. "Looking for the mechanic? Two miles up, left at the
grange hall. This isn't that." When she does turn around she gives him "one long level pass,
boots to haircut." She is "fifty-something, with thick forearms," in a leather apron. Her hand
tools are "shadow-boarded so carefully they looked like an evidence display."

She numbers her points. "One." "Two." Before the third she "pulled her glove off, finger by
finger." Then: "In 2009 I watched a company do to nine hundred of us what you think your machine
is going to do to everybody."

She eats "like a woman refueling a machine." She says "Huh," "which from her was a parade." She
"did not raise her voice. Everyone in the shed understood that to be the worst available
outcome." When Elijah stops coming out of his room she "knocked once and let herself in," says
nothing about the dishes, pours herself coffee "in one of his mugs," and turns it a quarter
turn.

She puts his best practice weld in the bench vise, slips a length of pipe over the free end, "and
broke it with one pull." "It sat on top of the steel and never became the steel. From six feet it
looks like mine." Then she "set the two halves on the bench where he could take them."

Her physical therapy is on Tuesdays, "for the shoulder she never mentioned."

At Thanksgiving, holding two hands, "her mouth made the words. It took everything she had."

**Reads as.** She tests everything to destruction because once she vouched for something she had
not tested. "I have been breaking welds in a vise for twenty years. It doesn't bring him back.
It's what I can do before noon."

**Voice.** "Whose app?" "That's not a limitation, college. That's the load-bearing wall." "There
was no machine. There was a spreadsheet and a tax break." "The layoff is one day, they cater it,
there's a folder. The after goes on, and nobody caters that." "Soup's on at noon. That's not a
reward, college. It's lunch." "Ugly is a maintenance feature." "I don't weld those seams tired,
and I don't let anybody weld them proud." "Things die of people, college." "Go find out if they
ate." "We'll see."

**Her plate.** The bench vise and the length of pipe.

**Blank.** Which plant, what it made, what town. Where she is from (the book gives two names,
Okonkwo and Emeka, and nothing else). A partner, children. Where she sleeps. It is never said
outright that Emeka died.

**Suggestions.**
1. "Marta: knows the Luddite hangings cold. Where does a welder learn that?" Elijah writes the
   question down and the book never answers it. Answer it with an object. A paperback on the
   shop shelf with its spine broken and a library's WITHDRAWN stamp inside the cover. That is
   what she did with the winter of 2009, and nobody has to say so.
2. Tuesday. Emeka died on a Tuesday. She has not been in a church "since a Tuesday twenty years
   ago." Her therapy, for the shoulder she never mentions, is on Tuesdays. Elijah arrives on a
   Tuesday and leaves on one. If that is deliberate, it is very good. If it is not, it should
   become deliberate.
3. Nobody knows where she sleeps. She designed the shouse as "a shop that's set up to keep the
   person running it alive." Let there be one thing in the shed that is not a tool.
4. In chapter 29 her cup shakes and Elijah "thought it was the shoulder." She has just offered a
   boy four years of her life. This is the method working: the narrator lets Elijah be wrong and
   trusts the reader. Leave it exactly as it is.
5. "We'll see" is said seven times and means yes six of them. The one time it does not, the book
   says so. I would cut that gloss and trust the count.
