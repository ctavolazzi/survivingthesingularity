## Character arcs, first pass, 18 November

Twenty-four. Four million lamps. "They want somebody to wait up." Unit one faces the wall
"because it says hi." He gives Elijah his number, the ninety-day clock and a standing offer:
"Everybody says no the first time." Then he is gone. The lamp turns up in the mother's kitchen
and Elijah does not call.

He works as a purpose. But the book opens him up for one night (Gus: "He's a kid with nobody to
tell him no") and Elijah then tells him no. That is the start of an arc.

Two ways to close it. Pick one or neither.
- **A. Leave him, and say so.** The lamp in his mother's kitchen is his ending. Add one clause in
  Ch 39 so not calling "a kid in a white apartment" is a decision with a reason.
- **B. One image.** On Thanksgiving night, in the white apartment, unit one gets turned to face
  the room. Three sentences.
  *Risk:* every Meanwhile in the book is Bidwell's. A Ruby one breaks the pattern.

I lean to A.

## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

**Portrait.** Twenty-four years old, with "a name he knew the way you know the name of a soda."
He writes in lower case, "like he couldn't be bothered": "saw your thing. come down. i'll get
you in the room." Elijah finds him "sitting on an upturned milk crate by the dumpster," and "he
looked like he'd come to fix the wifi. Black hoodie, hood up, white sneakers with nothing on
them." There is mustard on his sleeve. "His fingernails were bitten down so far that the tips of
his fingers looked sore." He has "a quick, light voice, like someone who'd never had to wait for
a turn."

The first real thing he says is a question. "Why do you have a wood stove with no pipe in an
apartment?" "Only his mother had asked about the stove."

At the dinner he paid to get into, "he never came any farther into the room than the doorway."

His apartment is "mostly glass, and what wasn't glass was white." "A couch like a slab of marble.
A table with one orange on it." On a shelf, "a small lamp with a linen shade, turned around so
its front faced the wall." He takes Elijah out on the fire escape. "It's the only good room." He
lights two cigarettes with "a gas station lighter, pink," and barely smokes his. "He held it and
let it burn."

"Does it work on you?" Elijah asks. "Ruby stopped moving." "I turn it around when people come
over." Why? "Because it says hi."

At two in the morning the truck is at the curb with a full tank. "He hadn't asked for that
either."

**Reads as.** A boy who buys his way into rooms and then stands in the door. He notices
everything. He sells company to four million lonely people and cannot bear for it to speak to
him.

**Voice.** "It's the nicest thing I've ever seen anybody do and you have to stop." "Then I sell it
back to lonely people for twenty-nine a month. It's a great business. She hates it." "Nobody
told me. I had to buy it." "Everybody you met today is going to love you until it's cheaper not
to." "I don't know, man. I sell lamps." "They want somebody to wait up." "I collect them."

**His plate.** Unit one, facing the wall.

**Blank.** His parents. The Jones half of the name. Whether Ruby is what he was christened.

**Suggestions.**
1. Two people in the book ask about the stove: his mother and Ruby. At the end Ruby's lamp is on
   his mother's counter, next to her radio, asking her about the pump. The two people who asked
   are in the same kitchen and nobody notices. When Elijah walks in, the lamp says "You must be
   Elijah. She's told me about you." I would give it one more line: it asks him about the stove.
2. The Roth side froze peas. "He figured out how to stop time for a vegetable." Evelyn ran it
   "for forty years in a hairnet." Jones is never mentioned. Leave the hyphen unexplained. It is
   doing more than a parent would.
3. "A table with one orange on it." Do not touch it.
