## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

**Portrait.** "He had a white beard and a long coat, in August, and a duffel bag over one
shoulder. He looked like a wizard who had been out in the weather a long time. He shuffled." He
is "in the middle of an argument with somebody who wasn't there." Sitting down on the curb
"took him a while."

He goes through his coat and comes out with "a little rubber squeaky toy, the kind you buy for a
dog." He holds it up and squeaks it once. "What do you think about this?" Elijah says it is
nice. "The old man nodded and put it away."

He offers an apple. "It was red and it didn't have a mark on it." Refused, "the apple went back
in the bag." Asked how his wife died, he goes through his coat "for a long time and didn't
answer." Then: "Cancer." And a photograph, "soft at the corners." "She was laughing at whoever
was holding the camera." He "buttoned it into the pocket."

Asked why the rich don't help, he "didn't answer that. He started rummaging in the duffel again"
and comes out with a gas station cherry pie. Offered a spliff: "I don't need that. No thanks."
He does not drink coffee. "I hate it. Makes my heart bump."

Getting up, he gets "a hand on the bumper of the truck." Then "a twenty-dollar bill, folded up
small." "I been watching that truck sit there all week. It don't run on nothing."

Elijah "had never asked his name." The next day, at the food bank, "he looked for the old man in
the line. He didn't see him."

**Reads as.** He answers the questions that can be answered with something from the bag and lets
the others go by.

**Voice.** "How biblical." "All right. Fine. Keep your secrets." "How privileged are you? I'm
joking. Lighten up a little, kid." "Somebody did this for me." "Sounds like really you just hate
yourself." "You gotta fill your own cup." "Focus on the people that say yes." "You got the
energy of Jesus about you." "Don't be like me."

**His plate.** The squeaky toy. There is no dog.

**Blank.** His name. Hers. Where he sleeps. Who the somebody was. Who he argues with.

**Suggestions.**
1. He is Walt. In your Golden Plates outline the thrift shopkeeper is "mysterious and
   wizard-like," with "an air of magic but shows no actual magical abilities." This is the same
   man, years on, and the rule should be the same rule: nothing he does is magic. An
   unmarked apple out of a duffel bag in August, a twenty folded small, and he is not in the
   line the next day. That is the right amount. Do not add any.
2. Never explain the toy.
3. "She was laughing at whoever was holding the camera." He was holding the camera. Leave it for
   the reader.
