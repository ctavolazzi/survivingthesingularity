## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

**The food bank ladies.** "Two women at a folding table" behind a church. "There was no form."
One of them "looked at him the way his mother would have, and handed him two." "You come back in
two weeks, honey."

**Trent.** "Named Trent and wore a vest." He walks Elijah through "acres of empty concrete
talking about story." When Elijah says he only wants to learn, Trent "looked at him like he'd
answered in Portuguese." "We'll sort you out Friday." "The second Friday Trent was in Los
Angeles."

**The kid with the tablet.** "The kid scrolled. He scrolled back up. He was nice about it." "I
believe you, man. I just can't. I'm sorry." He is the best-behaved person at the party and it
makes no difference.

**The room in San Francisco.** A man from Washington "with a flag pin" who "said the word future
nine times. Elijah counted. He did not say the word rules once." Two tribes: "They wore a lot of
gray and they said off-ramp and guardrails," and "They wore sneakers that cost what his water
pump cost, and they said ship." A founder of something to do with cooling who tells him "Nobody
in here has a friend," and then "her whole face came back on, and she went." Six months later,
when he texts her from a parking lot with a fever, she "sent back a heart."

**The strangers who write back.** A woman in Tulsa who drove a forklift until "one Monday her
supervisor was a tablet on a pole." "I thought I was the only one." A paralegal in Fresno. A
shop teacher. A nurse. A session drummer in Nashville, whose message "Elijah read twice." A guy
who fixed elevators. And Earl, "who thinks you're a demon, and you wrote Earl three paragraphs."

**Suggestions.**
1. Earl never appears and never should. But Elijah wrote him three paragraphs. If one copy of the
   book goes to a stranger, it goes to Earl.
2. The heart. It is the cruelest thing in the book and it is one character long. Leave it alone.
