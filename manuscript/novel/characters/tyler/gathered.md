## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

**Portrait.** He is nineteen and has "a mustache he was still negotiating with" and a 3D printer
in his bedroom. He turns up with "a milk crate of forty cases, printed in plastic the color of a
school bus." "Nobody had asked him to." Elijah has blocked out two weeks to write the firmware.
"Tyler had shown up on Thursday with it done." He works on an upturned bucket with "a laptop
covered in stickers on his knee." When he is attacked for how he did it, "his voice cracked on
free, and he went red, and kept going."

Frank lets him go "in the fastener aisle." Tyler "came out to the shed the next morning like it
was any other day." He builds a computer out of "a steel ammo can. A seven-inch screen. A
keyboard off a dead laptop. The battery from a cordless drill." When the models go dark he
admits it: "I can't read it. I told Patricia I read all of it. I read it like you read the terms
on an app." Then he fills half a notebook learning to. When he works out why Patricia's reports
have stopped, he runs them overnight and drives the thumb drive "two valleys over himself. He
never said a word about what he'd figured out."

At twenty-one he holds his coffee "like it might be repossessed," in "a welding jacket that was
still too big for him."

**Reads as.** He gives before he is asked and he keeps other people's secrets. He is braver than
his voice.

**Voice.** "I mean, yeah. Why wouldn't I?" "It's forty boxes that water tomatoes." "It's just a
tool. You drove here in a computer." "The future, but there's gardens. And the robots are nice.
And nobody's a dick about it." "You were right, you know. About all of it."

**His plate.** The ammo can.

**Blank.** His surname. Any family (there is only "his bedroom"). Where that bedroom is.

**Suggestions.**
1. The cases are school-bus yellow, and every controller the co-op ships for three years stays
   that yellow. Nobody asks why. If it is the one spool a nineteen-year-old on register wages
   had, then the co-op's whole product line is the color of what Tyler could afford, and nobody
   ever says the word poor.
2. Whose jacket is it? He never says yes to Marta's four years. If the jacket that is "still too
   big for him" used to be hers, the jacket is the yes, and it is also her answer.
3. "His bedroom," at nineteen, and at twenty-one. Do not explain. One line about whose Corolla it
   is.
