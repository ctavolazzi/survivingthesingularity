## Character profiles, first pass, 18 November

From `NOVEL-CHARACTER-PROFILES-2026-11-18.md`.

**Portrait.** She leads the Sunday circle and makes the candles. She carries "a thermos of
something that smelled like a wet lawn." She says everything "very gently." When she objects she
puts "her hand flat on her chest." "The food knows who grew it." "You can't automate reverence."
"I'm just noticing there's a lot of urgency in the room." "I'm noticing some scarcity." When a
vote goes against her: "I would like it written down, that I didn't decide."

When Lou makes a joke at her expense she "laughed too, a little, in spite of herself." At the
harvest festival she "fainted at the cider press on Saturday and went right back to it."

The morning after Elijah's plan dies there is a note on the whiteboard. "Green marker, block
capitals, no name": CAN WE TALK ABOUT EXTRACTIVE FRAMING IN OUR SHARED SPACES? There is "a
little heart after the question mark."

At Thanksgiving, when Patricia confesses, Wren "took her hand away from her own chest and moved
her chair six inches the other way." During the prayer her "lips weren't moving. She held on
anyway."

**Reads as.** She has a vocabulary instead of a past. Under it she works until she drops and she
laughs when the joke is good.

**Her plate.** The hand flat on her own chest. Elijah puts his hand flat on bark, steel and dirt.
She puts hers on herself.

**Blank.** Everything. She is the only member of the co-op with no body, no age, no history and
no work except the circle and the candles.

**Suggestions.**
1. Her words come from somewhere. "Noticing." "Scarcity." "Extractive framing." "I hear that."
   People learn to talk that way in an office. Give her one slip in the old register, one
   phrase from whatever job taught her to run a room, and let her catch herself. That is her
   "I never dropped a pass."
2. Never say who wrote the green note. The heart does it.
3. The six inches. She moves her chair away from Patricia and then holds a hand through a prayer
   that is not hers. Those two acts in one evening are the most alive she is in the book. If the
   tea circle gets its aftermath (arcs, section 5), it should be this small.
