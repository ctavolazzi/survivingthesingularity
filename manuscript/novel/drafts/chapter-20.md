# Chapter 20: The Bottom of the Column (draft 1, 26 Nov)

A draft for CT, from `PLAN-CHAPTERS-20-TO-36.md`. **Nothing here is logged.** The chapter stays as it is except for
three changes. CT's words would be in **bold**; there are none new in this chapter. Everything under "Change to" that
is new is Claude's.

## 1. The forty boxes, waiting on summer (NEW, after the paragraph that ends "on a floor in Stockton in August.")

Book now:

> He was thirty-seven years old and he had never been so tired in his life, and he had once bolted two million dollars
> of robot arms to a floor in Stockton in August.
>
> At noon they sat on the tailgate of Lou's truck and ate Grace's sandwiches ...

Change to (one paragraph between them):

> He was thirty-seven years old and he had never been so tired in his life, and he had once bolted two million dollars
> of robot arms to a floor in Stockton in August.
>
> Out in forty greenhouses two valleys over, the co-op's boxes were opening vents on their own every warm afternoon.
> Lou had driven six of them out himself and come home with a photo of each one bolted to its post, which he showed
> people whether they asked or not. Every one of those boxes had Elijah's board inside it, the one an Iowa company's
> machine had laid out at midnight, and nobody had tested a single one in July yet, because it was May.
>
> At noon they sat on the tailgate of Lou's truck and ate Grace's sandwiches ...

Why: the heat failure in Chapter 21 needs the reader to know the boxes are out, that Lou is proud of them, and that
the board is the secret from Chapter 19. "Because it was May" is the one dry line.

## 2. Ma, and Barry (NEW, after "There you go."; and line flag 1)

Book now:

> "There you go."
>
> She'd eaten his too, he thought. A barn of buckets. Wheat she had no way to grind.
>
> It was the best explanation of anything he'd gotten since he came north ...

Change to:

> "There you go."
>
> She'd eaten the difference for him too, he thought. A barn of buckets. Wheat she had no way to grind.
>
> "Where's she now?" Lou said.
>
> "A place in Santa Rosa with a rose garden. I call Sundays." Elijah hadn't planned on the rest of it. "Mostly she asks
> for somebody named Barry."
>
> "Barry," Lou said, the way he'd say the name of a seed he meant to look up.
>
> It was the best explanation of anything he'd gotten since he came north ...

Why: Chapter 25's Thanksgiving scene has Lou say "You said so yourself. All she says is Barry." This is where he
heard it. "She'd eaten his too" was line flag 1 (the reader can't tell what "his" is).

## 3. Wren's comeback (line flag 2)

Book now:

> "Then here's somebody who'd call you back," Wren said. "When the machine lays the tape on the big places, the crews
> from Michoacán don't get a titanium knee. They get nothing. Did anybody ask them?"

Change to:

> "The crews would call you back," Wren said. "When the machine lays the tape on the big places, the crews from
> Michoacán don't get a titanium knee. They get nothing. Did anybody ask them?"

Why: the reader had to work out who "somebody" was. Same joke, said straight.
