## 3. The heat (NEW, after a scene break)

> The last week of June the valley hit a hundred and four.
>
> Hector and Lupe Ochoa had the last of the north houses, up where the road bends, and a hoop house behind it where they
> started plugs for the network: cabbage, kale and broccoli for fall, six thousand of them in trays on wire benches.
> For eleven years Lupe had kept a clipboard on a nail by the door and written the temperature on it every morning and
> every afternoon. She stopped in May, when the co-op's box went up on the south end wall, in the sun, where the
> instructions said to put it.
>
> At three o'clock she went out with the hose and found the vent shut and the box dark. The thermometer by the door
> read a hundred and thirty-one. Every tray on both benches had gone the color of wet newspaper.
>
> She called Hector, and Hector called the co-op.
>
> By the time Lou got there she had the plastic rolled up on both sides and was standing in the doorway with the hose
> running into the gravel. Lou walked the benches with her, down one side and back up the other. He put his hand flat
> on a tray and took it off again.
>
> "Six thousand," Lupe said. "That's October."
>
> "I know it," Lou said.
>
> Patricia called Grace before the sun was down. The plugs had been the network's. Grace put her on speaker on the
> bench in the shed, so she wouldn't have to say any of it twice.
>
> "It's the code," Patricia said. "That program your boy wrote in one night. The one nobody can read."
>
> Tyler had the Ochoas' box open in front of him, with Marta's card propped against the vise. Power or brain? Halve it.
> He kept his eyes on the board.
>
> "It's all of it," Curtis said from the door. "Code, board, box. We hung a machine on Hector's wall and went home."
>
> "Nobody says what it was until it's been tested," Priya said. "Not on the phone, and not tonight."
>
> "I can have something up by morning," Denny said. "People are going to ask. Better they hear it from us."
>
> "Hear what?" Priya said. "We don't know anything yet."
>

>
> Elijah stood behind Tyler with his hands in his pockets. From there he could see the little square part beside the

>
> Grace had been typing on her phone the whole time. She looked up. "Eleven boxes are on south walls," she said.

>
> Outside it was still ninety-eight degrees, and it was eight o'clock at night.

## Notes for CT

- **The couple's names, Hector and Lupe Ochoa, are Claude's.** Change them if you like.
- **Your lines:** all of Priya's warning and her question to Elijah. Claude added "I lost by two hundred votes, and I
  still get letters" (the book says she lost by about two hundred).
- **The flaw** (from our talk): the small power part on the board is packed against the motor driver with no room to
  shed heat. In July sun it overheats and shuts itself off, and the vent stays shut. It passed the April test in a
  cold shed. Tyler finds that it works when cold, which clears his code. Elijah confesses it in Chapter 22.
- **Grace's plan** is the "intelligent" step toward the pond: she works the phones while the men argue about blame.
- **Lupe's clipboard** is the seed for the book's theme: she stopped checking by hand the day the machine went up.
- **Checked against the rules:** one idea per sentence; openings by what the sentence is about; no counting, no "Then
  he," no mirrored lines; no "the way" comparison; the light touches are Marta's "We don't have hats," Curtis's "We
  hung a machine on Hector's wall and went home," and the last line. No feeling is named; the reflection is shown
  through what is still in his phone.
- **Length:** about 1,050 words added. The chapter was the shortest in the book (937 words).
