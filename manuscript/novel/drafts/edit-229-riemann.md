## Added in Chapter 27, after "...and he didn't know how to start."

Dinner on the Friday before Thanksgiving was just dinner. Nobody had brought an argument. People were eating, and it was a normal day, right up until Curtis stood up with his phone in his hand.

"Guys. They just solved the Riemann hypothesis."

"Oh my God, Curtis, come on," Priya said. "Not another one. Can't you see we're eating?"

"No, guys, this is a big deal. This one's serious."

"Curtis, what does that mean?" Grace said. "No one understands what you're saying."

"This is crazy. That's one of the craziest things I've ever seen." Curtis hadn't sat back down. "I can't believe they solved the friggin' Riemann hypothesis. It's eluded all of humanity's best mathematicians for over a hundred years. And the latest model, the secret one they keep behind closed doors and don't let the public use, just solved it."

"Okay," Frank said. "What's that mean for those of us that don't speak math?"

Curtis went right past him. "What's next? I mean, they already did the Navier-Stokes one in September. Is fucking math solved? Is physics next?"

"Curtis, why are you freaking out right now?" Denny said.

"Because it's one of the Millennium Prize problems."

That was Marta, from the far end of the table.

"What is that?" Denny said.

"Each one of them is worth a million dollars," Marta said. "A lot of years ago, some guy solved one of them and then turned down the money."

Tyler already had his phone out. "Perelman. Russian guy. Twenty ten. He literally said no to a million dollars."

"If AI is solving that caliber of math…" Marta said, and she stopped there.

Curtis was a wingnut, and the table had learned to eat through his headlines. But Marta was shaken. Sentences didn't get left unfinished when they were hers. The forks stopped.

"Marta," Priya said. There was nobody at that table whose opinion she respected more. "What does this mean?"

"It means AI is better than humanity at math."

A chorus of voices came back at her. That can't be true. No, there's still this. No, there's still that. People said all the classic things, the whataboutism and the goalpost moving. But Marta knew, and Elijah could tell that she knew, that this was seriously a big deal.

"Wait, what is this, does it like break cryptography?" Tyler said. "Do we not have bank accounts anymore?"

"I mean, not exactly," Marta said. "It's more like…" She thought for a second. "It's more like removing doubt. Picture you had an older sibling, and you assumed they were always going to be better than you at, let's say, skateboarding."

"Okay, where are you going with this, Marta?" Priya said.

"Hold your horses, young lady. Let's just say you assume your older sibling is always going to be better than you. But you go do a competition anyway, and they just smoke you." Marta waved her hands. "Just absolutely wreck your shit. And you realize that no matter how much you practice, no matter what you do, they're always going to outcompete you at this one specific thing."

"Yeah," Curtis said. "AI's doing a kickflip with math right now."

Lou laughed so hard he had to put his cup down. Marta didn't laugh.

"And it always will," she said. "From this point forward, it's reasonable to assume that artificial intelligence will always be better at math than humans."

So it can do everything I did at Claypot and more, Elijah thought.

"So what? Who cares?" Tyler said. "It's good at math. Isn't that a good thing? I hate math. I don't like doing math problems. Isn't it good if that's solved?"

Elijah spoke up. "It means we're one step, well, more like one giant leap closer to RSI."

The whole room went silent. He meant recursive self-improvement, a machine that could do the math to build a better machine.

"RSI?" Tyler said.

Heads shook up and down the table. "Oh my God," somebody said.

"What? Come on. What is that, like a video game?" Tyler looked at Elijah with a twinkle in his eye.

Oh wait, Elijah thought. Does he know?

But Tyler went right back to it. "What? Who cares? GTA 6 is awesome. The physics are really good. Why is this a bad thing?"

People got up grumbling. They walked off to go think about it, and also just to get back to what they'd been doing.

"I gotta get back to work," Priya said.

That was how people had been reacting so far. I gotta get back to my job. I can't think about this. But Elijah knew. And now he suspected Tyler knew too, more than he was letting on.

Grace stayed behind after the rest had gone. She had questions. She got it, but she didn't totally get it.

"One moment it can't do video at all," Elijah said. "And the next moment we've got photorealistic Will Smith eating spaghetti."

"Okay, but isn't that dangerous?"

"I mean, only if people use it wrong."

Grace scoffed. "Elijah, when have people ever used things right the first time?"

"Yeah, I know, but this is different. This is something where on the table is curing all disease, is solving so many problems."

"Oh, come on. You don't actually believe that, do you?"

Elijah was taken aback. He furrowed his brow, confused. "I staked my life on it."

Grace got tense. Both of their bodies tensed up, and their shoulders went up.

"Elijah."

"Grace."

"Don't tell me you drank the Kool-Aid while you were there at Claypot."

"Not exactly, but," he said, and gestured to his notebooks. "I am sort of working on a book about this."

"Look." She took him by the hands. "I care about you so much. Please tell me that no matter what happens, you'll put people first."

"Of course."

"No, I mean it. I'm serious."

Elijah was still a little confused, still a little lost.

"Babe," she said. "Please promise me, no matter what happens, you will put humans first."

"Grace, I'm already doing that."

"No. Promise me."

He looked at her, and she looked at him.

"I promise."

She smiled. She kissed him on the forehead and tousled his hair. "Thank you. I believe you. Now, do you want to help me with these dishes?"
