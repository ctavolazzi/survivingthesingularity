introduced to it and he marvels at it? Like, wow, this is really cool. How'd you guys get the permits for this? And
Priya can demonstrate her value, right, to the group. She's like, I helped get all this together. And Elijah ... is
still marveling at the rafters. And don't use these words exactly, you know, like use the writing guides to make this
better. I'm just brainstorming. And Priya can be like, I helped put this together. Building codes. Can be very
difficult, but fortunately, there are no laws explicitly prohibiting this type of miniature barn dominium setup. He's
like, I just helped patch the holes and put things together so that we could have industrial living space, industrial
equipment, so we could have manufacturing equipment and a living space combined together under one roof. And Elijah
looks over at the RV and like kind of gazes at everything, and ideas start forming in his mind. And whoever's there
with him, I would assume Lou, will be like, what wheels are turning in that brilliant mind of yours, Elijah? And he'll
just smirk and be like, oh, I got some good, bad ideas. I got some really good, bad ideas. And Priya can ask, did you
mean to say good, bad? And he's like, oh yeah. And then it's the end of that scene. And then maybe that can connect to
their manufacturing of the parts later. Maybe that can connect to all kinds of other things if you can figure out a
skilled way to do that."

## Edit 296, the scene as logged (Chapter 21)

> The drawing went up on the shed whiteboard in the middle of June, right beside Marta's three numbers. Elijah found it when he came in for the crimper. Two long boxes sat side by side, with the wall between them rubbed out. There was a bed and a stove at one end and a workbench at the other, and one word across the top.
>
> "What the fuck is a shouse?" he asked.
>
> "Shop. House," Marta said. "A shop that's set up to keep the person running it alive." She was at the vise with a file, and she didn't stop. "Two shipping containers. They come in August."
>
> Elijah forgot about the crimper. A person could weld a bracket at that bench and sleep at the other end of the same room, with the stove going. He liked it a lot more than he had expected to like a drawing of two boxes.
>
> "Wait. No, this is really cool," he said. "How'd you guys get the permits for this?"
>
> "That was me." Priya was down at the end of the bench with a stack of paper sample bags, writing the date on each one. "Reuben did the filing. I read the county building code."
>
> "All of it?"
>
> "All of it. It's a miserable read. There's a chapter for houses and a chapter for shops, and I couldn't find one line in either that says they can't share a roof."
>
> Elijah had stopped looking at her. He was looking up into the rafters of the old packing shed like he had never been in the building before. Then it was the steel racked along the wall, and then the kitchen trailer, out the roll-up door. The machines were in here and supper was out there. Nobody had ever told him you were allowed to put them together.
>
> "It isn't against the law," Priya said, a little louder. "That's all I'm saying. The county still sends one inspector for the house and another for the shop, and we have to pass both."
>
> "She's the reason there's a drawing," Marta said.
>
> Two orange robot arms were sitting in crates in Richard Praether's pole barn, doing nothing. An idea had started up in Elijah, and he let it run.
>
> Lou had come up from the beds to fill his water jug. He had been watching Elijah's face.
>
> "What wheels are turning in that brilliant mind of yours, professor?"
>
> Elijah smirked. "Oh, I got some good, bad ideas," he said. "I got some really good, bad ideas."
>
> Lou screwed the cap back on his jug and didn't ask him anything else.
>
> Priya stopped writing. "Did you mean to say good, bad?"
>
> "Oh, yeah."
