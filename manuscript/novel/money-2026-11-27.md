## What holds (11 of 12)

The book's money is sound. The route at $11.40 a person a week for 140 people is $82,992 a year, and Elijah's plan
(a hundred visitors at $800) comes to $80,000: his arithmetic is right, so the table's no is about something else, as
written. The storage unit at $340 a month can't be carried on ten dollars a day, and the lien notice comes in the fifth
month. The trust's payment comes up short once, in the April after Denny leaves, and the channel's return explains the
October surplus. Sixteen uncashed checks from July 2026 to November 2027. Six arms at about $330,000 installed is Gus's
two million; installed is not resale, which is why nobody bids. Sixty dollars for Walt's tractor in all three mouths;
twelve hundred and two dollars for the hose in both places.

**Ma's room and the account** never get numbers, so they can't contradict each other; everything the page does say
agrees (the sale over what the bank wanted, none of it taken, the room still paid three years on).

**Grace's "why did you pretend to be poor"** (not in the book; CT described her anger this way). Tested against what he
had on the page: the farm money, in Ma's name, paying her room, none taken; two arms nobody would buy (no bids, scrap
under two months of storage); a guitar pawned for $110 and lost; seventeen dollars, then four; and a million refused,
which Grace never learns. He was poor. What he hid was an asset with no buyer. If the line goes in, the page gives
Elijah his answer ("Nobody had ever bid on the arms."), and Grace doesn't have it (knowledge sheet A5).

1. **Where Elijah's pocket money comes from on the farm** (does not hold). From March 2025 to November 2027 the page
   names no income for him: nine people there don't get paid, his pay isn't voted until July 2026, and he never cashes
   those checks until the book is printed. At the land vote he "didn't have it." Yet he has "enough money for gas there
   and back and for food" at Thanksgiving 2025, an oil change, prints at the drugstore, flowers for Ray, and gas for the
   drive south. The only income ever named is the app's forty or sixty dollars every couple of weeks, in Santa Rosa
   (Ch 15). Smallest fix, Claude's wording and an invented fact (that the app money kept coming): in Ch 25, "He had
   enough money for gas there and back and for food." becomes "The app still sent him forty or sixty dollars every couple
   of weeks, and he had enough for gas there and back and for food." Flagged for CT: he may prefer another source.
2. Already known, in the calendar: "the last one ran out in June" (Ch 27, October 2026) means next June. Not repeated here.
