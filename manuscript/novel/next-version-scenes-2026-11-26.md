### The draft (shown to CT, not approved)

Curtis found the robot operating system in September.

He had been reading about it all week, most of the night, the way he read everything. On Friday he came into the packing shed early with his phone already out, before the plates were down. For once he didn't have the end of the world in his pocket.

"It's free," he said. "That's the part I can't get over. It's an operating system for robots, and they just give it away. Arms, wheels, eyes, all of it. You can open it up and read every line."

"Thought you didn't want anything in that field that could see," Lou said. He was setting out forks.

"I didn't want theirs. This one's nobody's. No man with a laptop two states away." Curtis put the phone down on the plywood and turned it around to face them. "Seed to table. One of them plants it. One of them weeds it and picks it. Then you put a couple in the kitchen, and they cook it, and they pack it, and they freeze it. A tray shows up on your step. Four minutes in the microwave and it's dinner." He looked down the table. He was lit up like a kid at a parade. "There's so much here."

"It's not feasible, Curtis," Marta said. "It took three of us a summer to get one gantry down one bed. Then it rained."

"And it's not in the budget," Lou said. "There isn't a budget. There's a route that's paid through December."

"It doesn't matter." Curtis waved it off. "We can make more budget if we do this."

Priya had her pencil out. She hadn't written anything.

"Do you really want the optics of putting all the farmers out of work?" she said. "Do you want to take the backlash? Because I sure don't."

"We're not putting farmers out of work. We're empowering them."

Nobody picked that up. Curtis turned to Elijah.

"Back me up, bud. This is your whole thing."

It was. Two years ago he'd have had a slide. Elijah looked at the phone on the plywood, turned around to face him.

"Look, man. I really like you, Curtis," he said. "But the women are right. This is not the right time. We do not want to be the first to do this. Trust me."

He had been first once. He had turned a laptop around on a bar, just like that phone, and slid a napkin across the wood. A week later there was a hand on his shoulder and three glasses in the air. On the fourth floor at Claypot they had called going first winning. He had watched what first cost, and who paid it. It wasn't the men with the glasses.

"Our time will come," he said. "We don't want to be the first. But we might want to be the second."

"Or third," Lou said.

"Whichever number it is, we just need to make sure the farm is protected," Priya said. She wrote something down now. "This is coming. We should stay on top of it."

Curtis picked up his phone and looked at it. He put it in his shirt pocket.

"Fine," he said. "I know you guys are right." He slid down in his chair. "I want my Star Trek future so bad."

"We know you do, Curtis," Lou said. "We'll get there. You have to give people time to catch up on their own."

"You can't force anybody before they're ready," Marta said. "It's wrong."

"I don't know," Grace said.

She had been quiet through all of it. She was squaring a stack of napkins that was already square.

"Maybe sometimes people choose the wrong thing."

She said it lightly, to the napkins. Marta let it go by. Elijah couldn't. He had sat on the edge of bed four in July and heard about a porch.

"Sure they do," he said. "It's still theirs to choose."

"Is it." She didn't look up. "If you're standing right there watching."

"You knock. That's what you told me. He gets to slam it in your face."

"I said you knock, Elijah." Now she looked at him. "I didn't say you walk back down the steps and call it respect."

"That's not what I said."

"It's what it comes to."

It was the start of the hitch voice. His face had gone warm. He had an answer, and it was about doors.

"Okay," Lou said. He put both hands on the table and pushed himself up, slowly, for the knee. "Who wants dinner? We got squash in the oven."

Curtis was the first one out of his chair.

"That I'll be first for," he said.
