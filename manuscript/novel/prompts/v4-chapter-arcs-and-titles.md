## What I want
The way books like Harry Potter are written, every chapter is basically a mini episode, a vignette. I think we have that energy here, but I want to make sure. Two jobs, in this order.

### Job 1: a chapter arc for every chapter
For each chapter of v4 (Author's Note through Afterword), read the v4 text itself and write its story arc:
- What the chapter is about: X, Y and Z. The things that take place.
- The transformations the characters go through. What happens to each person.
- How it connects to other chapters, and why, because of those things.
- Where it leaves off, and what makes the reader want to keep reading.
- How it advances the plot (A, B and C), and why that makes sense and matters (1, 2, 3, 4).
- Flag any chapter that doesn't yet stand on its own as a vignette, and say why.

Put the arcs in a new Google Doc called "StS v4 Chapter Arcs" in the Drive folder "Surviving the Singularity, Story Map". Do not put them in the scaffold. The scaffold stays just a scaffold.

### Job 2: chapter titles, written from the arcs
The table of contents needs chapter titles, not just chapter numbers. The story arc of each chapter should inform its title. It is not just some concept ripped from it.
- Look at the old editions to see what the chapter titles covered before. Map those ideas onto the chapters as they are now in v4. Do not reuse the old titles exactly.
- Make them clever and kind of nondescript. I always enjoyed how the Harry Potter chapter titles were a puzzle in and of themselves. Channel that energy, in original wording; don't borrow anyone's titles.
- Put the titles in v4's table of contents and on each chapter heading.
