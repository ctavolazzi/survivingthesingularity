## 1. Contradictions found (most serious first)

### C1. Elijah leaves on Sunday but arrives on Tuesday, with no bridge (chapter 15 to 16)

- Text, 1716: "On Sunday he told his mother he was going north for work."
- Text, 1717-1718: he then sets up the night aide ("Sundays ... Nine-forty at night").
- Text, 1724: "He drove to the gas station on the corner, put all twenty dollars in the tank, and went north."
- Text, 1725-1726: he drives past Healdsburg and Cloverdale; the check-engine light comes on.
- Text, 1736: Marta says "She's on the route. It's Tuesday."
- Text, 1769: "He'd practiced it for three hours on the 101." (a single drive, same day)
- My read: the departure is Sunday evening and the arrival is Tuesday dusk. Monday and most of Sunday night are unaccounted for. He has twenty dollars and no money for a motel. Either change 1736 to "It's Monday" or add a line (slept at a rest stop, left Monday). The fifth-week timeline otherwise works: Grace's first email Tuesday (1599), app notice Wednesday, old man Friday, food bank Saturday, depart Sunday. Tuesday 29 October 2024 fits "late October".

### C2. "The last twenty years on his mother's place" vs the new backstory (1968)

- Text, 1968: "what he'd actually done on his mother's place for the last twenty years was fix gate latches and eat."
- Text, 1363: "He had been living in his old bedroom that spring."
- Text, 1216: "He let the apartment go in April."
- Text, 1874: "his old apartment".
- Also: eighteen years with the band, fifteen years pouring/drinking at David's bar (523), six years of delivery (1554), and the Stockton job.
- My read: leftover from a version where he never left the farm. After the rewrite he lived away (apartment, Stockton, on the road) and moved home only that spring. Suggest "for the first twenty years of his life" or "every time he came home".

### C3. "His own savings math" when he has no savings (1874)

- Text, 1874: "He did his own savings math four times, arriving at four different terrifying answers."
- Text, 1216: "The savings were for emergencies, and at some point he noticed he was the emergency." (the savings are gone by April 2024)
- Text, 1600-1601: seventeen dollars, then four dollars (before the co-op).
- Text, 1772 and 2342: no pay until summer 2026.
- My read: arriving at four different answers implies a nonzero balance. The mother's account (1562) is explicitly "never his". It could mean "his runway", but it reads like old text from a version where he had savings.

### C4. The "been here before" list in chapter 18 gets the 4 a.m. scene wrong (1876)

- Text, 1876: "At a kitchen table with a chatbot at four in the morning. In a truck in a parking lot in Stockton. Nobody had come either time."
- Text, 1580-1587 (chapter 15): the truck 4 a.m. chatbot scene is in Santa Rosa, under a sycamore (1553). The Stockton lot is the cannery (1302).
- My read: the most recent and most relevant instance (Santa Rosa, ch15) is missing, and "truck in Stockton" is the cannery, where no chatbot scene is shown. Probably intended as spiral episodes, but it is a mismatch with the on-page scenes.

### C5. "That October" vs "three weeks after he picked up the broom" (1784, 1789)

- Text, 1784: "He was thin enough that October that he could see it scared people. By the second week it had stopped scaring them."
- Text, 1781 and 1789: first Friday dinner is three weeks after arrival.
- Text, 1871: the December spiral is "seven weeks after he'd walked into the shop".
- My read: arrival about 29 October means the three-week mark is mid-November. "That October" is true only for the first days. Low priority. "that fall" would be safe.

### C6. Two different 140s (1970, 1981, 1858, 2395)

- Text, 1970: Lou "grew vegetables for a hundred and forty households".
- Text, 1981: "we give a third of them away".
- Text, 1858: Elijah to Rosa: "For the food? Nothing. It's the kitchen's delivery."
- Text, 2395: Grace: "I've got a hundred and forty on the route".
- My read: if "the route" is the free delivery (140 on it, all free), that does not match "a third given away" of 140 households. If the two 140s are different groups, it is a coincidence the reader will merge. Pick one number or change one.

### C7. "Thirty years selling parts" vs the store's history and Frank's age (2394, 2224, 2277)

- Text, 2394: "I spent thirty years selling parts".
- Text, 2224: "wood worn pale where four generations of forearms had rested", the bell "Frank's grandfather had hung".
- Text, 2277: "When I was his age we put a man on the moon" (Tyler is twenty, so Frank was about twenty in 1969, now about 76).
- My read: thirty years does not fit a man who has presumably worked the counter since youth. Maybe "thirty years as owner". Minor.

### C8. "They had not been two fences from her" (2107)

- Text, 2107: "Three letters and an LLC. Same agent. Three hours apart, the same signature. ... They had not been two fences from her. He had sold them her kitchen."
- Text, 1424: "It was the same people who had bought the Hollis place. They liked the valley, he said."
- My read: unclear. If the Hollis place was next door, "had not been" negates a fact chapter 13 implies, or it is a deliberate "not just neighbors". "Three hours apart" reads as the distance between his mother's farm and the co-op. Needs a one-line check against what the author means.

### C9. Frank gets a "paid job" in December 2025, but the table cannot pay Elijah until summer 2026 (2253, 2342, 1983)

- Text, 2253: the deal with Frank includes "a paid job".
- Text, 2342: "The pay finally went to the table that summer, a year and a half late."
- Text, 1983: "nine people here don't get paid".
- Text, 2272: Tyler "gained forty hours a week".
- My read: not impossible (Frank's pay could come from the tool-library funding), but a reader may ask why the table could pay Frank in winter and not Elijah. Possibly intended (Grace's failure at the table). Nothing says where Frank's pay comes from.
