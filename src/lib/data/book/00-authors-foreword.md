# AUTHOR'S FOREWORD: Why Tell You All This?

There's a TV show you've probably forgotten about called *Farscape*. An astronaut named John Crichton is test-flying an experimental spacecraft when a wormhole opens up and swallows him. He comes out the other side in a distant part of the universe, a very long way from home, with no clear path back. And he just lives out there now.

The show is full of strange and wonderful aliens, but here's the part that stuck with me: the ship he ends up living on is alive. Moya is a giant biomechanical creature, a Leviathan, and the whole crew lives inside her.

I want to put that image on the table, because I think it's where this technology is heading. Not the aliens. The living vessel.

Here are two real examples, so you know I'm not just talking about television. In early 2025, DARPA put out a call for ideas on biologically grown structures built in space: antennas, nets for catching debris, pieces of space stations, some of them more than 500 meters long. Its own list of inspirations included fungal filaments. Meanwhile, at NASA's Ames Research Center, Lynn Rothschild's team has been working on growing habitats for the Moon and Mars out of mycelium, the root network of mushrooms.

And back on Earth, mycelium is already shipping. A company called Ecovative grows it into packaging that replaces polystyrene foam, and IKEA signed on to use the technology in 2020. People are literally growing the box that your product arrives in.

It is a short skip and a jump from growing a package to growing a vessel.

Now take one more step. Grow the vessel, put a local AI model inside it, and let it pilot itself. Do you not have all the ingredients for the plane from the movie *Stealth*? An AI aircraft that flies itself and goes after people around the world?

That's not only a movie plot anymore. In 2021, a UN panel of experts reported that a Turkish-made Kargu-2 drone may have hunted down and engaged fighters in Libya in 2020, possibly without a human directing the final attack. The report stopped short of confirming it. Since then, drone makers in Ukraine have claimed to field systems that choose their own targets, and those claims are hard to verify. I'm not telling you the sky is full of robot assassins. I'm telling you the direction is not subtle, and these are clear and present dangers.

That's the doom and gloom. We have to address it. It would be irresponsible not to.

**So why would I tell you about all that and bring all of that to your attention in a book called *Surviving the Singularity*?**

Because I believe something else, and I believe it just as hard.

There are ways this can go well, and that things can turn out all right.

> We are not going to get there unless somebody chooses to put energy into building the future we all want. Somebody has got to bite the bullet and take the heat in order to use the tools that exist to ensure every individual person has the right to self-determine the outcome of their own lives. This work of semi-fiction is dedicated to the people who are willing to take responsibility for using their skills and talents to give energy to the effort, the collective effort, to ensure humanity remains in control of itself.

Each individual person is a microcosm of us all.

We all put our pants on one leg at a time, except for some strange and gifted individuals who put their pants on both legs at once. They just pull them up. Maybe it's because they're in a wheelchair and their feet don't touch the ground, so they've always done it that way. Or maybe it's someone in a hospital bed who can't move their arms anymore, has no insurance, and has no idea what happens next. Maybe it's an old lady whose grandkids visit once a year, but she still keeps all the plates up in the cupboard in case they ever come over. Maybe it's a young man who feels hurt and alone. Maybe it's any one of us who feels like society has turned its back on us.

And if you've never felt that sting, maybe you're one of the people privileged enough to mostly feel bored.

What a privilege it is these days to be bored. Boredom is where a lot of creativity starts, and plenty of the people we remember for mastering something got there because they were bored. Now think about what you need in order to be bored at all: free time. If every hour of your life goes to surviving, fighting the elements, looking for resources, worrying about money, being denied health care, having ideas with no means to bring them to life, or juggling the cost of childcare against every other bill eating into a salary that rises once every other year, if that, then boredom is a luxury you never get.

So think about the ways your life is lacking. Then think about what you might do with an assistant you train yourself. One you teach, that asks you meaningful questions, that understands how you want your life to be and helps you get there. One that doesn't work for anybody but you, because you built it from the ground up.

First principles.

When I say first principles, I don't mean learning electrical theory. I mean learning what electrons are, and what the theories say about how many of them might be out there. What if there's more to every single thing you see than meets the eye? How deep do you go? Where do you choose to draw the line? What do you stop learning about? Where do you choose to put your energy?

Most of us put it into our jobs, into making money, managing that money, budgeting, shopping, and blowing off steam. Some of us, maybe half the planet, spend most of our time on survival, not knowing where the next meal is coming from, be it breakfast, lunch, or dinner.

Now imagine all of that, all the heaviness of the world. Feel its weight around your neck like it's hanging on a chain. Now picture that weight lifting. Breathe into your chest and feel the weight of the world come off of it. You don't have to carry that anymore. All the stress I just described has been relieved.

In this moment, as you read this, how much energy just opened up? If you look around, how does the world look when you let go of that weight?

The next time you're out on a walk, look around and think about how many tasks it takes to maintain every building, business, road, and piece of infrastructure you can see. Now imagine those maintenance costs, in both money and labor, cut in half. Then imagine the remaining half cut in half again, so only a quarter of the work is needed to keep all of that standing. What do you think becomes the main barrier to one very specific thing: people spreading out into new frontiers, as we always have, as a species?

One of our most core and consistent elements is that we are explorers. We poke and prod and pry things open and look inside, like a toddler on Christmas Day.

So in this foreword I want to assert one very specific premise.

**We have always been explorers. We will never stop going on new adventures.**

It's less about what we'll do and more about who we are now. When the barrier to entry is as low as simply asking for it, those with the courage to ask, and those who know how, will be the ones who win.

I believe humanity will keep spreading out and settling new areas of the world. There's a lot of unsettled territory that could host brand new towns, and if there are machines, humanoid or otherwise, that can do the work of building new settlements, you can bet your ass a whole lot of people are going to jump on that opportunity. Like the pioneers of the Old West, but hopefully without all the genocide. It's not looking good so far. But if more people put more energy into building better ways of life, if we spent less time doomscrolling and dropping pessimistic comments and slurs in social media comment sections, maybe we'd have more time to do the thing we actually all want to do, which is eat more snacks with our friends.

Who doesn't want that? If you're thinking *I don't have any friends*, good news: you're still alive. You're reading this, which means you still have time to go make some.

And think about how much time would open up in your day if you knew you had the God-given right, as a human being born on planet Earth or elsewhere, to food, shelter, clean water, and health care. Because of the tools of our time, as I most often call them, you can simply ask, and receive.

Those tools are moving into the physical world next. I, for one, am very excited to see the practical applications of humanoid robotics and other physical AI (or SI, or whatever we end up calling it). In a handful of years we'll have at our fingertips the same level of tech as most sci-fi movies. It literally feels like it's only a matter of time until every YouTuber builds a lightsaber in real life.

Maybe it could be a benchmark.

Christopher Tavolazzi  
Chico, California  
October 2026
