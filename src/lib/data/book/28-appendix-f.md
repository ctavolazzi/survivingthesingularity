# Appendix F: Glossary

*Every term this book leans on, in plain words, with the chapter that explains it. Definitions here follow how this book uses a word; other writers may use it differently.*

**Accelerating Change.** One of three ways of thinking about the Singularity: technology feeds on itself and speeds up, so you can read the curve and name a date. This book declines it as a planning tool. (Chapter 1)

**Adequate Level of Care.** The book's yardstick for a community: can it make sure the people who live there have food, shelter, and basic care, dependably, including through a bad week? (Chapter 18)

**AGI (artificial general intelligence).** A system that can do the range of intellectual work people can. The author's forecast for it is U.S. Thanksgiving, November 25, 2027; it's a forecast, not a finding. (Chapters 1 and 2)

**Algorithmic judo.** Wrapping useful, practical knowledge in a human story strong enough that a recommendation system carries it to people for you. (Chapter 10)

**ASI (artificial superintelligence).** A mind far beyond ours in every field. In this book's map, Stages 6 and 7. (Chapter 3)

**Cognitive firewall.** Treating your attention the way a security engineer treats a critical server: batch your inputs, prefer text, and control what crosses into your head. (Chapter 8)

**Community land trust.** Usually a nonprofit, governed partly by the people who live on and around its land. It holds the land; the people using it own what they build, under a long ground lease. (Chapters 12 and 15)

**Create Over Consume.** The protocol of spending your attention and hours making things (writing, building, recording, teaching) instead of only absorbing what's fed to you. (Chapter 10)

**Crucible.** The co-op's local AI server in the story: a rack of salvaged hardware where models are tested before anyone relies on them. More generally, any local machine where you run and check models yourself. (Chapter 11)

**CSA (community-supported agriculture).** An arrangement where households share in a farm's harvest, and its risk, directly. (Chapter 12)

**Cyberdeck.** A personal computer you build instead of buy, from parts you understand, to run without a cloud, a subscription, or anyone's permission. (Chapter 5 and Appendix E)

**DC-native.** Running a small solar system's loads directly on direct current, skipping the losses of converting to household AC and back. (Chapter 17)

**Deglobalization.** Shortening the supply lines we depend on for survival, so a shock on the other side of the planet doesn't reach all the way to your dinner. Not isolation, and not the same as "decentralization." (Chapter 9)

**The Entitled and the Ready.** Two mindsets toward the change, not two kinds of people: needing the hierarchy to hold, or needing the suffering to stop. Most of us carry a little of both. (Chapter 7)

**Event Horizon.** The view that once something smarter than us exists, the far side becomes unpredictable to everyone, builders included. This book uses it for the map. (Chapter 1)

**Food baseline.** Adequate food, available to everyone regardless of income or employment, as a dependable service rather than a favor. The first commitment of the Adequate Level of Care. (Introduction and Appendix A)

**Food first.** The book's priority order: food, then land care, cleanup, shelter, and everything further out. A place to start, not a waiting list. (Introduction)

**Ground lease.** A long lease on land, often ninety-nine years in a land trust, that lets people own their home or greenhouse without owning the dirt under it. (Chapter 15)

**Hyper-local.** Designed for a neighborhood or a region, close enough that the people depending on it can see, fix, and govern it. The book prefers it to "decentralized." (Chapter 9)

**Intelligence Explosion.** I. J. Good's 1965 idea: a machine smart enough to design better machines starts a loop that runs faster each turn. This book uses it for the engine. (Chapter 1)

**Kiwix.** Open-source software that packages Wikipedia and other reference libraries to read offline. (Appendix E)

**Local model.** An AI model running on hardware you own, with no company's server in the loop. "Local" says where it runs; it doesn't say the answers are right. (Chapter 11)

**LoRa and Meshtastic.** A long-range, low-power radio standard, and open-source software that uses it to pass short text messages between devices with no cell carrier in the middle. (Chapters 8 and 17, Appendix E)

**Luddites.** English textile workers who broke machines in 1811 to 1816. They read what the machines would do to their pay correctly and chose a strategy that failed. (Chapter 7)

**Mesh network.** A network where every node connects to several neighbors, so damage becomes a detour instead of an outage. (Chapters 8 and 17)

**Model collapse.** What happens when AI models are trained on the output of earlier models: the rare, varied parts of human data wash out, generation after generation. (Chapter 10)

**Money optional.** The book's goal for the economy: markets and money stay for everything above survival, but nobody needs money to stay alive. Not abolished. Optional. (Introduction and Chapter 19)

**Neighborhood factory.** Repair and small-scale production brought close to home: 3D printers, a plasma table, a welder, and people who know how to use them. (Chapter 9)

**Network surplus.** Everything the households in a local network produce, minus everything they need. When it's positive, the network can cover a household that has a bad month. (Chapter 12)

**Nine stages.** This book's map of the Singularity, from Stage 1, the Cash Grab, to Stage 9, the Apex Intelligence. Stages 1 to 5 are where the choices are still ours. (Chapters 2 to 4, Appendix C)

**Open weights.** An AI model whose trained parameters are published, so anyone can download and run it. Once released, they can't be recalled. (Chapter 2)

**Photobioreactor.** A vessel that grows algae under controlled light and conditions. The builder in Chapter 17 printed one at home. (Chapter 17)

**Precedent Ledger.** The book's twenty-four documented cases of people meeting a new capability, or a new rule, before this one, each with a story, a mechanism, a rule, and a practice. (Introduction and Appendix D)

**Premortem.** Imagining that a plan has already failed and working backward to find out why, before it fails for real. (Chapter 18)

**Primate Backlash.** Stage 5: grief and anger at the change, including real objections, sometimes expressed with a brick. (Chapter 2)

**Recursive self-improvement.** An AI system helping to design the next, better version of itself. (Chapters 1 and 3)

**SB 1383.** California's law requiring large food businesses to recover edible food and setting a target to recover 20 percent of edible food that would otherwise be landfilled. (Chapters 14 and 19)

**Short loop.** A tighter circle of food, repair, tools, and knowledge drawn around your region, so it keeps circulating when the long global line hiccups. (Chapter 14)

**Shouse.** A shop-house: half workshop, half compact home, under one roof. (Chapter 13)

**Singularity (this book's definition).** The point where intelligence stops being scarce, and every institution quietly built on its scarcity starts failing before anyone has written the replacement. (Chapter 1)

**Sneakernet.** Moving data by carrying it: a memory card handed person to person when the network isn't there. (Chapter 16)

**Stage 4 (the New Social Contract).** The hinge of the nine stages, where a society decides how the gains from automation reach the people affected. This book's first proposal for it is food, regardless of employment. (Chapter 2)

**Surveillance capitalism.** Shoshana Zuboff's name for the business of harvesting people's attention and personal data and selling predictions about them. (Introduction)

**Transformer.** The neural-network design introduced by Google researchers in 2017, in "Attention Is All You Need," that lets a model process every word in relation to every other at once. The basis of today's language models. (Chapter 1)

**Universal Basic Services.** Providing needs like food, shelter, transport, care, and information to everyone, free at the point of use and paid for together, instead of handing out cash for people to buy them. (Chapter 19)

**USI (universal superintelligence).** Stage 8's speculation: intelligence at the scale of the cosmos. (Chapter 4)
