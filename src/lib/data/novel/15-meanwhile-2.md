# Meanwhile

Aaron Bidwell left the dinner before the fish, because he had been told what he came to be told.

“How long?” he’d asked the table, and six people had looked at an empty chair.

So he went down the front steps with his reader a pace behind him, and on the way he got rid of his glass. There was a waiter standing right there. A guy in a bad jacket, holding a notebook.

“Is the car here?”

“I don’t work here,” the waiter said.

“Nobody does anymore,” Bidwell said.

In the car the reader had the tablet open.

“The farmland. You asked for a number.”

“I asked what it costs.”

“It depends how much of it you want.”

“I want the part with the notebooks.”

The reader had prepared for this and still did not enjoy it. “The people who keep the best records are the small ones. Co-ops. Family places. They write everything down because they can’t afford to be wrong. There’s a cluster of them north of Cloverdale. Your companies are buying around them now. None of the companies have your name on them.”

“Good. What’s that?”

The reader had turned the tablet without meaning to. There was a video paused on it. A thin man sitting on a floor in front of a wood stove, holding a notebook too close to the camera.

“Nothing. It was in the briefing for tonight. He’s a farm boy from Sonoma. He made a list. The internet believes him.”

“Believes him about what?”

“About all of it. He was at the dinner. He didn’t sit down.”

Bidwell looked at the frozen face. It didn’t ring any bells. Faces rarely did. He remembered what people owned.

“What does he want?”

“According to this, he just wants people to see what’s coming.”

“Nobody just wants that.”

“No, sir.”

Bidwell thought about it for almost a block, which for him was a long time.

“I don’t need to meet him,” he said. “I don’t like meeting them. Find out where he lands. Then buy what’s next to it.”
