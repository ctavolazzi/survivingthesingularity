# Afterword: We Don’t Have to Live Like This

What if it all goes right?

What if we become capable of doing much more of the work that makes life meaningful?

Not flawlessly. Not without arguments, broken machines, bad decisions, or people who try to keep the best parts for themselves, but with a new kind of social contract that guarantees your basic needs get met today, tomorrow, and forevermore.

Imagine being fired next Tuesday.

There’s an awful conversation to have at home. There are plans to change and bills to sort out.

But what if you knew dinner wasn’t a concern? What if your family was guaranteed food while you figure out what comes next, regardless of how much money you all have?

What if losing your job didn’t revoke your permission to feel safe?

That’s where I want to begin.

## Listen to the words between the lines

When someone says, “AI will take my job,” listen to what follows. “How will I pay the rent?”

“How will I feed my kids?”

“What happens if I get sick?”

Even if nobody says it out loud, you can feel those questions in the heavy air.

Those fears deserve a better answer. Telling a frightened worker to be excited about productivity is a failure to listen. We are getting better at producing things while people are less able to obtain them.

The food can still grow. The houses can still stand. The machines can do exactly what they were built to do. And yet someone can easily lose the income that gives them access to any of it.

If a machine helps grow the food and the person it displaced can’t afford to eat, the threat isn’t that we learned to grow food more efficiently. It’s that we failed to carry the benefit through to our fellow human beings.

## We don’t have to live like this

Here’s what I sincerely believe, as plainly as I can say it:

We don’t have to live like this.

We could have a beautiful world where AI and robots provide for the physical survival needs of every single person. Machines could grow our food and build our houses automatically. AI could help cure diseases, help us learn and grow, help us explore the universe, and discover new ways of building new kinds of technology, better societies, and a better life for every individual person.

We already have abundance. What we lack is distribution.

The United Nations says it flatly: “today’s global food production is enough to feed everyone on the planet.” (Source: United Nations Global Issues: Food)

Why should we toss a perfectly good sandwich just because it didn’t sell?

Almost everything in this world has been arranged around money, as if nothing else actually matters. That arrangement is enforced by the threat of suffering. Seems like a better system could be created were it demanded by the people, no?

The way many people see it is there’s nothing inherently wrong with chasing money. If you want to make a billion dollars and buy things, fine. Go for it. But why should everyone else have to suffer at your expense?

In the age of AI, perhaps money could become optional for survival. Not abolished completely, just optional.

If we have all the tools necessary to feed, clothe, and house every person on the planet, why wouldn’t we?

We could have a society where people grow and learn, fix their own bodies, build amazing things, make wonderful art, and do it all because they want to. Instead we have one where people go hungry and suffer because they have to.

Greed isn’t a law of physics. It’s a set of choices, written down and enforced, and choices can be changed.

If this resonates… you’re on the right track, babe. Please, for the sake of us all, keep going.

## Food first

I want adequate food available to everyone, regardless of income or employment. Enough food is grown every year to feed everyone alive and more.

Our society should be able to promise that to us all.

Robots can be part of how we keep that promise. So can farmers, cooks, drivers, public services, and the people already doing the unglamorous work of getting food to other people. We don’t have to wait for a machine that can do everything before giving useful machines a better assignment.

“Nobody wants to work anymore,” as the saying goes, is wrong. People do want to make things, get good at hard work, run kitchens, take care of each other, and build something that lasts. What I personally object to is spending forty years renting the opportunity to survive, afraid that getting fired from a job will take my next meal with it.

So let’s start asking better questions.

If a robot can fold laundry and walk a dog, why can’t it help grow food and build a house?

If a robot can grow my food and build my house, why am I still renting my survival?

The answer isn’t to flee the world or bunker down against it. I believe the answer is to build the thing that makes the question obsolete, close to home, with your neighbors, starting now.

## Get involved

If you’re worried about who controls this technology, give that concern somewhere to go. Talk to your government. Form a coalition with your peers. Get together with local business owners and petition your city council. Write a book. Pick a decision you want changed and work on it with other people.

Take fucking action.

Find the next meeting agenda. Check the public-comment rules and speaking limit. Write one request that fits the time: what you want, who it serves, and what the next step would cost. Ask for a specific next step, such as a staff report or a budget item, and a date when you can follow up.

Afterward, send the request in writing. Read the minutes. Find out whether anyone was assigned to act, and come back when the item returns. A speech starts a conversation; the follow-up keeps the request alive.

Frederick Douglass put the demand plainly in 1857: “Power concedes nothing without a demand. It never did and it never will.”

Make one. Then follow it through.

What if it all goes right? Then losing a job stops being a threat to a person’s existence. Necessary work gets less punishing. More people get time and room to choose. We use these extraordinary machines to make ordinary life more secure for everyone, forever.

That’s a future worth surviving for.
