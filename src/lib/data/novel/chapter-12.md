# Chapter 12: Not on the List

He worked at the cannery for three weeks on a promise.

"We'll sort you out Friday," Trent said, the first Friday. The second Friday Trent was in Los Angeles. Elijah bolted orange arms to a concrete floor with a crew of six guys who were getting paid, and at night he slept in the cab of the Ranger at the far end of the cannery lot, with the windows cracked and a towel over the wheel. A shower at the truck stop on the highway was seventeen dollars. He took one every other day, and when the money got low he stopped.

He had started smoking again in February, on a fire escape. By August it was spliffs. A guy on the crew sold him a bag the first week, and at night he rolled one on the dash and smoked it down so he could sleep.

He didn't mind the work. He liked it more than anything he'd done in a year. You put a bolt in and it stayed in.

The third Friday was the launch party.

They'd lit the arms purple. He could see them through the big roll-up door, all six, moving together to music like a row of slow dancers, and a couple hundred people in good shoes standing around with drinks. There was a kid at the door with a tablet.

"Madrone," Elijah said. "Elijah."

The kid scrolled. He scrolled back up. He was nice about it.

"I'm really sorry. Is it maybe under a company?"

"I installed those."

"I believe you, man. I just can't. I'm sorry."

Past the kid's shoulder a caterer went by with a tray of little sandwiches with the crusts cut off.

He walked back across the lot to the truck. He'd felt off since noon, and by the time he got the door shut he was shaking. It had been a hundred and one in Stockton that day, and he was cold. He lay across the bench seat with his knees up and his jacket over him and listened to the bass come across the asphalt.

Around ten he got the phone out. There were people in that building who had squeezed his arm in February. He had their numbers. He picked four and sent each of them the same thing.

<em>Hey, it's Elijah. I'm out in the lot, pretty sick. Could use a hand if you've got a sec.</em>

He watched the screen. One of them, the woman with the cooling company, sent back a heart.

That was all that came. He waited an hour to make sure.

There were two other numbers he could try. He looked at them. <em>ruby,</em> lower case. And a contact he'd typed in himself off a gas receipt, which just said <em>Gus.</em>

He put his thumb over the first one and thought about what he'd say. <em>Hey, remember me, I'm in a parking lot.</em> He thought about a kid in a white apartment reading that, and what the kid would think, and knew exactly. One more person who wanted something.

Then the second one. Gus would come. That was the thing. He was pretty sure Gus would drive to Stockton at five under the limit. And Elijah had told that man no three weeks ago. He couldn't call him up now and ask.

He didn't call his mother. She'd have gotten in the car at eleven at night, and he wasn't going to do that to her.

He turned the phone face down on the dash.

He wasn't angry. He was too sick to be angry. He'd spent ten months saying yes to people. If he died in this truck tonight, the party would go until one. Somebody would notice the Ranger on Monday. They just didn't have a place to put him.
