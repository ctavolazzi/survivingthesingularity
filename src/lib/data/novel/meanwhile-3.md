# Meanwhile

Aaron Bidwell liked a map. A map was the one thing he would look at with his own eyes.

This one covered a wall in an office he used four days a year. Three counties north of the bay, in pale green, and over the green a spread of red parcels, fifty-three of them, growing toward each other like a rash.

In the middle of the biggest patch there was a hole.

"What's that?"

The reader had a page ready. The reader always had a page ready for the thing he was going to point at.

"Forty acres. It was a widow named Calder. We made three offers. The last was twenty-three percent over."

"And?"

"She put it in a trust."

"So we buy the trust."

"It's the kind you can't buy. It's written so it can never be sold to… well. To us. Specifically. There's language."

Bidwell walked up to the wall and looked at the hole from about a foot away, as if it might be a printing error.

"Who writes language like that for a widow?"

"A man named Vance. He does it for free. And there's a welder."

"A what?"

"A welder. She fixed the husband's tractor for sixty dollars, and it appears to have been decisive."

Bidwell turned around.

"I have eleven companies up there."

"Yes, sir."

"I have the dairy down south. I have the water on both sides."

"Yes, sir."

"And I'm losing to a widow and a welder."

"And nineteen households. They make a kind of greenhouse box. They have a video channel." The reader hesitated. "The farm boy is there. The one from the dinner. He landed on that parcel last fall."

Bidwell thought about being angry. It was a lot of effort and it had never once improved a map.

"What are they doing on it?"

"Writing things down. Soil, water, who gets fed. They're very thorough. They keep it all in one place."

"Hm."

He went back to the wall and put one finger on the hole, lightly.

"We wait," he said. "They're doing the collecting for me. Let them finish."
